Tuan Dam

Lecturer, Hanoi University of Science and Technology. Affiliated with a Vietnamese university since 2024.

Prof. Tuan's papers analyse Monte-Carlo tree search and bandit algorithms, mostly through convergence rates and sample-complexity bounds. Work on tree search with richer value backups for stochastic and continuous-action planning, including return distributions, shared graph nodes, risk sensitivity and non-stationary rewards. Work on best-arm identification with one-bit feedback and top-two sampling, plus variance-driven exploration and conserved quantities of training, with provable guarantees. Work on orthogonal perturbations for sharpness-aware training constrains the weight-matrix perturbation to be approximately orthogonal using Newton-Schulz iterations instead of an SVD.

Open challenges. Work on risk-sensitive tree search leaves open distributional value nodes, learned dynamics, static risk objectives, the overhead of state sharing and broader baseline comparisons. Work on best-arm identification with one-bit feedback leaves open adaptively optimized thresholds, the anchor-point assumption, early-learning validity of variance approximations and weak conservation laws. Work on orthogonalized sharpness-aware minimization leaves open its extra cost, interplay with adaptive optimizers, and whether gains come from the orthogonal perturbation.
