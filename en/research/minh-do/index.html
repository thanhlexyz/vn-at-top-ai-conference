Research directions: Minh Do

Minh Do (VinUni) · All professors

Prof. Minh's papers study representation learning for multimodal and image data, together with ways to generate or compress the training data itself. Work on multimodal pretraining with missing modalities and robust multimodal learning with limited labels. Work on closed-form contrastive alignment replaces repeated minibatch gradient training with one spectral decomposition, for linear and kernelized encoders. Work on condensing gigapixel pathology slides into a slide-adaptive set of concept prototypes by decomposing each slide, condensing, and pruning unused concepts.

Open challenges. Work on missing-modality reconstruction with open uncertainty benefits and absence-pattern coverage. Work on closed-form contrastive alignment leaves open whether it scales to large projection heads, full encoder fine-tuning and datasets far larger than those tested. Work on condensing gigapixel pathology slides leaves open the gap to full-slide features and the reliance on public datasets with site-specific staining artifacts.

Robult: Leveraging Redundancy and Modality-Specific Features for Robust Multimodal Learning IJCAI 2025 · accepted
The problem
Multimodal models must cope with modalities that are missing at inference time and with limited labelled data, which are common in real-world deployments.
The idea
Robult combines a soft Positive-Unlabeled (PU) contrastive loss that aligns task-relevant features while making effective use of limited labelled data in a semi-supervised setting, and a latent reconstruction loss that keeps information unique to each modality, within a modular design.
Strengths
The paper reports higher performance than existing approaches across diverse datasets in both semi-supervised and missing-modality settings, with a lightweight design that plugs into existing architectures.

Are you SURE? Enhancing Multimodal Pretraining with Missing Modalities through Uncertainty Estimation ICLR 2025 · rejected
The problem
Pretrained multimodal models assume all modalities are available and struggle on small, incomplete datasets; earlier methods that reconstruct missing modalities do not account for how unreliable the reconstruction may be.
The idea
SURE (Scalable Uncertainty and Reconstruction Estimation) adds latent-space reconstruction of missing modalities to a pretrained multimodal model and estimates uncertainty for both the reconstructions and the downstream outputs, using a Pearson-correlation-based loss and statistical error propagation.
Strengths
As summarised by reviewers, the paper evaluates SURE on sentiment analysis, genre classification and action recognition against previous missing-modality methods and applies it to different pretrained multimodal architectures.
What the authors leave open
  • Certain modalities dominate the reconstruction process, which makes missing ones easier to predict but causes large performance drops when the dominant modalities are unavailable; this imbalance is unexplored in SURE and left for future work.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The authors consider a limited number of modal absence scenarios, and the study of modal absence should encompass both intramodal absence and intermodal absence obeying real-world scenarios.
  • It is unclear how and why uncertainty estimation enhances overall performance.
  • the notation in equation (4) suggests that the PCC loss still relies on the Gaussian assumption.
  • Figure 3 suggests that a large number of reconstructors may be necessary to handle all possible missing-modality scenarios, which could affect the model’s scalability.

Deep learning and representation learning (1 paper · CSRankings score 0.33)

Topic description

Contrastive objectives behind multimodal models such as CLIP are usually trained with long stochastic minibatch optimization. The paper defines a contrastive similarity weight matrix under which the objective becomes a spectral problem with a closed-form solution by singular value decomposition, in linear form and in reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces, covering one-to-one and many-to-many alignment. It evaluates on synthetic, unimodal, multimodal and zero-shot tasks and reports 100–400× speedups over SGD-based contrastive training at matching or better performance.

Papers (1)
UniCon: Unified Framework for Efficient Contrastive Alignment via Kernels ICLR 2026 · accepted
The problem
Contrastive objectives, which pull matched pairs together and push unmatched pairs apart, underlie state-of-the-art multimodal models such as CLIP, but training them relies on long stochastic minibatch optimization.
The idea
UniCon introduces a contrastive similarity weight matrix S(γ) that turns contrastive alignment into a spectral problem with closed-form global solutions, obtained by singular value decomposition, for linear encoders and, through reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces (RKHS), for nonlinear encoders and many-to-many alignments.
Strengths
The paper proves that the closed-form updates replace minibatch back-propagation with exact updates and evaluates on synthetic, unimodal, multimodal and zero-shot tasks, where a reviewer summarises 100–400× speedups over SGD-based contrastive training at matching or better performance.
What the authors leave open
  • Performance under extremely biased or imbalanced data distributions remains an open question.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The paper lacks validation on large-scale datasets (such as LAION-400M or Conceptual-12M) and complex modalities including multilingual and video data.
  • Experiments are limited to small projection heads (e.g., two-layer MLPs). Real multimodal setups are larger. It is unclear how the method scales to larger/complex heads, partial/full CLIP fine-tuning, or training from scratch.
  • All experiments use frozen or very shallow trainable encoders and comparison baselines are limited to vanilla SGD-CLIP without comparing against other efficient contrastive learning methods.
  • Since efficiency is a claimed contribution, a convergence analysis (or at least a theoretical discussion) is needed to justify why the method should be more efficient.

Papers (1)
Nonparametric Unsupervised Data Condensation for Gigapixel Histological Images ICLR 2026 · rejected
The problem
Whole-slide images are several gigabytes each and cannot be used directly in standard vision pipelines; prior methods condense each slide into a fixed number of prototypes, which the abstract says overlooks differences in slide complexity and diversity and loses critical information.
The idea
NICER first decomposes each slide into a deliberately redundant set of feature patterns to capture heterogeneity, then condenses them into concept prototypes and prunes unused concepts, so the number of prototypes adapts to slide complexity.
Strengths
The abstract reports that NICER outperforms prior methods on four histological datasets with up to 90% performance gains; reviewers list cancer subtyping and survival prediction on PANDA, NSCLC, BRCA and LUAD against unsupervised prototype baselines including PANTHER.
What the authors leave open
  • The formulation does not explicitly model tissue architecture and spatial priors, which the authors name as an important direction for future work, particularly in clinically critical settings.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The “nonparametric” property arises mainly from pruning unused prototypes, not from a Bayesian nonparametric process.
  • Table 1 shows that NICER features still underperform the vanilla method (whole bag).
  • there is a complete and inexplicable absence of any empirical data regarding efficiency—no training times, no inference speeds, no memory usage comparisons.
  • The evaluation relies exclusively on public TCGA and PANDA datasets, which are known to exhibit site-specific staining and preprocessing artifacts.

Generative models (1 paper · CSRankings score 0.14)

Topic description

The paper addresses generating synthetic training images with diffusion models that follow precise 3D structure and pose. It scales down visual-prompt conditioning adaptively through a self-supervised modulator and uses a multi-agent vision-language model to write text prompts consistent with the 3D geometry. It reports improved image quality and dataset usefulness for downstream 2D and 3D vision tasks.

Papers (1)
AC3S: Adaptive Conditioning for 3D-Aware Synthetic Data Generation ECCV 2026 · accepted
The problem
Diffusion models can generate photorealistic synthetic training images, but enforcing precise 3D structure and pose in them is hard: visual prompts such as edge maps guide generation yet often over-condition the model, producing artifacts that lower realism and dataset quality.
The idea
AC3S adds a self-supervised visual prompt modulator that adaptively scales down ControlNet conditioning to avoid over-conditioning, and a multi-agent vision-language model (VLM) system that writes detailed text prompts consistent with the underlying 3D geometry.
Strengths
The paper reports that combining the two adaptive conditioning modules improves image quality and the usefulness of the generated datasets, which come with 2D and 3D annotations, for downstream 2D and 3D vision tasks.
