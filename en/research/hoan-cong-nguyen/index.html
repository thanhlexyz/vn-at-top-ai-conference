Research directions: Hoan Cong Nguyen

Hoan Cong Nguyen (UIT) · All professors

Work on generating and simulating environments and perception for aerial and endovascular robots. Work on generating drone simulation scenes and on learning for endovascular robots, from guidewire shape prediction to catheterization simulators.

Open challenges. Work on catheterization simulators and navigation leaves open evidence beyond simulated tasks, bias from simulator-made expert data, and clinical or porcine settings.

Papers (3)
AeroScene: Progressive Scene Synthesis for Aerial Robotics ICRA 2026 · accepted
The problem
Simulation environments for drones are largely built by hand, which is slow and hard to scale, and generative models for scene synthesis are underexplored in robotics.
The idea
A hierarchical diffusion model for progressive 3D scene synthesis, with hierarchy-aware tokenization and multi-branch feature extraction to reason over both global layouts and local details.
Strengths
Outperforms prior methods on a newly collected dataset and a public benchmark; generated over 1,000 physics-ready 3D scenes for NVIDIA Isaac Sim and used them in downstream drone navigation tasks.
What the authors leave open
  • Extending the synthesis process to generate dynamic elements is left as future work.

SplineFormer: An Explainable Transformer Network for Autonomous Endovascular Navigation IROS 2025 · accepted
The problem
Robot-assisted endovascular navigation requires precise control of guidewires, whose shape must be predicted in real time; segmentation-based approaches struggle with this.
The idea
A transformer network predicts the guidewire as a B-spline, giving a compact, smooth and explainable state representation used within an imitation learning framework for autonomous navigation.
Strengths
Achieves a 50% success rate when fully autonomously cannulating the Brachiocephalic Artery in a real robotic setup.
What the authors leave open
  • Future work will incorporate porcine-based environments and clinical data to bridge the gap toward real clinical use.

Autonomous Catheterization with Open-source Simulator and Expert Trajectory ICLR 2024 · rejected
The problem
Progress toward autonomous catheterization is hampered by widely used closed-source simulators and physical phantoms, and large datasets are infeasible to collect from expensive medical procedures.
The idea
An open-source, real-time simulator for endovascular intervention validated against a real robot, plus a multimodal expert navigation network trained on it for downstream navigation tasks.
Strengths
Reports that the simulator mimics the behaviour of the real robot and that the navigation network is effective on downstream endovascular navigation tasks.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The manuscript mainly created a new environment based on MuJoCo and only showed the effectiveness of the simulator in simulated tasks with rendered inputs.
  • Authors should clearly explain that the expert trajectory is creating using their own simulator, and therefore, may be biased so that the results may not be representative of practical use of their method.
  • 10 participants in user study is a small sample size
  • I would expect real-world images are highly diverse than rendering images that has clean background, unified rendering parameters, and a fixed camera position.
