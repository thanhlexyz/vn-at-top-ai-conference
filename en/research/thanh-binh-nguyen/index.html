Research directions: Thanh-Binh Nguyen

Thanh-Binh Nguyen (HCMUS) · All professors

Machine learning methods for efficient models, language model alignment, explanation, forecasting, federated learning and medical imaging. Work on aligning language models from offline preference data with importance sampling, and on retrieval-augmented compliance checking of Vietnamese medical advertisements. Work on server-trained generators of synthetic data that reduce catastrophic forgetting in federated class-incremental learning, plus fractional neural ODEs for trajectory forecasting. Work on domain generalization in hierarchical federated learning, token merging in Transformers and hypernetwork routing in sparse mixtures of experts.

Open challenges. Work on preference-based alignment leaves open the clipping threshold and preference-data assumptions; compliance checking leaves open semantic and logical reasoning errors. Work on generators for federated class-incremental learning leaves open client storage burden and the training cost of two generators, and what learned fractional orders capture. Work on hierarchical federated generalization with open model adaptation, token merging with open higher merge ratios and hypernetwork routing with open cross-layer sharing.

VietCheckMed: Explainable Regulatory Compliance Checking for Medical Advertisements on Vietnamese Social Media AAAI 2026 · accepted
The problem
Checking whether online medical advertisements comply with regulations needs authorization-based, evidence-grounded, explainable reasoning, particularly in low-resource languages; existing automated systems are ill-suited to it.
The idea
A retrieval-augmented framework that checks advertisements against an authoritative regulatory corpus of 9,978 facilities, plus an expert-validated benchmark of 8,329 Vietnamese advertisements.

Reports that the evidence-grounded approach outperforms unassisted LLM baselines by over 0.3805 F1-score; code, data and evaluation scripts are to be released.
What the authors leave open

Mitigating Reward Over-optimization in Direct Alignment Algorithms with Importance Sampling NeurIPS 2025 · accepted
The problem
Direct alignment algorithms such as DPO are prone to over-optimization, where the model drifts from the reference policy and degrades; the authors attribute this to the mismatch between the offline distribution and the language model policy.
The idea
Multiplies the alignment objective by an importance ratio that accounts for the reference policy distribution, clipped to a maximum value to avoid high variance.
Strengths
Reports mitigating over-optimization, especially under low regularization strength, with better performance than other methods designed for this problem.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The analysis of the online DPO baseline which is cited as a motivation to approximate is missing in the results.
  • it means that SFT on the preference dataset is likely required for IS-DPO to work.
  • However, the paper offers no principled way to set this crucial hyper-parameter, aside from a single ablation study shown in Figure 5.
  • the experimental results are a bit limited in comparison to several other modern LLM papers, however I understand computational constraints are a factor.

Deep learning and representation learning (2 papers · CSRankings score 0.45)

Topic description

In federated class-incremental learning, the server trains a data generator and a feature generator on all seen classes without client data and sends synthetic data to clients, which use feature-direction-controlling losses to retain old knowledge. Accuracy and forgetting improve over prior frameworks on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100 and tiny-ImageNet. A neural ODE with a per-axis fractional derivative and a co-evolving latent context forecasts chaotic systems and 3D kinematics better than baselines over long horizons, with proofs of an error bound, suppressed Lyapunov divergence and linear convergence.

Papers (2)
Context-fNODE: Context-aware Fractional Neural Ordinary Differential Equation ICLR 2027 · rejected
The problem
Standard neural ODEs are Markovian and miss the long-tail memory of physical motion; existing fractional neural models use one scalar order for all axes and assume closed, autonomous environments.
The idea
Replaces the scalar operator with a vectorized Grünwald-Letnikov fractional derivative (one order per axis) and couples it with a co-evolving first-order latent context that absorbs unobserved external perturbations.
Strengths
Reports better long-horizon forecasting than baselines on chaotic dynamical systems and 3D kinematics, and proves an error bound, suppression of Lyapunov divergence and a linear numerical convergence rate.
What the authors leave open
  • Future work will look at attention-modulated fractional integration, which would shrink the effective memory window when the context changes quickly.
  • Future work will focus on dynamic memory modulation and computational optimization.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The empirical advantage depends on one window size.
  • The paper also does not establish whether the adjoint method, a central technique for memory-efficient Neural ODE training, is applicable to this mixed-order model.
  • However, whether the new mechanisms actually learn these effects is unverified as the authors only measure model performance with respect to trajectory error.
  • the sensitivity of the long term trajectories to state and parameter error is an intrinsic property of chaotic systems, not deficiencies of the training method or the model.

The problem
In federated class-incremental learning, algorithms such as FedAvg suffer catastrophic forgetting, and generative-model approaches show lower test accuracy on previous classes than on recent ones (better plasticity than stability).
The idea
The server trains a data generator and a feature generator on all seen classes without accessing client data and sends synthetic data to clients, which use feature-direction-controlling losses to retain old knowledge and learn new tasks.
Strengths
Reports improved accuracy and forgetting measures on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100 and tiny-ImageNet, and analyzes robustness on natural images, convergence to flat local minima and calibration.
What the authors leave open

Questions raised by reviewers
  • The datasets used throughout the paper are relatively simple; I recommend conducting further experiments on high-resolution datasets such as ImageNet to strengthen the evaluation.
  • It seems that training two generators are quite costing, and it already needs more parameter during the training.
  • The framework contains too many hyperparameters, as each loss term needs one.
  • It is unclear whether the GAN trained after each task is independently trained or incrementally trained based on the previous task.

Accelerating Transformers with Spectrum-Preserving Token Merging NeurIPS 2024 · accepted
The problem
Token merging for Transformer throughput relies on Bipartite Soft Matching, which is sensitive to token-splitting strategies and damages informative tokens in later layers.
The idea
Scores tokens with an energy score: large clusters of similar tokens have high energy and are merge candidates, while small isolated clusters have low energy and are preserved; a theory shows preservation of spectral properties of the token space.
Strengths
Reports 40-60% fewer FLOPs, with a 0.5% average drop for ViT-MAE-H on image classification versus 2.6% for baselines and a 0.3% drop for CLIP on Flickr30k retrieval versus 4.5%, plus results on visual question answering with LLaVA-7B.
What the authors leave open
  • Extending the method to generative tasks such as image generation or segmentation requires an unmerge mechanism in the decoder, which remains an open question.
  • The energy score uses a fully connected graph of input tokens, which raises complexity as input size grows; sparse graphs might help scaling.
  • A differentiable mechanism to learn the reduction rate could improve robustness across downstream tasks.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The lack of results for higher merging ratios weakens the practicality of the proposed approach.
  • The proposed method, PITOME, does not show significant improvement over the baseline, particularly ToMe, in many tasks.
  • there is little discussion about the term $m$ when computing the energy score
  • There is a lack of discussion on related works (e.g., CrossGET [1], TRIPS[2]).

HyperRouter: Towards Efficient Training and Inference of Sparse Mixture of Experts EMNLP 2023 · accepted
The problem
Sparse Mixture-of-Experts routers can collapse so that all experts learn similar representations; fixing routers avoids this but gives possibly sub-optimal policies and requires extensive resources in training and evaluation.
The idea
Dynamically generates the router's parameters through a fixed hypernetwork and trainable embeddings, balancing training and freezing of routers.
Strengths
Reports superior performance and efficiency gains over existing routing methods across a wide range of tasks; the implementation is public.
What the authors leave open
  • Further gains may come from regularization (such as an l2 penalty or dropout) or better initialization of the fixed hypernetwork and trainable embeddings.
  • The current design uses one hypernetwork per Transformer layer generating all router parameters; sharing hypernetworks across layers or generating the router coordinate-wise may improve results.

Papers (1)
OptSHAP: Explaining Dimensionality Reduction-based Models for Tabular Data via Optimization ICLR 2026 · rejected
The problem
Dimensionality reduction-based models, which couple a projection with a predictor, hide the original features; model-agnostic explainers rely on sampling and give unstable, low-faithfulness explanations.
The idea
Computes attributions in the reduced space and redistributes them to the original features with an optimization-based transformation that satisfies the efficiency principle; also defines a k-Local Stability Score for stability of attribution methods.
Strengths
A reviewer's summary reports evaluation on three high-dimensional tabular datasets with PCA, ICA and LDA projections and four tree models, with better faithfulness and stability metrics than KernelSHAP, EIC and LIME.
What the authors leave open
  • Future work should investigate principled ways to quantify and mitigate uncertainty in the attributions, for a broader class of dimensionality reduction methods.
  • Future work should explore whether the observed trade-offs persist across other dimensionality reduction techniques, data distributions and model architectures.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The empirical results focus entirely on tree-based models as the predictor.
  • The formula is not derived from a theoretical decomposition of SHAP or any formal game-theoretic principle.
  • linear transformations are generally regarded as explainable/interpretable by themselves.
  • All the other competitors are black-box approaches and relatively old (earlier than 2017).

LVM-Med: Learning Large-Scale Self-Supervised Vision Models for Medical Imaging via Second-order Graph Matching NeurIPS 2023 · accepted
The problem
Large pre-trained models that fine-tune with few annotated samples remain an open challenge for medical imaging, since ImageNet and vision-language models face a domain shift from natural to medical images.
The idea
Collects about 1.3 million medical images from 55 public datasets (CT, MRI, X-ray, ultrasound), benchmarks self-supervised algorithms, and proposes a contrastive learning method based on second-order graph matching trained end-to-end with gradient estimation for black-box solvers.
Strengths
Evaluated on 15 downstream medical tasks (segmentation, classification, detection) in and out of distribution; reports gains of 6-7% on brain tumor classification and diabetic retinopathy grading over vision-language models trained on 1 billion masks, using only a ResNet-50.
What the authors leave open
  • Extending the model to a hybrid 2D-3D architecture would allow direct application to 3D medical tasks instead of 2D slices.
  • The ViT backbone has more parameters but is in some cases less effective than the ResNet-50 backbone, raising the question of whether a new approach could improve ViT.
  • Integrating multimodal information such as knowledge graphs, bio-text or electronic health records is needed to make the model more useful in real-world applications.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • Only 2D backbone is conducted: The LVM-Med model focuses primarily on 2D backbone.
  • The paper did not provide enough details regarding the proposed datasets, especially in terms of the usage of the dataset
  • The reproducibility of the results and methods is a concern.
  • The model's performance benefits seem to be tied to its training on a large-scale medical imaging dataset.
