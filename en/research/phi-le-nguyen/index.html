Research directions: Phi Le Nguyen

Phi Le Nguyen (HUST) · All professors

Prof. Le studies machine learning with limited, incomplete or shifted data, often building datasets for low-resource settings like Vietnamese. Work on condensing gigapixel pathology slides, medical image grounding and report generation, clinical records and EEG data, and neural simulation of coupled physics. Work on regularizing surrogate models in offline optimization by sensitivity or sharpness, and on translating low-value designs into high-value ones with a probabilistic bridge. Work on domain generalization by unified style mapping, cross-modal distillation without paired data, test-time adaptation and noise-resilient time-series representations.

Open challenges. Work on condensing gigapixel pathology slides leaves open gains versus prototype budget, the gap to whole-slide features, site-specific data artifacts and scripted clinical speech. Work on translating low-value designs into high-value ones leaves open how the design of the probabilistic bridge affects learning performance. Work on noise-resilient time-series representations leaves open the high-frequency noise assumption, evaluation on truly unpaired data, bi-level optimization cost and training on unseen-like domains.

AI for health and science (9 papers · CSRankings score 1.27)

Topic description

Medical vision-language models are made to attend to disease regions through disease-aware prompting, with a 20.74% grounding gain on chest X-rays, and medical visual question answering is trained to localize the pathological region before answering. PET/CT reports are generated region by region with a fine-grained dataset and graph-based modules, and clinical reasoning generated by LLMs is added to risk prediction from health records, with gains of up to 11.2%. A Vietnamese speech-to-record dataset and a multi-institution EEG benchmark are built, and a one-step flow-matching neural operator is proposed for coupled multi-physics PDEs. Gigapixel whole-slide images are condensed by decomposing each slide into a redundant set of feature patterns, condensing them into concept prototypes and pruning unused ones, so that the number of prototypes adapts to slide complexity, with gains of up to 90% over prior methods on four histological datasets. Whole-slide image condensation is recast as matching the distribution of patch features under the squared 2-Wasserstein distance with a nonparametric prior that lets the number of prototypes adapt to each slide, with an average 7.44% accuracy gain on five histopathology datasets and a pathologist's clinical evaluation.

ViMed-Struct: Structured EMR Generation from Vietnamese Clinical Speech ICLR 2027 · rejected
The problem
Physicians spend substantial effort turning patient encounters into structured electronic medical record (EMR) fields; automating this from speech is held back by the scarcity of paired clinical audio and structured EMR annotations, especially for low-resource languages such as Vietnamese.
The idea
A framework that derives structured targets directly from existing hospital EMR data and separates them from how the content is spoken, removing manual structured annotation; it is used to build ViMed-Struct, with 2,572 clinical cases and 2,892 recordings (47.99 hours) from 58 speakers in matched quiet and noisy conditions.
Strengths
According to the reviews, benchmarks direct speech-to-structure models and cascaded speech recognition plus LLM pipelines, scores field presence separately from value quality, and reports up to 94% macro field-presence F1 for extraction from reference transcripts.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The benchmark uses scripted clinical speech rather than natural clinical interactions.
  • The paper does not clearly establish patient-disjoint, speaker-disjoint, or hospital-disjoint evaluation.
  • Several quantitative claims in the text don't match the paper's own tables, the evaluation setup can't yet support the reported differences
  • Presence F1 only evaluates whether a field is populated, irrespective of whether its content is correct.

Nonparametric Distribution Matching for Self-Supervised Whole-Slide Image Condensation NeurIPS 2026 · accepted
The problem
Histological whole-slide images (WSIs) have extremely high resolution, often several gigabytes per slide; existing self-supervised condensation methods compress each slide into a few representative features (prototypes) but rely on heuristic prototype learning that does not explicitly preserve the feature distributions downstream tasks use.
The idea
NICER recasts WSI condensation as matching the distribution of patch features under a fixed pretrained encoder, measured with the squared 2-Wasserstein (optimal transport) distance, and approximates it with a nonparametric prior (a Poisson point process, per the reviews) that lets the number of prototypes adapt to each slide.
Strengths
The abstract reports an average accuracy improvement of 7.44% over prior methods on five histopathology datasets, improved efficiency-accuracy trade-offs, and a clinical evaluation by a board-certified pathologist.
What the authors leave open
  • The analysis uses the squared 2-Wasserstein distance as the matching distance and leaves other distances to future work.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The bag-of-features assumption treats patches as independent, discarding spatial context information—yet spatial structure carries significant biological meaning in histology
  • the PPP prior assumes that concepts are mutually independent, which may be overly simplistic for frequently co-occurring related morphological structures
  • the final implementation still relies on several approximation and design choices. As a result, it is not fully clear whether the theoretical formulation directly leads to the proposed algorithm, or mainly serves as a high-level motivation.
  • it is not fully clear whether the performance gain mainly comes from the proposed method design or from retaining more information through a larger storage/prototype budget.

CoupledFlow: One-Step Neural Operators for Coupled Multi-Physics PDEs NeurIPS 2026 · accepted
The problem
Multi-physics PDE systems, in which several physical processes evolve together and interact, are expensive to simulate numerically, and most neural operators (networks that learn the solution map of a PDE) target single-physics settings.
The idea
CoupledFlow uses structure-constrained flow matching to learn process-specific mean velocity fields conditioned on the full multi-physics state, allowing one-step prediction; its loss comes from a derived multi-physics mean-flow consistency identity, and its training interpolants are sampled from posterior multi-output Gaussian processes instead of independent linear interpolants.
Strengths
Proves that the Wasserstein-2 distance between predicted and true PDE solution distributions is upper-bounded by the flow matching loss and the smoothness of the learned flow, and reports outperforming existing neural operators and generative PDE solvers on coupled PDE benchmarks.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The current evaluation relies mainly on relative 𝐿2 prediction error, which is insufficient to demonstrate that the model has actually learned better physical coupling structure.
  • Only fixed coefficients and boundary conditions are considered in the experiments.
  • It seems that this achieves quite good performance and there is no statistically-significant gain from using the GP-derived interpolant (costly with a cubic computation).

Seeing the Trees for the Forest: Rethinking Weakly-Supervised Medical Visual Grounding ICCV 2025 · accepted
The problem
Medical visual grounding, locating the image region that matches a text description such as a disease finding in a chest X-ray, supports interpretability, but current vision-language models (VLMs) struggle to link text to disease regions without pixel-level labels.
The idea
Shows empirically that medical VLMs assign high norms to background tokens and that global tokens do not represent local disease tokens, and proposes Disease-Aware Prompting (DAP), which uses the model's explainability map to amplify disease regions and suppress background.
Strengths
Reports a 20.74% gain in grounding accuracy over prior methods on three chest X-ray datasets without pixel-level annotations, and a 32% CNR improvement over pixel-masking prompting.

CARER - ClinicAl Reasoning-Enhanced Representation for Temporal Health Risk Prediction EMNLP 2024 · accepted
The problem
Deep learning models for health risk prediction from electronic health records (EHR) need large datasets to generalize.
The idea
CARER adds clinical reasoning about the patient's history, generated by medically capable LLMs, to multimodal deep learning models, and aligns the patient-data ('local') view with the LLM-reasoning ('global') view through a cross-view alignment loss.
Strengths
Reports up to 11.2% improvement over prior models on two prediction tasks with two EHR datasets, especially in data efficiency, and similar results when GPT-3.5 is replaced by open-source LLMs (Qwen2-7B, Mixtral-8x7B, Llama 3-8B).
What the authors leave open
  • Experiments cover two health risk prediction tasks; other clinical prediction tasks such as mortality prediction, hospital readmission and medication recommendation are not tested.
  • Large pretrained text encoders and LLMs bring heavy computational cost and processing time, a challenge for facilities with limited computing resources or time-critical use.
  • Using proprietary models such as GPT-3.5 may not be feasible in clinical settings because of budget and privacy constraints.

Optimization methods (5 papers · CSRankings score 1.15)

Topic description

Offline black-box optimization searches for designs, such as materials, molecules or robot morphologies, that maximize an unknown function using only a fixed dataset of past evaluations. One line of papers regularizes the learned surrogate model as an add-on to existing optimizers, penalizing its sensitivity to parameter perturbations or its sharpness through the gradient norm; another recasts the task as translating low-value designs into high-value ones with a diffusion-style or probabilistic bridge trained on synthetic data from Gaussian processes. The papers report gains on Design-Bench tasks, up to 9.6% for the sharpness regularizer, and new best results for the bridge-based method.

Papers (5)
ROOT: Rethinking Offline Optimization as Distributional Translation via Probabilistic Bridge NeurIPS 2025 · accepted
The problem
Offline black-box optimization looks for inputs that maximize an unknown function using only a fixed dataset of past input-output pairs; both surrogate-based methods (learn a model of the function, then optimize it) and inverse-modeling methods (map a target value back to inputs) are limited by the small amount of offline data.
The idea
ROOT casts the task as distributional translation: a probabilistic bridge learns to transform a distribution of low-value inputs into one of high-value inputs, and is trained on low- and high-value pairs sampled from synthetic functions built as the posterior means of many Gaussian processes fitted to the offline data.
Strengths
Reports new best results on a benchmark that includes the most recent offline optimization methods (four Design-Bench tasks and three RNA inverse-folding tasks according to the reviews).
What the authors leave open
  • How the choice of bridge design affects learning performance is stated as an open question.
  • Future directions named by the authors are alternative bridge parameterizations and extending the framework to more complex optimization settings.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • One potential concern is that the synthetic functions may not closely reflect real-world tasks, such as biological sequence design.
  • there is no theoretical guarantee that the learned bridge preserves function maxima
  • GP‑derived synthetic data may embed biases of the initial kernel, potentially restricting exploration.

Incorporating Surrogate Gradient Norm to Improve Offline Optimization Techniques NeurIPS 2024 · accepted
The problem
Offline optimization replaces costly online experiments with a surrogate learned from past queries, but the surrogate becomes inaccurate outside the offline data, and existing conditioning techniques are tied to particular surrogate or search models.
The idea
IGNITE is a model-agnostic regularizer that adds surrogate sharpness, the largest change in the surrogate's output under small perturbations of its parameters (approximated through its gradient norm), to the surrogate's training loss.
Strengths
Proves that reducing surrogate sharpness on the offline data reduces its generalized sharpness on unseen data, and reports up to 9.6% improvement, with a 91% probability of not degrading and a 79.55% probability of improving the base optimizer.
What the authors leave open
  • Adapting the technique to robust optimization and reinforcement learning is named as a future direction.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The effectiveness of the method heavily depends on the correct setting of hyperparameters like the sharpness threshold, which can be tricky to optimize without extensive experimentation.
  • Assumption 2 (positive minimum eigenvalue of the parameter Hessian) might be too restrictive.
  • the overall improvements are marginal and sometimes even decrease, as shown in table 1.
  • The proposed method involves computationally expensive operations, such as gradient norm calculations and constrained optimization.

Boosting Offline Optimizers with Surrogate Sensitivity ICML 2024 · accepted
The problem
In offline optimization, for example in material engineering where online experiments are too expensive, a surrogate learned from offline data may predict unreliably outside the data, which happens when it is sensitive to small perturbations of its parameters.
The idea
Develops an optimizable measure of surrogate sensitivity and, from it, a sensitivity-informed regularizer that can be attached to a wide range of offline optimizers.
Strengths
Reports on a benchmark of experiments that the regularizer is complementary to, and combines with, prior offline optimizers.
What the authors leave open
  • The regularizer is not a standalone method: its effectiveness depends on the base performance of the optimizer it is coupled with.
  • Whether the paper's notion of a robust certificate would be accepted by experts in robust machine learning is left for further investigation.

Deep learning and representation learning (4 papers · CSRankings score 0.80)

Topic description

Knowledge is distilled from a teacher trained on one modality to a student on another without paired samples, by aligning distributions, with infinite-sample and finite-sample generalization bounds and gains in the unpaired setting on four benchmarks. Models are adapted at test time by minimizing prediction entropy on likely-correct samples and maximizing it on likely-incorrect ones, with a theoretical analysis of the separation and gains of up to 8.06% over the second-best method. Training and test data are mapped into a unified domain for domain generalization, with up to 19.82% higher accuracy than the next best approach when few seen domains are available. Noise-resilient time-series representations are learned from low-pass-filtered views with an Inception-style dilated-convolution encoder, with improvements on forecasting, classification and anomaly detection using fewer parameters.

Papers (4)
Cross-Modal Knowledge Distillation without Paired Data: Theoretical Foundation and Algorithm ICML 2026 · accepted
The problem
Cross-modal knowledge distillation (CMKD), where a teacher trained on one modality (e.g. images) guides a student on another (e.g. audio), usually requires paired multimodal samples, which are costly or impractical to collect.
The idea
Derives a distributional relationship between teacher and student that identifies two quantities governing distillation, feature alignment and label alignment, and builds UCMKD, which aligns distributions rather than individual samples with a meta-learning-style optimization.
Strengths
Establishes infinite-sample and finite-sample generalization bounds for the student and reports improvements in the unpaired setting on AVE, RAVDESS, CREMA-D and VGGSound, while remaining competitive when paired data are available.
What the authors leave open
  • The formulation extends beyond prediction to general distribution-matching losses; the authors name large-scale multimodal transfer and unsupervised cross-modal generative modeling as open directions.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The unpaired setting is simulated by random permutation of paired datasets.
  • the bi-level optimization process used seems computationally expensive and seems to me that it might not be scalable in real-world scenarios.
  • some parts of the theory seem to serve more as high-level motivation than as a tight characterization of the implemented method.
  • Severe lack of baselines in the core (unpaired) setting.

Dual Strategies for Test-Time Adaptation CVPR 2026 · rejected
The problem
Test-time adaptation (TTA), updating a model on unlabeled test data under distribution shift, typically uses only a small fraction of test samples, often those with low-entropy predictions.
The idea
DualTTA splits test samples into likely-correct and likely-incorrect groups with a reliability criterion based on prediction stability under semantic-preserving and semantic-altering transformations, then minimizes prediction entropy on the first group and maximizes it on the second.
Strengths
Provides theoretical analysis of a tighter separation between reliable and unreliable samples and reports gains of up to 8.06% over the second-best TTA method across multiple datasets.

Phantasia: Context-Adaptive Backdoors in Vision Language Models CVPR 2026 · rejected
The problem
Backdoor attacks on vision-language models (hidden triggers that make the model output attacker-chosen content) mostly produce fixed, easily identifiable poisoned responses, and their stealthiness had not been tested against adapted defenses.
The idea
Adapts defenses designed for vision-only and text-only models (including a STRIP variant, STRIP-P) to VLM backdoors, and proposes Phantasia, a context-adaptive backdoor whose poisoned outputs follow the semantics of each input.

Back-Tracking from Clarity: Self-Learning to See Text from Afar ECCV 2026 · accepted
The problem
Scene text detectors miss text seen from far away, where instances are small and blurred, and manually annotating such distant text is costly.
The idea
A self-supervised framework that takes confident detections from an existing text spotter on large, clear text and back-tracks them through the video to earlier frames with a dedicated scene text tracker (combining point tracking and carrier tracking), producing pseudo-labels to train a student detector without manual annotation; it also introduces SceneText50, a multilingual outdoor dataset.
Strengths
Reports that the tracker outperforms existing trackers on multiple scene text datasets, and that fine-tuning several detectors on the generated labels raises recall and detects text earlier in video than standard augmentation.

The problem
Large vision-language models (LVLMs) hallucinate objects, and existing detectors use whole-image relevance scores, in which weak correlations scattered over many regions can add up to a deceptively high relevance.

MAGE: Towards Generalizable Multi-timescale EEG Representations NeurIPS 2026 · accepted
The problem
EEG foundation models (large models pretrained on unlabeled brain-signal recordings and reused across tasks) usually process the signal at one fixed temporal scale, while neural dynamics recorded by EEG span multiple timescales.
The idea
MAGE cuts the EEG into patches of several sizes, learns representations at each scale with a multi-timescale attention mechanism, and uses a trainable aggregation module that weights the timescales according to the input signal.
