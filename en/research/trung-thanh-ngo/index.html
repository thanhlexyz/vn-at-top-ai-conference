Research directions: Trung Thanh Ngo

Trung Thanh Ngo (HUST) · All professors

Prof. Trung works on recovering the 3D shape and material properties of objects from images. Work on inverse rendering of translucent objects estimates shape and light scattering inside the material from very few images, using flash photos or polarized light.

Open challenges. Work on inverse rendering of translucent objects leaves open scattering that varies across the object and capture without a flash and no-flash image pair.

Computer vision and multimodal learning (2 papers · CSRankings score 0.67)

Topic description

One paper jointly estimates 3D shape, spatially varying reflectance, homogeneous subsurface scattering parameters and environment lighting from a flash and no-flash image pair, combining differentiable physically based and neural renderers through a reconstruction loss. It is trained on a synthetic dataset of 117K translucent-object scenes. The other paper observes that subsurface scattering also changes the polarization of light and uses a single shot of polarization images to estimate shape and scattering parameters together, reporting better results than shape-from-polarization and inverse-rendering baselines on synthetic and real data.

Papers (2)
Deep Polarization Cues for Single-shot Shape and Subsurface Scattering Estimation ECCV 2024 · accepted
The problem
The paper estimates the shape of translucent objects together with their subsurface scattering (SSS) parameters, which describe how light penetrates the material, scatters inside and exits elsewhere. Polarization cues have been used for shape from polarization (SfP), BRDF (bidirectional reflectance distribution function) estimation and reflection removal, but, according to the paper, not yet for SSS estimation.
The idea
A learning-based method jointly estimates the shape and SSS parameters of a translucent object from a single shot of polarization images, based on the authors' observation that SSS affects not only light intensity but also the polarization signal; it is trained on what the authors describe as the first large-scale synthetic dataset of polarized translucent objects.
Strengths
The paper reports that the method outperforms several baselines from shape from polarization and inverse rendering on both synthetic and real data, in qualitative and quantitative comparisons.

Inverse Rendering of Translucent Objects Using Physical and Neural Renderers CVPR 2023 · accepted
The problem
Inverse rendering recovers 3D shape, reflectance, scattering and lighting from images, a problem the paper describes as ambiguous; for translucent objects, subsurface scattering (light entering the material and exiting elsewhere) must be estimated as well.
The idea
From a flash and no-flash image pair, the model jointly estimates 3D shape, spatially varying reflectance, homogeneous subsurface scattering parameters and environment illumination; it uses a physically based renderer and a neural renderer, both differentiable, so a reconstruction loss can guide the estimates, and adds an augmented loss to strengthen supervision of the neural renderer.
Strengths
The authors build a synthetic dataset of 117K translucent-object scenes for training, report qualitative and quantitative results on synthetic and real-world data, and release code and data.
