Research directions: Cuong Do

Cuong Do (VinUni) · All professors

Machine learning kept reliable under physical constraints, uncertainty and distribution shift, applied to air quality, medical imaging and federated learning. Work on learned rejection policies lets medical image classifiers abstain by training a small gating network on ensemble confidence and disagreement. Work on federated domain generalization uses normalization layers that filter client-specific features and a regularizer that encourages domain-invariant representations. Work on closed-loop physics-informed forecasting of air quality: a physical simulator is corrected by a learned model at each step to reduce long-horizon drift.

Open challenges. Work on learned rejection policies leaves open validation beyond twelve medical imaging classification benchmarks. Work on federated domain generalization leaves open applicability beyond models built on normalization layers. Work on physics-informed air quality forecasting leaves open explicit chemical constraints, reliance on future meteorological inputs, and systematic evaluation of uncertainty.

Trustworthy AI (1 paper · CSRankings score 0.20)

Topic description

Selective prediction lets a model abstain on inputs it considers unreliable and defer them to a human expert. The paper trains a lightweight gating network on the consensus confidence and the variance of a deep ensemble to predict misclassification, instead of applying a static threshold to the mean prediction. Across twelve medical imaging benchmarks (MRI, X-ray, CT), it reports an average 68.2% reduction in area under the risk-coverage curve (AURC) compared with the standard ensemble baseline.

Papers (1)
DEGRE: Dynamic Gating Ensembles for Trust-Aware Rejection in Medical Image Diagnostics AAAI 2026 · accepted
The problem
For safe deployment in high-risk domains, models must know their limits; selective prediction lets a model abstain on unreliable inputs and defer them to a human expert, but rejection methods that apply static thresholds to the mean prediction of a deep ensemble waste the ensemble's information.
The idea
A meta-learning approach that trains a lightweight gating network on the ensemble's consensus confidence and its internal disagreement (variance) to discriminate between correct and incorrect predictions, learning a dynamic rejection policy instead of a fixed heuristic.
Strengths
Across twelve medical imaging benchmarks (MRI, X-ray, CT), the method reports an average risk-coverage (AURC) reduction of 68.2% compared with the standard ensemble baseline.

Deep learning and representation learning (1 paper · CSRankings score 0.20)

Topic description

Domain shift degrades a model tested on unseen domains, and federated domain generalization trains a global model across clients so that it works on unseen clients while keeping data private. The paper designs a normalization scheme that lets each client model filter features biased toward its local data, plus a simple regularizer that guides models toward domain-invariant representations the global classifier can use. It reports better results than existing methods on two benchmarks (PACS, Office-Home) and a medical dataset (Camelyon17).

Papers (1)
Efficiently Assemble Normalization Layers and Regularization for Federated Domain Generalization CVPR 2024 · accepted
The problem
Domain shift degrades a model tested on unseen domains; federated domain generalization trains a global model across collaborating clients that generalizes to unseen clients, but most existing methods add privacy risks of data leakage or large client communication and computation costs.
The idea
An architectural method: Personalized eXplicitly Assembled Normalization lets client models selectively filter domain-specific features biased toward local data while retaining their discrimination, and a simple regularizer guides the models to capture domain-invariant representations that the global classifier can use.
Strengths
Experiments on PACS, Office-Home and the real-world medical dataset Camelyon17 report that the method outperforms existing methods.
What the authors leave open
  • A potential limitation is the method's suitability only for models that use normalization layers.

AI for health and science (1 paper · CSRankings score 0.17)

Topic description

Long-term air quality forecasting is hard because of long-range transport, varying weather and the nonlinear chemistry behind secondary pollutants. The paper couples a process model based on a universal differential equation with a Mamba-based branch (a state space sequence model) that produces probabilistic virtual observations, and corrects the latent physical state at each step with a Kalman-style fusion. It also introduces a new air quality dataset of 27 monitoring stations in Northern Vietnam and reports improved long-horizon stability and sudden-change forecasting on that dataset and a public benchmark.

Papers (1)
AeroChem: Closed-loop Physics-Informed State Space Modeling for Long-term Chemically-Reactive Air Quality Forecasting NeurIPS 2026 · accepted
The problem
Accurate long-term air quality forecasting is hard because of long-range transport, meteorological variability and nonlinear chemical reactions behind secondary pollutant formation; purely data-driven models drift over long horizons and lack physical consistency, while existing physics-informed approaches are open-loop and fuse neural and physical components only at the output level.
The idea
A closed-loop physics-informed state space framework with two branches: a process branch based on a universal differential equation (UDE) gives a structured but imperfect physical prior, and a Mamba-based virtual measurement branch captures long-range temporal context and produces probabilistic virtual observations. A Neural Kalman Fusion step corrects the latent physical state at each forecasting step and feeds the posterior state back into the simulator. The paper also introduces a real-world dataset of pollutant concentrations, meteorology and emission inventories.
Strengths
Experiments on the new dataset and a public benchmark show improved long-horizon stability and sudden-change forecasting.
What the authors leave open
  • Future work will extend the framework to richer multi-species chemical interaction modeling and probabilistic risk assessment for environmental decision-making.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The paper emphasizes “physics-informed” and “chemically reactive,” but the actual chemical reaction component is mainly implemented through neural attention and meteorological gating, without explicitly constraining stoichiometric relationships, mass conservation, known reaction pathways, or reaction-rate laws.
  • The method relies on future meteorological variables and emission inputs.
  • the discussion of uncertainty calibration, prediction-interval coverage, and uncertainty behavior in sudden-change or long-horizon scenarios remains limited.
  • Experiments are only applied over Northern Vietnam and apparently coastal China.
