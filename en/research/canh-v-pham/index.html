Research directions: Canh V. Pham

Canh V. Pham (Phenikaa) · All professors

Combinatorial optimization on sets and graphs: approximation algorithms with provable guarantees and a learned generative solver. Work on parallel algorithms with linear queries for non-monotone submodular maximization, fair k-submodular maximization under matroids, knapsack-constrained submodular maximization and linear-query knapsack algorithms. Work on learned generative solvers for network problems that mix variational autoencoders, energy-based guidance and reinforcement learning to degrade service quality under budgets.

Open challenges. Work on fair k-submodular maximization with open richer constraint settings and parallel linear-query algorithms with open proof rigor and per-metric gains. Work on generalizing beyond good initial solutions and to unseen or dynamic networks, with accuracy of the learned estimator as a bottleneck.

Optimization methods (5 papers · CSRankings score 1.33)

Topic description

A paper on non-monotone submodular maximization under a cardinality constraint gives a constant-approximation algorithm with O(log n) adaptive rounds and linear query complexity, needing only O(1) guesses of the optimal value, improving the approximation ratio from 0.172-epsilon to 0.193-epsilon and the query complexity from O(n log k) to O(n), with better solution quality, query count and running time than state-of-the-art methods on benchmark datasets. A paper on fairness k-submodular maximization under a matroid constraint gives a constant-factor approximation algorithm for general non-monotone objectives, using a two-stage framework that first ensures feasibility for the upper fairness bounds and is then extended to enforce all of them, with competitive objective values on benchmark datasets. A paper on boosting parallel algorithms with linear queries for non-monotone submodular maximization uses a staggered greedy threshold framework that alternately builds two disjoint solution sets over O(log k) sequential rounds, with a boosted variant reaching 0.25-epsilon at O(log n log k) adaptive complexity and O(n log k) queries. A paper on non-monotone submodular maximization under a knapsack constraint builds two disjoint candidate solutions in an alternate threshold framework within a constant number of sequential rounds, improving the best known approximation factor of a parallel algorithm from 8 to 7 with O(log n) adaptive complexity, with higher solution quality on revenue maximization, image summarization and maximum weighted cut. A paper on linear-query algorithms for non-monotone submodular maximization under a knapsack constraint gives the first constant-factor algorithms with linear query complexity, a deterministic one with factor nearly 6 and a randomized one with factor nearly 4, returning results comparable to state-of-the-art algorithms with significantly fewer queries.

Papers (5)
Efficient Parallel Algorithms with Linear Queries for Non-Monotone Submodular Maximization IJCAI 2026 · accepted
The problem
Maximizing a non-monotone submodular function (a set function with diminishing returns whose value can drop when elements are added) under a cardinality constraint k over n elements, using few adaptive rounds (sequential batches of parallel function queries) and few total queries.
The idea
One algorithm reaches a constant approximation ratio with O(log n) adaptive rounds and linear query complexity by needing only O(1) candidate guesses of the optimal value. A second algorithm uses a staggered greedy threshold framework that alternately builds two disjoint solution sets over O(log k) sequential rounds.
Strengths
Reports the first constant-approximation algorithms with O(n) query complexity and O(log n) adaptive complexity, improving the approximation ratio from 0.172-epsilon to 0.193-epsilon and the query complexity from O(n log k) to O(n). A boosted variant reaches 0.25-epsilon with O(log n log k) adaptive complexity and O(n log k) queries. Experiments on standard benchmark datasets show better solution quality, query count and running time than state-of-the-art methods.

Fairness k-Submodular Maximization Subject to Matroid Constraint IJCAI 2026 · accepted
The problem
Fairness k-submodular maximization: choosing k disjoint subsets that maximize a general non-monotone k-submodular objective under a matroid constraint, subject to fairness bounds. Earlier work was limited to monotone objectives or simple size constraints.
The idea
A two-stage framework: a first algorithm guarantees feasibility with respect to the upper fairness bounds only, and is then extended systematically to enforce all fairness bounds while keeping the approximation guarantee.
Strengths
Gives a constant-factor approximation algorithm for the general non-monotone objective under a matroid constraint. Experiments on standard benchmark datasets report competitive objective values and a favorable balance between fairness guarantees and query complexity against state-of-the-art methods.
What the authors leave open
  • The authors name extending the framework to richer and more general constraint settings as an important direction for future work.

Improved Parallel Algorithm for Non-Monotone Submodular Maximization under Knapsack Constraint IJCAI 2024 · accepted
The problem
Maximizing a non-monotone submodular function under a knapsack constraint (items with costs and a total budget) with a parallel algorithm, that is, with few adaptive rounds.
The idea
An alternate threshold framework that alternately builds two disjoint candidate solutions within a constant number of sequential rounds, then boosts solution quality without increasing adaptive complexity.
Strengths
Improves the best known approximation factor of a parallel algorithm from 8 to 7 with O(log n) adaptive complexity. Experiments on revenue maximization, image summarization and maximum weighted cut report higher solution quality with adaptivity comparable to state-of-the-art algorithms.

Linear Query Approximation Algorithms for Non-monotone Submodular Maximization under Knapsack Constraint IJCAI 2023 · accepted
The problem
Maximizing a non-monotone submodular function under a knapsack constraint using only a linear number of function queries in the ground-set size n.
The idea
Divide the ground set into two suitable subsets and find a near-optimal solution over them with linear queries, then combine a threshold greedy with properties of two disjoint sets (deterministic version) or a random selection process (randomized version).
Strengths
Introduces, for the first time, two constant-factor algorithms with linear query complexity: a deterministic one with factor nearly 6 and a randomized one with factor nearly 4. On revenue maximization, image summarization and maximum weighted cut they return results comparable to state-of-the-art algorithms with significantly fewer queries.

Generative models (1 paper · CSRankings score 0.17)

Topic description

This paper treats an adversary that perturbs network edge weights within a budget to degrade service quality, including nonlinear edge-weight functions. It combines a path-based approximation algorithm, a mixture of conditional variational autoencoders guided by an energy-based model, and a reinforcement learning agent in latent space. Experiments on synthetic and real networks report lower cost than classical and learning-based baselines.

Papers (1)
Hephaestus: Mixture Generative Modeling with Energy Guidance for Large-scale QoS Degradation NeurIPS 2025 · accepted
The problem
Quality of Service Degradation (QoSD): an adversary perturbs edge weights of a network, within a budget, to degrade network performance. Classical methods use combinatorial optimization, and earlier machine-learning approaches cover only restricted linear variants on small networks, not nonlinear edge-weight functions.
The idea
A three-phase generative framework: (1) a path-stressing algorithm that uses graph learning and approximation to produce feasible solutions with a performance guarantee; (2) a mixture of conditional variational autoencoders (VAEs, generative models with a latent space) guided by an energy-based model to capture solution distributions; (3) a reinforcement learning agent exploring the latent space with a differentiable reward.
Strengths
Experiments on synthetic and real-world networks report that it outperforms classical and machine-learning baselines in cost, feasibility and scalability, particularly with nonlinear cost functions where traditional methods fail to generalize.
What the authors leave open
  • The framework depends on a reasonably good approximation algorithm to produce the initial solution dataset, and its success relies on the quality of those initial solutions.
  • Generalization to unseen networks depends on how well the graph neural network estimator predicts shortest-path costs on new graphs and on the quality of the graph representation fed to the generative model.
  • Future work could improve generalization with graph-invariant features, more diverse graph distributions for training, or more expressive graph-based foundation models.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The current setup assumes static graph topologies and fixed critical pairs.
  • The framework relies on SPAGAN's accuracy—robustness to prediction errors in large, noisy graphs is not extensively discussed.
  • The expert expansion mechanism in Mix-CVAE adds computational overhead; resource usage and efficiency trade-offs are not evaluated.
  • The paper does not compare with recent powerful generative optimization methods (e.g., diffusion-based solvers or large-scale neural MIP solvers).
