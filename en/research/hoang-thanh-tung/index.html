Research directions: Hoang Thanh-Tung

Hoang Thanh-Tung (VNU-UET) · All professors

Reliability of deep models under distribution shift, label noise, backdoors and unlearning. Work on unlearning robustness and representation steering in large language models, and on clean-label backdoors from target-class data only. Work on label noise in conditional diffusion models uses an adversarially trained discriminator as guidance to realign pre-trained models without retraining. Work on recurrent test-time computation studies how extra inference iterations help under distribution shift and how to stop before accuracy drops.

Open challenges. Work on unlearning and backdoor robustness leaves open knowledge hidden rather than erased, relearning and jailbreak tests, and evaluation against only older defense strategies. Work on guiding noisy-label diffusion models so far covers pre-trained conditional models in the tested noise settings; other settings are not stated. Work on label-free stopping of iterations still relies on a proxy task staying correlated with accuracy; extension to language models is open.

Papers (3)
Improving LLM Unlearning Robustness via Random Perturbations ICLR 2026 · rejected
The problem
Current large language model (LLM) unlearning methods reduce robustness: unlearned models misbehave even when a single non-adversarial forget-token appears in a retain-query.
The idea
Reframes unlearning as a backdoor attack and defense problem: forget-tokens act as backdoor triggers; the retaining process is reinterpreted as a backdoor defense by adding small random Gaussian noise to retain-query representations during fine-tuning, a model- and method-agnostic approach with theoretical guarantees.
Strengths
Reports that the approach significantly improves the robustness of unlearned models while preserving forget and retain performance, with experiments on several 7B-8B LLMs.
What the authors leave open
  • The authors state that current LLM unlearning methods do not truly erase knowledge; they suppress and redirect how the target knowledge and behaviors are expressed under trigger conditions.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • Many proofs rely on overly strong assumptions. For example, Eq. (9) asserts that adding Gaussian perturbations increases the expected loss, which depends on a strong assumption of local convexity or a positive-definite Hessian.
  • It would be valuable to test the method against relearning and jailbreaking attacks to more comprehensively assess robustness.
  • The proposed method requires manipulation of the model's inner layers, which may be impractical for real-world scenarios where users cannot modify these layers to inject random noise.
  • The experiments focus mainly on the WMDP benchmark and a single model scale.

Wicked Oddities: Selectively Poisoning for Effective Clean-Label Backdoor Attacks ICLR 2025 · accepted
The problem
Clean-label backdoor attacks poison training data without changing labels, but early versions add triggers to a random subset, ignoring that samples contribute unequally; existing selection strategies need the whole labeled training set and training, which is expensive and not always practical.
The idea
The paper studies a threat model where the attacker only supplies target-class data and knows nothing of the victim model or other classes, and proposes strategies that find hard samples within the target class to poison, using a pretrained model or an out-of-distribution dataset.
Strengths
The paper reports high attack success rates compared to baselines on benchmark datasets and that the attacks can bypass several representative defenses.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The evaluation is conducted only on CIFAR-10 and GTSRB datasets, limiting insight into the method's performance across other dataset types and application domains.
  • The paper primarily tests against older defense strategies.
  • The pretrained model strategy relies on the availability of pretrained models in similar domains, which may not always be accessible in real-world applications.
  • The OOD approach rely on out-of-distribution data but it’s not clear how this dataset could be obtained

On Effects of Steering Latent Representation for Large Language Model Unlearning AAAI 2025 · accepted
The problem
A method that steers intermediate-layer representations of forget samples to a random target is effective for LLM unlearning, but its cause is underexplored and it is less effective when applied to the middle and later layers.
The idea
Theoretical analysis shows that steering forget representations reduces token confidence, causing wrong or nonsense responses; the paper studies how the steering coefficient affects alignment with the random direction and proposes a variant that adaptively adjusts the coefficient from the norm of the forget representation.
Strengths
Reports that the unlearned models are robust against adversarial jailbreak attacks and that the adaptive variant improves unlearning performance over prior art for most layers with no additional computational cost.

Generative models (1 paper · CSRankings score 0.33)

Topic description

This paper studies how manual labeling mistakes in large datasets affect conditional diffusion models. It proposes a guidance technique that uses a discriminator trained with adversarial loss to assess sample authenticity and applies it only in the early phase of generation. The method adds little inference time, needs no retraining, and is reported to outperform prior methods across noise settings.

Guiding Noisy Label Conditional Diffusion Models with Score-based Discriminator Correction ICCV 2025 · accepted
The problem
Large datasets used to train conditional diffusion models contain manual labeling mistakes, and the extent to which such label noise compromises generative quality and controllability is not well studied.
The idea
A guidance technique for aligning noisy pre-trained conditional diffusion models: a discriminator trained with adversarial loss, drawing on noise-detection techniques, assesses the authenticity of each sample; the guidance is limited to the early phase of generation.
Strengths
Reports that limiting the guidance to the early phase of generation improves performance, that the method adds only marginal inference time and needs no retraining, and that it outperforms previous state-of-the-art methods across different noise settings.

Deep learning and representation learning (1 paper · CSRankings score 0.25)

Topic description

This paper studies recurrent deep-thinking models, which improve by running more inference iterations, for object recognition on out-of-distribution (OOD) data. Accuracy rises and then drops when too many iterations are used (overthinking). A self-supervised rotation-prediction proxy selects the stopping iteration without labels and is reported to reach accuracy close to the peak across several OOD benchmarks.

Papers (1)
Deep Thinking on Out-Of-Distribution Data: How can we know when a model is overthinking? ICLR 2026 · rejected
The problem
Recurrent deep-thinking models improve by running more inference iterations, but on out-of-distribution (OOD) object recognition too many iterations lowers accuracy (overthinking); the question is how to detect this without labels.
The idea
A self-supervised proxy task (rotation prediction) dynamically detects overthinking and approximates the iteration of peak accuracy without ground-truth labels, so the number of thinking iterations is chosen per input at test time.
Strengths
Reports performance gains and accuracy close to the peak across multiple OOD object-recognition benchmarks, avoiding overthinking-related drops; experiments use CIFAR-C, Tiny-ImageNet-C and STL10.
What the authors leave open
  • Overthinking remains an open challenge; future work will extend the framework to logical reasoning and large language models, where the tension between underthinking and overthinking may be sharper.
  • The authors acknowledge an inherent limitation of using rotation prediction as a universal self-supervised proxy.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The method could fails if main and auxiliary tasks decouple under certain OOD shifts (e.g., texture, style, or adversarial).
  • The proxy must be trained jointly, not plug-and-play on pretrained models, limiting applicability to frozen backbones (e.g. foundation models).
  • assumption A1, A2 (lines 208-211) appears a big limitation of method: how can one know that for a given task, dataset, and model, which ssl task will hold a direct correlation with the downstream accuracy.
