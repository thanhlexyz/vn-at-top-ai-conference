Research directions: Thanh Trung Huynh

Thanh Trung Huynh (VinUni) · All professors

Prof. Trung's papers study how to learn compact, adaptive representations when data are very large or partly missing. Work on condensing gigapixel pathology slides into prototypes that adapt to each slide, through concept decomposition and pruning or through distribution matching. Work on federated learning with missing modalities learns client embeddings recording which modalities and features are absent, used to adapt the shared representation.

Open challenges. Work on condensing gigapixel pathology slides leaves open the gap to whole-bag features, gains versus prototype budget, and reliance on public datasets with staining artifacts. Work on federated learning with missing modalities leaves open continuous or dependent missingness, communication overhead with thousands of clients, and guarantees beyond Lipschitz models.

Papers (2)
Nonparametric Distribution Matching for Self-Supervised Whole-Slide Image Condensation NeurIPS 2026 · accepted
The problem
Histological whole-slide images (WSIs) have extremely high resolution, often several gigabytes per slide; existing self-supervised condensation methods compress each slide into a few representative features (prototypes) but rely on heuristic prototype learning that does not explicitly preserve the feature distributions downstream tasks use.
The idea
NICER recasts WSI condensation as matching the distribution of patch features under a fixed pretrained encoder, measured with the squared 2-Wasserstein (optimal transport) distance, and approximates it with a nonparametric prior (a Poisson point process, per the reviews) that lets the number of prototypes adapt to each slide.
Strengths
The abstract reports an average accuracy improvement of 7.44% over prior methods on five histopathology datasets, improved efficiency-accuracy trade-offs, and a clinical evaluation by a board-certified pathologist.
What the authors leave open
  • The analysis uses the squared 2-Wasserstein distance as the matching distance and leaves other distances to future work.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The bag-of-features assumption treats patches as independent, discarding spatial context information—yet spatial structure carries significant biological meaning in histology
  • the PPP prior assumes that concepts are mutually independent, which may be overly simplistic for frequently co-occurring related morphological structures
  • the final implementation still relies on several approximation and design choices. As a result, it is not fully clear whether the theoretical formulation directly leads to the proposed algorithm, or mainly serves as a high-level motivation.
  • it is not fully clear whether the performance gain mainly comes from the proposed method design or from retaining more information through a larger storage/prototype budget.

Nonparametric Unsupervised Data Condensation for Gigapixel Histological Images ICLR 2026 · rejected
The problem
Whole-slide images are several gigabytes each and cannot be used directly in standard vision pipelines; prior methods condense each slide into a fixed number of prototypes, which the abstract says overlooks differences in slide complexity and diversity and loses critical information.
The idea
NICER first decomposes each slide into a deliberately redundant set of feature patterns to capture heterogeneity, then condenses them into concept prototypes and prunes unused concepts, so the number of prototypes adapts to slide complexity.
Strengths
The abstract reports that NICER outperforms prior methods on four histological datasets with up to 90% performance gains; reviewers list cancer subtyping and survival prediction on PANDA, NSCLC, BRCA and LUAD against unsupervised prototype baselines including PANTHER.
What the authors leave open
  • The formulation does not explicitly model tissue architecture and spatial priors, which the authors name as an important direction for future work, particularly in clinically critical settings.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The “nonparametric” property arises mainly from pruning unused prototypes, not from a Bayesian nonparametric process.
  • Table 1 shows that NICER features still underperform the vanilla method (whole bag).
  • there is a complete and inexplicable absence of any empirical data regarding efficiency—no training times, no inference speeds, no memory usage comparisons.
  • The evaluation relies exclusively on public TCGA and PANDA datasets, which are known to exhibit site-specific staining and preprocessing artifacts.

Papers (1)
Learning Reconfigurable Representations for Multimodal Federated Learning with Missing Data NeurIPS 2025 · accepted
The problem
In multimodal federated learning, each client may observe a different subset of modalities and also miss input features within a modality, which misaligns local feature representations and limits the effectiveness of model aggregation.
The idea
PEPSY learns client-side embedding controls that encode each client's data-missing pattern and uses them to reconfigure the globally aggregated representation to the local context; the server aggregates these controls across clients with similar missing patterns, using probabilistic federated prompt tuning (PFPT) according to the reviews.
Strengths
The paper reports up to 36.45% performance improvement under severe data incompleteness on federated multimodal benchmarks (PTBXL and Sleep-EDF, per the reviews), and a theoretical performance bound that it states matches the empirical observations.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The theoretical analysis relies on the assumption of μ-Lipschitz continuity of the model, failing to cover non-smooth data distributions.
  • The experiments generate binary missing matrices with fixed probabilities, failing to simulate continuous missing in time-series data or inter-modality dependencies (such as synchronous audio-visual missing).
  • The dynamic selection of client-side embedding controls and server-side PFPT probabilistic clustering may lead to a surge in communication overhead in large-scale federated scenarios (e.g., thousands of clients).
  • Although performance on PTBXL and Sleep-EDF is promising, both datasets are from biosignal/health domains.
