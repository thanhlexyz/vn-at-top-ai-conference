The paper trains a spectrally normalized proximal policy optimization (PPO) agent to output parameter updates from the current parameters, loss, accuracy and previous update, and proves its variance escapes the barren plateau bound. Papers (1)
Modeling Quantum Neural Network Gradient With Reinforcement Learning NeurIPS 2026 · accepted
The problem
Training quantum neural networks (QNNs), parameterized quantum circuits used as learnable models, on near-term hardware is hampered by barren plateaus, where the variance of gradients vanishes exponentially as circuits grow, and by a time and memory cost of order L times 2^n for differentiating through an n-qubit, L-layer circuit.
The idea
RLQ-Grad trains a classical policy, a spectrally normalized proximal policy optimization (PPO) agent, to propose QNN parameter updates directly from the current parameters, loss, accuracy and previous update, so the surrogate gradient is not obtained by differentiating through the quantum circuit; the paper proves that its variance is not constrained by the barren plateau concentration bound and that its cost scales with the number of trainable parameters rather than the Hilbert-space dimension.
Strengths
On a hardware-efficient ansatz across four supervised benchmarks with up to 20 qubits, the paper reports a near-flat gradient-variance curve where backpropagation, parameter-shift and adjoint differentiation decay by 1 to 2 orders of magnitude, under 2 MB of memory and per-iteration speedups of 2490x, 7876x and 673x over these methods at 20 qubits, accuracy gains of up to 10% on circuits of up to 12 qubits, and parity with dedicated barren plateau mitigation methods on CIFAR-10 at 14 to 20 qubits.
What the authors leave open
  • RLQ-Grad does not address loss concentration, underparameterization or gradient collapse in classical preprocessing layers.
  • Meta-training the agent across circuit scales, locality-aware rewards and extension to generative and variational tasks are left to future work.
  • Performance on noisy quantum hardware is still to be evaluated.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • Table 2 does not show those baselines collapsing as circuit size increases. In many cases, baseline accuracy improves with larger circuits.
  • It is unclear that PPO can stably learn useful high-dimensional continuous actions for larger QNNs with many more parameters.
  • The reported agents are trained separately for every combination of qubit count, circuit depth, and dataset.
