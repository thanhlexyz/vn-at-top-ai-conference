Research directions: Danh Le-Phuoc

Danh Le-Phuoc (VinUni) · All professors

Prof. Danh's listed work studies federated learning, where clients train a shared model without sharing their data. Work on forecasting missing clients' models lets a federated learning server estimate them from past weights, so training continues when devices drop out. Work on server-trained generators of synthetic data that reduce catastrophic forgetting in federated class-incremental learning.

Open challenges. Work on forecasting missing clients' models leaves open what happens when clients stay offline for many rounds, where forecast errors may compound. Work on generators for federated class-incremental learning leaves open client storage burden and the training cost of two generators.

Efficient and scalable systems (1 paper · CSRankings score 0.20)

Topic description

In cross-device federated learning, clients that miss a round bias the aggregated update. The paper keeps a server-side digital twin of each client's weight history and fills in missing updates with moving-average or weighted-smoothing forecasts, leaving client-side training unchanged. Across four participation patterns and four time-series datasets it reports tracking full participation more closely than FedAvg, with about 6.11-50.65% lower RMSE in representative settings.

Papers (1)
FedDTW: Federated Digital Twin Weighting for Mitigating Client Heterogeneity and Unreliable Connectivity ICLR 2026 · rejected
The problem
In cross-device federated learning, where many devices train a shared model and a server aggregates their updates, selected clients that fail to participate produce biased global updates and slower convergence.
The idea
FedDTW keeps a server-side digital twin of each client's model, and when a client is unavailable it forecasts that client's current parameters from its historical weight trajectory with moving-average or weighted-smoothing forecasting and uses the forecast in aggregation, without changing client-side training.
Strengths
Under four participation patterns (random client dropout, variable participation rates, network partitions, delayed updates) on four time-series datasets with CNN and recurrent backbones, the paper reports that FedDTW tracks the full-participation reference more closely than FedAvg, with about 6.11-50.65% lower RMSE in representative settings.
What the authors leave open
  • Future work will explore applying FedDTW to additional areas and integrating it into standard federated learning platforms.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The main comparison is only with FedAvg. It omits stronger baselines like FedVARP, FedAR, FedStale, or Mimic, which address similar problems.
  • The paper lacks theoretical analysis of convergence guarantees or bias correction under the introduced forecasting mechanisms.
  • The memory and computational overhead of this on the server is not discussed but seems non-trivial and would scale linearly (or worse, if forecasting is complex) with the total number of clients, N.
  • What happens if a client is unavailable for many (e.g., 10, 20, 50) consecutive rounds? The forecast error from such a simple model would likely compound, potentially leading to model divergence.

Papers (1)
Overcoming Catastrophic Forgetting in Federated Class-Incremental Learning via Federated Global Twin Generator ICLR 2025 · rejected
The problem
In federated class-incremental learning, algorithms such as FedAvg suffer catastrophic forgetting, and generative-model approaches show lower test accuracy on previous classes than on recent ones (better plasticity than stability).
The idea
The server trains a data generator and a feature generator on all seen classes without accessing client data and sends synthetic data to clients, which use feature-direction-controlling losses to retain old knowledge and learn new tasks.
Strengths
Reports improved accuracy and forgetting measures on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100 and tiny-ImageNet, and analyzes robustness on natural images, convergence to flat local minima and calibration.
What the authors leave open
  • Clients need the generative models, the previous task's global weights and the current global model, which adds storage burden between server and clients; the authors note the generators' memory does not grow with the number of classes.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The datasets used throughout the paper are relatively simple; I recommend conducting further experiments on high-resolution datasets such as ImageNet to strengthen the evaluation.
  • It seems that training two generators are quite costing, and it already needs more parameter during the training.
  • The framework contains too many hyperparameters, as each loss term needs one.
  • It is unclear whether the GAN trained after each task is independently trained or incrementally trained based on the previous task.
