Research directions: Hieu Pham

Hieu Pham (VinUni) · All professors

Prof. Hieu's papers study how machine learning models are trained, made robust and interpreted across several data types. Work on domain generalization by unified style mapping, closed-form continual learning under memory limits and noise-resilient time-series representations. Work on predicting gene expression from tissue images reweights supervision toward within-slide variation instead of slide-level averages. Work on explaining neural networks through neuron groups, grouping neurons by shared function into hierarchical circuits, including neurons that encode several unrelated features.

Open challenges. Work on noise-resilient time-series representations leaves open the high-frequency noise assumption, penalty sensitivity in continual learning and training on domains resembling unseen ones. Work on predicting gene expression from histology leaves open whether the error decomposition extends beyond squared-error objectives and holds on more than one fixed split. Work on explaining networks through neuron groups leaves open evaluation beyond CNNs, polysemantic behavior beyond top activations, and more distributed activation patterns.

Deep learning and representation learning (3 papers · CSRankings score 0.60)

Topic description

Analytic continual learning under a matched memory budget compares exact ridge regression on a narrow feature expansion with wide readouts that compress the Gram matrix, on three class-incremental benchmarks. Training and test data are mapped into a unified domain for domain generalization, with up to 19.82% higher accuracy than the next best approach when few seen domains are available. Noise-resilient time-series representations are learned from low-pass-filtered views with an Inception-style dilated-convolution encoder, with improvements on forecasting, classification and anomaly detection using fewer parameters.

Papers (3)
Width or Interaction Fidelity? A Matched-Budget Study of Analytic Continual Learning ICLR 2027 · rejected
The problem
Analytic continual learners fit a ridge classifier on frozen features in closed form, but the Gram matrix grows quadratically with the feature width, so a fixed memory budget forces a choice between more features and exact feature interactions.
The idea
Diagonal-Preserving Spectral Ridge (DPSR) keeps the feature-label statistic and the Gram diagonal exact and stores the remaining interactions in a low-rank factor, and is compared against exact ridge on a narrow expansion under a matched budget.
Strengths
On three class-incremental benchmarks, the abstract reports that packed storage of the exact baseline's Gram matrix reduces the settings where wide compression is significantly more accurate from 15 to 8 of 45, with exact ridge significantly more accurate in 3.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • Storing only the Gram matrix's upper triangle would increase the exact baseline's width from 3,307 to 4,647 on CIFAR-100 at 43 MiB (Appendix E.4).
  • The allocation benefit is sensitive to penalty selection. Under validation and refit, DPSR has lower mean accuracy than narrow exact ridge at all three CUB-200 budgets (Table 1).
  • The methodological novelty is limited. DPSR combines established spectral updates, diagonal correction and the Woodbury identity.
  • At 43 MiB on CIFAR-100, DPSR requires more total state (76.10 vs. 53.93 MiB) and fitting time (12.8 vs. 0.9 seconds per task) than narrow exact ridge (Tables 25 and 27).

ConstStyle: Robust Domain Generalization with Unified Style Transformation ICCV 2025 · accepted
The problem
Domain generalization, training models that keep working on unseen test domains, is hard when few training domains are available or when the gap to unseen domains is large.
The idea
ConstStyle maps all training samples onto a unified domain optimized for the seen domains and projects unseen-domain test samples onto the same domain before prediction, with a theoretical analysis.
Strengths
Reports outperforming existing methods across diverse scenarios, with up to 19.82% higher accuracy than the next best approach when only a few seen domains are available.

Enriching Time Series Representation: Integrating a Noise-Resilient Sampling Strategy with an Efficient Encoder Architecture ICLR 2024 · rejected
The problem
According to the reviews, self-supervised time-series representation learning has to handle noise in real-world series and needs encoders that are both lightweight and able to capture long-range dependencies.
The idea
Reviewers describe CoInception: a noise-resilient sampling strategy that builds positive views with a spectral low-pass filter (via the Discrete Wavelet Transform), an encoder combining Inception blocks with dilated convolutions, and a triplet loss, built on TS2Vec.
Strengths
Reviewers report improvements over TS2Vec and other baselines on forecasting, classification (UCR, UEA) and anomaly detection (Yahoo) with fewer parameters.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • One of the key assumptions that the authors make is that noise is usually in high frequency
  • How can useful high-frequency data be preserved and what trade-offs are made in addressing this issue?
  • The direct forecasting baselines compared in the paper are no longer the state of the art.
  • The diversity of forecasting datasets seems limited, focusing only on the electricity domain.

Papers (1)
Is H&E Image-to-Spatial Transcriptomics Simpler Than It Looks? ICLR 2027 · rejected
The problem
Predicting spatial gene expression from routine H&E histology images is a route to spatial molecular profiling, but the within-slide variation of expression is hard to recover even when aggregate scores are high.
The idea
The paper decomposes mean-squared error into slide-level and within-slide components and proposes Component-Guided Loss (CGL), which increases supervision of the within-slide component; CGL-Linear is a closed-form affine instantiation.
Strengths
The paper reports that CGL-Linear achieves overall state-of-the-art results across HEST-1k cohorts and gene-panel sizes and that the same supervision improves existing neural models.
What the authors leave open
  • The authors state that extending the component-level analysis beyond squared-error objectives is an important direction for future work.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • Even the ordinary Linear baseline without CGL outperforms HEXST, STFlow, and BLEEP on several reported metrics.
  • The evaluation uses one fixed train, validation, and test split, with only two or three validation or test slides in several cohorts.
  • Since SEPAL and HEXST already supervise expression deviations, CGL’s contribution appears mainly incremental, introducing direct slide-wise reweighting based on a standard error decomposition and weighted least-squares formulation.
  • Since Local R² can improve through changes in prediction amplitude, a validation-fitted rescaling baseline and within-slide correlations would help clarify whether CGL also predicts spatial patterns more accurately.

Papers (1)
NeurFlow: Interpreting Neural Networks through Neuron Groups and Functional Interactions ICLR 2025 · accepted
The problem
Interpreting neural networks one neuron at a time is hard when a neuron encodes several unrelated features (polysemantic neurons), and most work studies how single neurons relate to the final prediction.
The idea
NeurFlow identifies core neurons, clusters them into groups by shared function, and builds a hierarchical circuit of interactions between neuron groups across layers.
Strengths
Reports empirical tests of the fidelity of the identified neurons and applications to image debugging and automatic concept labeling.
What the authors leave open
  • The framework builds on top-k activations; frameworks that also handle more distributed activation patterns are named as a future direction.
  • Comparing the inner workings of different architectures is left for future work.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • However, the proposed method only evaluated on CNNs (ResNet and GoogLeNet).
  • There is a mismatch between the strong claims and the evidence provided.
  • the experiments only show that, on average (but not always), the identified critical neurons have a greater impact than randomly selected neurons.
  • Polysemantic behavior is not limited to the highest activations.
