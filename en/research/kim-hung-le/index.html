Topic description

One-class anomaly detection trains only on normal data and must flag points that deviate from it, without labels to tune hyperparameters. The method derives discriminant directions from a deterministic reference point, keeps directions from both ends of a bounded spectrum to balance compactness and structure preservation, and scores samples by nearest-neighbour distance. The paper reports the best average AUROC (89.65%) over 28 baselines on 47 ADBench tabular datasets and top rank on 10 NLP/CV embedding benchmarks.

Papers (1)
Tuning-Free One-Class Discriminant Learning for Tabular Anomaly Detection ICML 2026 · accepted
The problem
Anomaly detection on real-world tabular data faces diverse anomaly types, scarce labels and sensitivity to dataset-specific hyperparameters, and the paper states that different anomaly types favour opposing representations: a compact normal class exposes pointwise deviations, while preserved cluster and cross-feature structure exposes others.
The idea
DVM-AD is a closed-form one-class method from discriminant analysis: it builds a deterministic reference point from the training data, solves a generalized eigenvalue problem with a Moore-Penrose pseudo-inverse, keeps discriminant directions from both ends of the bounded spectrum (compressive and structure-preserving), and scores test samples by normalized nearest-neighbour distance in that space.
Strengths
Across 47 ADBench tabular datasets and 10 NLP/CV embedding benchmarks against 28 baselines, the paper reports the best average AUROC on tabular data (89.65%, average rank 2.98) and an average rank of 1.60 (AUROC 72.68%) on embedding tasks, with no dataset-specific tuning.
What the authors leave open
  • The training-time spectral computation is costly; the authors suggest randomized or iterative eigensolvers, low-rank approximations, feature screening or structured projections to reduce it.
  • Applying the linear projections to inherently nonlinear data needs more study, for example through nonlinear feature learning, kernelized or piecewise-linear variants, or guidance on when linear projections are sufficient.
  • Experiments with higher noise levels and other noise types are left to future work, and choosing the anomaly threshold remains application-dependent.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • A potential drawback of DVM-AD is that its training time may become large for high-dimensional data.
  • in real-world datasets, normal data may consist of multiple classes or sub-distributions. It would be helpful if the authors could discuss whether the proposed method can handle such more realistic settings.
  • The current explanation appears to assume that larger feature values are more likely to correspond to anomalies. However, in many real-world datasets, larger feature values do not necessarily indicate anomalous behavior.
  • The analysis of why the learned projections improve separability is difficult to understand.
