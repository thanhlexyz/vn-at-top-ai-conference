Topic description

Next-token prediction places no constraint on the path hidden states follow through a sequence. The paper adds a loss from input-to-state stability theory that penalizes violations of a contraction condition on how far hidden states drift from the line between two semantic anchor points. It proves that trajectories stay in a tube when the condition holds and reports gains in accuracy and data efficiency over standard fine-tuning and prior trajectory regularizers across six benchmarks and six model families.

Papers (1)
ControlJEPA: Principled Trajectory Regularization via Lyapunov Tube Loss NeurIPS 2026 · accepted
The problem
Next-token prediction trains a language model with a sum of per-token losses and puts no constraint on the path its hidden states follow through a sequence; prior trajectory regularizers mainly control the direction, not the magnitude, of deviations from that path.
The idea
ControlJEPA adds a Lyapunov Tube Loss that penalizes violations of a discrete-time contraction condition from input-to-state stability (ISS) theory, applied to a normalized measure of how far hidden states drift sideways from the line between two semantic anchor points; it needs no architectural change.
Strengths
The paper proves that trajectories stay in an explicit tube when the condition holds and bounds transversal energy and variance in the partial-ISS regime, and reports gains in accuracy and data efficiency over standard fine-tuning and prior trajectory regularizers across six benchmarks and six model families.
What the authors leave open
  • The guarantees apply only to trajectories observed during training; stability at inference time is shown only empirically, and a theoretical account of why the smoother profile persists at inference remains open.
  • Experiments are limited to models of up to 8B parameters, and the semantic anchors depend on segmentation provided by the dataset.
  • The authors list vision and multimodal extensions and Lyapunov-constrained decoding as future work.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • Major theoretical issues, which directly invalidate Proposition 4.1 and Theorem 4.2
  • the tube regularization computation strictly relies on predefined start and end points of semantic segments.
  • All Lyapunov stability guarantees are only valid for trajectories within the training distribution.
  • Figures 2-4 appear to include error bars, but the paper does not state whether they are standard deviations, standard errors, or confidence intervals, nor how many runs were used.
