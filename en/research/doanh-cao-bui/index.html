Research directions: Doanh Cao Bui

Doanh Cao Bui (UIT) · All professors

Prof. Doanh's paper studies how a small image classifier can learn from a larger one when training and test images come from different domains. Work on Vision Transformer-to-CNN distillation for unsupervised domain adaptation, using a graph-based classifier head and the teacher's pseudo labels.

Open challenges. Work on distillation for domain adaptation leaves open how to stop teacher pseudo-label errors spreading under severe domain shift, and the teacher's heavy training cost.

Computer vision and multimodal learning (1 paper · CSRankings score 0.25)

Topic description

Unsupervised domain adaptation trains on labelled source images and adapts to unlabelled target images from a different distribution. The paper pairs a Vision Transformer teacher with a classifier head that passes messages between samples of the same class, and uses the teacher's pseudo labels to guide the gradient updates of a CNN+MLP student. It reports gains over state-of-the-art methods on several domain adaptation benchmarks, with a cheap student at inference time.

Papers (1)
GraDA: Gradient-Guided Knowledge Distillation for Domain Adaptation ICLR 2026 · rejected
The problem
In knowledge distillation (KD) a small student network learns from a larger teacher; under domain shift, where test images come from a different distribution than the labelled training images, the paper identifies the teacher's capability and the distillation strategy as the two factors that limit the student.
The idea
GraDA builds a teacher from a Vision Transformer (ViT) feature extractor and a Category-level Aggregation (CA) classifier head that uses graph-convolution message passing to group samples of the same class across domains, and trains a CNN+MLP student whose gradient updates are guided by the teacher's pseudo labels on unlabelled target data.
Strengths
According to the reviewers' summaries, the paper reports experiments with baselines and ablations on multiple domain adaptation benchmarks in which the method outperforms state-of-the-art approaches while the deployed CNN student keeps inference cost low.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The method heavily relies on teacher-generated pseudo labels for both self-enhancement and student guidance, which could propagate errors if initial labels are inaccurate or if the confidence threshold (τ) is poorly tuned, especially in severe domain shifts.
  • it is not clearly demonstrated why an MLP head would fail in the particular setting of teacher-student knowledge distillation, domain adaptation, and architecture mismatch (e.g., ViT teacher → CNN student).
  • The proposed class-level aggregation techniques resemble prototype-based methods, which have been extensively explored in domain adaptation.
  • While the student is efficient, the ViT+CA teacher requires substantial resources during training, limiting scalability for very large datasets or low-resource environments.
