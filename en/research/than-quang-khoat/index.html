Research directions: Than Quang Khoat

Than Quang Khoat (HUST) · All professors

Generalization theory for deep networks and GANs, plus methods for continual learning, pruning and generative-model control. Work on non-vacuous test-error bounds for deep networks, generalization bounds with synthetic data for few-shot image classification and Lipschitz-based generalization of GANs. Work on theoretical impossibility results for fairness showing that subgroup disparity can arise from task structure, finite data and limited model capacity. Work on continual learning with pretrained models through classifier alignment, random-projection ensembles, prompt matching and evolving centroids for online incremental learning.

Open challenges. Work on error bounds with open data partitioning, non-vacuous bounds with open tightness and few-shot synthetic-data generalization with open limited real data. Work on fairness impossibility results leaves open how severe the effects are in real applications, since worst-case assumptions and missing experiments limit practical relevance. Work on continual learning with pretrained models leaves open how to size projections, how costs grow with tasks, and when earlier tasks can benefit.

Topic description

A paper on test-error guarantees for deep learning presents two bounds obtained by decomposing the data space into local areas and approximating local errors with training samples; one bound is computed exactly from the training set without altering the model and is reported as non-vacuous on 32 ImageNet-trained networks, the first at this scale. A paper on the generalization and stability of GANs shows that Lipschitz continuity of both the discriminator and the generator leads to generalization and stability, derives a generalization bound for a large class of existing models, and explains why data augmentation can ensure Lipschitz continuity on both players.

A Non-vacuous Test Error Guarantee for Deep Learning without Altering the Model ICLR 2026 · rejected
The problem
Prior theories do not give a meaningful bound on the test error of deep neural networks; the non-vacuous ones require stringent assumptions and extensive modification (compression, quantization) of the trained model, so they guarantee only the modified model.
The idea
The paper presents two bounds on the true error of a model, obtained by decomposing the data space into local areas, approximating local errors with training samples in a controlled way, and using them to approximate the true error; one bound is exactly computable from the training set alone, without altering the model.
Strengths
The bounds are verified on 32 modern neural networks trained with PyTorch on ImageNet; the exactly computable bound is found to be non-vacuous, which the paper states is the first non-vacuous bound at this scale.
What the authors leave open
  • The first bound does not diminish as the number of training samples grows while the partition size is held fixed.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The error bound still depends on the optimal partitioning of the data, it is not clear how tight the bounds are.
  • The experiments are limited to image classification/image models.
  • Despite the fact that the bound is non-vacuous, it did not provide more insight about why certain models generalize better or how to improve training as it is not depedent on generic properties of models.
  • The evaluation is limited to ImageNet only; and while this is indeed a very challenging dataset, it would be interesting to see how tight the bounds are for other datasets.

Non-vacuous Bounds for the test error of Deep Learning without any change to the trained models NeurIPS 2025 · rejected
The problem
Prior theories based on PAC-Bayes and mutual information give non-vacuous bounds for deep networks only under stringent assumptions and after modifying the trained model (for example compression or quantization), so they guarantee the modified versions only.
The idea
The paper proposes two bounds on the test error of a model that use only the training set and require no modification to the model.
Strengths
The bounds are verified on a large class of modern networks pretrained by PyTorch on ImageNet and are non-vacuous; the paper states these are the first non-vacuous bounds at this scale without modifying the pretrained models.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The numerical bounds remain loose, often two to three times the true test error, limiting immediate practical value.
  • Choosing the partition size K and confidence parameter α is done by grid search on the same data, so the advertised ninety-five percent confidence may not strictly hold after tuning.
  • The proposed bounds are not compared to any existing bounds in the empirical analysis.
  • The robustness variant works unevenly across architectures and offers little guidance on selecting a suitable data transformation.

The problem

Why are Biases Not Rare in AI Systems? Theoretical impossibility and Trade-offs ICLR 2027 · rejected
The problem
Biased outcomes across subgroups are common in learning systems; the paper asks whether they can be explained by theory alone, without assuming biased data or an imperfect algorithm.
The idea
The paper proves a set of impossibility results (the No-Free-Fairness theorems) that isolate three sources of disparity: irreducible cost on a subgroup, finite-sample learning, and the limited expressivity of the model class.
Strengths

An Optimal Transport-driven Approach for Cultivating Latent Space in Online Incremental Learning CVPR 2026 · accepted
The problem
In online incremental learning, data arrives with substantial distribution shift and earlier samples have limited replay value; prior methods represent each class with a single adaptive centroid or multiple fixed centroids, which struggle when class data streams are multimodal and need continual centroid updates.
The idea
An online mixture model learning framework grounded in optimal transport theory lets class centroids evolve incrementally with new data and gives centroid-based class similarity estimates at inference; a Dynamic Preservation strategy regulates the latent space to maintain class separability and reduce catastrophic forgetting.
Strengths
The abstract states that the method gives a more precise characterization of complex data streams, improves class similarity estimation for unseen samples, and that experiments on benchmark datasets confirm its superior effectiveness.

The problem
Semi-structured N:M sparsity (keeping N of every M consecutive weights) accelerates large language models, but existing learnable-mask approaches add substantial parameter and memory overhead, which limits scalability to large models and aggressive sparsity.
The idea
Reservoir of Importance learns sparsity masks through differentiable subset sampling: a compact-logit parameterization and sampling without replacement replace full categorical distributions over all feasible N:M patterns, reducing trainable parameters from combinatorial complexity to O(M).
Strengths
The paper reports 1.5 to 8.75 times fewer learnable parameters and lower memory cost than prior learnable-mask methods, with competitive performance, stability and scalability to more aggressive N:M patterns across the Qwen2.5 family (0.5B to 7B parameters).

Papers (1)
Kernelized Activation Steering NeurIPS 2026 · accepted
The problem
Activation steering controls attributes of generative models (such as sentiment, style or helpfulness) at inference time without training, but the standard Difference-in-Means approach applies one input-independent steering vector to all activations and ignores the local geometry of the activation space.
The idea
Kernelized Activation Steering lifts steering into a reproducing kernel Hilbert space, writes it as an optimization problem expressed purely through kernel evaluations against source and target reference sets, and applies gradient ascent to obtain an activation-dependent steering field; Difference-in-Means is recovered under a linear kernel.
Strengths
Across standard activation steering tasks, including jailbreaking LLMs and image style control, the paper reports that the method outperforms or is on par with existing methods, and that Difference-in-Means is a special case.
What the authors leave open
  • The intervention applies the same number of gradient ascent steps to every token, which wastes computation on tokens already near the target and may be insufficient for harder ones; a token-adaptive schedule is suggested.
  • A principled way to choose the kernel function for a given steering task remains open; the RBF kernel is shown only as a reliable default.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • Cost grows linearly with reference set size (n_A + n_B kernel evaluations per token), unlike DiM's O(1) cost; Nystrom approximations are deferred to future work.
  • Theorem 4.3 assumes λ > 0 for strong concavity, yet experiments set λ = 0 with single-step updates; practical guarantees are unclear.
  • Only Curveball is compared as a nonlinear alternative; emerging SAE-based steering methods are not evaluated.

Reinforcement learning, planning and agents (1 paper · CSRankings score 0.12)

Topic description

Learning controllable embedding methods map high-dimensional observations such as images to a low-dimensional latent space, estimate latent dynamics, and plan control there. Existing approaches decode back to observations to predict the next observation, which is a high-dimensional task and adds decoder parameters discarded during control. The paper shows theoretically that this prediction can be replaced with predictive coding, builds a decoder-free model with locally-linear latent dynamics, and reports better performance than state-of-the-art baselines on benchmark tasks.

Optimal control from high-dimensional observations with unknown dynamics is hard; existing Learning Controllable Embedding approaches embed observations in a low-dimensional latent space but decode back and explicitly predict the next observation, a challenging high-dimensional task that adds nuisance decoder parameters discarded during control.
