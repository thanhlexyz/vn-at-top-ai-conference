Research directions: Trung-Nghia Le

Trung-Nghia Le (HCMUS) · All professors

Diffusion models applied to controllable image generation, image editing and few-shot instance segmentation. Work on multi-object generation in dense occluded scenes, zero-shot real-image editing through attention control and personalized single- and multi-concept generation. Work on few-shot instance segmentation with diffusion mask models.

Open challenges. Work on multi-object generation with open real-world transfer, mask-guided editing with open mask dependence and personalized generation with open many-concept prompts. Work on few-shot instance segmentation with open non-COCO evidence.

Generative models (3 papers · CSRankings score 0.62)

Topic description

A paper on text-to-image generation of dense, occluded scenes adds layout-aware attention biases and a training loss scaled by each instance's amodal extent so that overlapping instances stay distinct, introduces a synthetic benchmark of densely overlapping scenes, and reports higher count accuracy and less instance merging without masks at inference. A paper on zero-shot real image editing with diffusion models keeps the edited object's identity and texture apart from the background through a preservation adaptation of self-attention, limits cross-attention to the object's pixels with a localized extraction module, tailors mask strategies to different editing tasks without fine-tuning Stable Diffusion, and introduces a new real-image editing benchmark. A paper on personalized image generation combines a single-concept part, with a Kronecker adapter, disentangled learning and attention regularization, and a multi-concept part that reuses the single-concept models to generate multi-concept images directly without layout boxes or semantic masks.

Papers (3)
Learning to Generate Multiple Objects from Dense and Occluded Layouts NeurIPS 2026 · accepted
The problem
Text-to-image diffusion models generate wrong object counts in dense scenes: overlapping instances merge into indistinguishable structures even though the image looks plausible.
The idea
Layout-aware attention biases push token interactions toward region-consistent grouping and suppress leakage between instances; a training loss scales each instance by the ratio of its amodal extent (including hidden parts) to its visible area; a synthetic benchmark of densely overlapping scenes with amodal supervision is introduced.
Strengths
Reports substantially higher count accuracy and less instance merging while preserving image quality, with no mask needed at inference time; zero-shot evaluation on T2I-CompBench is reported to show strong generalization.
What the authors leave open
  • In extremely packed scenes, latent-space resolution limits force feature overlap, so nearby objects are still merged even with balanced gradients.
  • Thin or articulated objects such as umbrellas lose structural detail during latent compression, which inflates per-category errors.
  • The benchmark is entirely synthetic and may encode non-canonical lighting and artifactual layouts; future work includes explicit shape priors, category-aware loss reweighting and real-world hybrid datasets.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • But this is on their own synthetic dataset where training and test data share the same FLUX generation distribution. Hence it is unclear whether the improvements transfer to real-world prompts and natural images.
  • tokens in overlapping regions are hard-assigned to the frontmost instance via the painter’s algorithm, meaning occluded objects have no dedicated tokens in the overlapping zones
  • The current work validates the method mainly on static, non-interactive object overlaps.
  • The method involves multiple steps, but the ablations for each component feel a little limited.

Rethinking Attentions in Zero-Shot Real Image Editing ICLR 2025 · rejected
The problem
Editing real images from text with diffusion models is hard when consistency and complex non-rigid objects are needed; existing methods often fail to preserve texture and identity, need extensive fine-tuning, or struggle to edit one region while keeping the background.
The idea
A zero-shot method that does not fine-tune Stable Diffusion: a preservation adaptation of self-attention keeps the edited object's identity and texture apart from the background, a localized extraction module limits cross-attention to the object's pixels, and mask strategies are tailored to different editing tasks.
Strengths
Shows edits for object replacement, removal, background alteration, adding items and changes of pose or view while preserving the structure and layout of the original image, and introduces a new benchmark for real-image editing.
What the authors leave open
  • A limitation is attributed to diffusion models being trained on image-caption datasets whose captions may lack detailed descriptions of text prompts for real-world images.
  • Failure cases occur when the mask is imprecise; the authors list addressing these problems as future work.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The dependency on high-quality masks can limit practicality in fully automated editing and in situations where generating precise masks is difficult.
  • The proposed mask-guidance strategies are rule-based and not flexible enough to cope with diverse editing needs.
  • Table 1 shows only marginal improvements in metrics that measure overall image quality and background.

ConceptFlow: Unified Framework for Personalized Image Generation ICLR 2025 · rejected
The problem
Personalized image generation: for one concept it is hard to balance identity preservation and prompt alignment in complex prompts; for several concepts, generating from a single prompt without layout boxes or masks causes identity loss and concept omission.
The idea
A framework with a single-concept part (a Kronecker adapter with weight and embedding decomposition, disentangled learning and a new attention regularization objective) and a multi-concept part that reuses the single-concept models to generate multi-concept images directly, without additional conditions.
Strengths
Covers single- and multi-concept personalization in one framework; the multi-concept part needs no layout boxes or semantic masks, and evaluation uses DINO (identity) and CLIP-T (editability) scores.
What the authors leave open
  • The framework cannot yet handle prompts with too many concepts.
  • The single-concept model fails to capture sophisticated concept details such as text and logos when training and generation resolutions differ.
  • The multi-concept model shows failures in generating multiple humans, and the underlying Stable Diffusion 1.5 struggles with complex prompts of more than two subjects.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • it remains uncertain if the proposed work can handle multiple personalized instances (> 2), particularly for contexts involving up to five subjects.
  • Only a limited number of comparison methods, especially in multi-concept generation, does not adequately support the authors' claim.
  • This is particularly evident in CLIP-T, which evaluates editability and does not support the authors' claims of balancing the trade-off between reconstruction and editability.

Computer vision and multimodal learning (1 paper · CSRankings score 0.17)

Topic description

A paper on few-shot instance segmentation replaces prototype-based point estimates with a diffusion probabilistic model of the binary mask conditioned on an object region and K-shot information, using classifier-free guided sampling for category information, and reports consistent gains over state-of-the-art methods on base and novel classes of COCO.

Papers (1)
MaskDiff: Modeling Mask Distribution with Diffusion Probabilistic Model for Few-Shot Instance Segmentation AAAI 2024 · accepted
The problem
Few-shot instance segmentation segments objects of novel categories from a few annotated examples; prototype-based point estimation (for example the mean of K shots) leads to unstable performance.
The idea
Models the conditional distribution of a binary mask given an object region and K-shot information with a diffusion probabilistic model, and uses classifier-free guided mask sampling to bring in category information.
Strengths
Reports consistently better results than state-of-the-art methods on both base and novel classes of COCO while being more stable than existing methods.
