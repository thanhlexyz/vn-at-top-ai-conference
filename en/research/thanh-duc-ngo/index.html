Research directions: Thanh Duc Ngo

Thanh Duc Ngo (UIT) · All professors

Prof. Thanh's papers work on computer vision, both proposing recognition methods and re-examining how vision systems are evaluated. Work on dictionary-guided scene text recognition, few-shot object detection with diffusion-generated images and conversational image retrieval headroom beyond accuracy plateaus. Work on detecting poisoned 3D Gaussian Splatting images tests whether high-frequency patterns still reveal attacks when clean scenes have fine natural texture.

Open challenges. Work on scene text recognition with open similar characters, few-shot detection with open optimal synthetic data and conversational image retrieval with open new-information sources. Work on detecting poisoned 3D Gaussian Splatting scenes leaves open why detectors fail, weak features or the signals themselves, and whether 28 static scenes generalize.

Computer vision and multimodal learning (3 papers · CSRankings score 0.51)

Topic description

A paper on scene text recognition uses a dictionary in training and inference to generate candidate words and select the one most compatible with the visual appearance of the text, improving text spotting frameworks such as ABCNet on TotalText, ICDAR13, ICDAR15 and VinText, a new Vietnamese dataset released with the code. A paper on few-shot object detection augments training with diffusion-generated objects through in-context object synthesis with bounding-box adjustment, prompt-embedding mixing to produce hard novel samples and a background proposal step, with an average 17.5% nAP50 gain over the baseline on PASCAL VOC. A paper on conversational image retrieval studies retrieval headroom through controlled comparisons on a generated dialogue benchmark and on VisDial, showing that cumulative retrieval success can rise while current-turn retrieval falls, that different representations of the same dialogue such as mean pooling change retrieval substantially, and that a paired human sketch improves all twelve dialogue-state-retrieval-space pairs by 9.8-22.7 points, so saturation should be judged relative to the dialogue states and observations tested rather than read from a flat retrieval curve.

Papers (3)
A Plateau Is Not a Ceiling: Evaluating Headroom in Conversational Image Retrieval ICLR 2027 · rejected
The problem
In conversational image retrieval a user describes a target image over several dialogue turns, and retrieval accuracy often flattens in late turns; the paper argues that such a plateau summarises the outcome of an interaction but does not show which opportunities for improvement have been exhausted.
The idea
The paper studies retrieval headroom through three controlled comparisons: continuations without additional target observations, alternative representations of a fixed dialogue such as mean pooling or rewriting, and additional target-grounded observations such as a paired human sketch.
Strengths
The paper reports that on FS-COCO-Dialog reordering a frozen top-100 shortlist raises cumulative H@10 (Hit@10) from 39.7 to 50.5 while current H@10 falls from 30.9 to 6.0, that mean pooling on VisDial raises current H@10 from 62.2 to 74.9 under ChatIR-FT and from 51.6 to 79.9 under BLIP-ITC, and that a paired human sketch improves all twelve dialogue-state–retrieval-space pairs by 9.8–22.7 points.
What the authors leave open
  • The evidence is limited to generated FS-COCO-Dialog answers of uncertain fidelity, human sketches from FS-COCO alone, and a fixed linear fusion rule.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • A substantial portion of the representation and sketch analysis relies on FS-COCO-Dialog, a generated benchmark whose answer fidelity is uncertain: the two automated judges agree on only 57.8% of sampled answers.
  • the paper does not provide a human evaluation of whether these rewrites preserve the dialogue's information, omit important details, or introduce unsupported attributes.
  • I think the authors should further analyze whether the performance improvements arise from new information or from representing the same information.
  • The authors evaluate conversations with up to 20 turns (Section 5.3). However, I wonder whether the same findings would hold for more longer conversations.

Multi-Perspective Data Augmentation for Few-shot Object Detection ICLR 2025 · accepted
The problem
Few-shot object detection (FSOD) must detect novel classes from a handful of labelled examples; recent methods add synthetic samples generated by diffusion models, and the paper argues that such data lacks typical and hard samples, especially in foreground–background relationships.
The idea
MPAD (Multi-Perspective Data Augmentation) combines in-context learning for object synthesis with bounding-box adjustment, a Harmonic Prompt Aggregation Scheduler (HPAS) that mixes prompt embeddings at each diffusion step to produce hard novel samples, and a Background Proposal method (BAP) that samples typical and hard backgrounds.
Strengths
The paper reports an average increase of 17.5% in nAP50 (novel-class average precision at IoU 0.5) over the baseline on PASCAL VOC, with experiments on multiple FSOD benchmarks including MS COCO.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • if ChatGPT and DM are being used, why not simply generate a substantial number of samples of the corresponding categories and conduct direct training on the generated data?
  • The present approach heavily depends on utilizing pre-trained models to select typical and challenging samples, potentially causing interference when assessing the efficacy of augmentation strategies.
  • The framework combines multiple advanced techniques, including diffusion models, harmonic prompt scheduling, and complex background sampling, which may make it challenging for practitioners to implement effectively in real-world scenarios.
  • Evaluating how much synthetic data is optimal or exploring the impact of different amounts of augmented data on performance could provide insights into the scalability and efficiency of the framework.

Dictionary-Guided Scene Text Recognition CVPR 2021 · accepted
The problem
Scene text recognition methods often use a lexicon only by casting the output to the dictionary word with the smallest edit distance, which the paper says has many limitations, in particular for text whose characters are visually ambiguous.
The idea
The method incorporates a dictionary in both training and inference: it uses the dictionary to generate a list of candidate words and selects the one most compatible with the visual appearance of the text.
Strengths
The paper reports that adding the dictionary improves state-of-the-art text spotting frameworks such as ABCNet on TotalText, ICDAR13, ICDAR15 and VinText, a new Vietnamese scene text dataset released with the code.
What the authors leave open
  • VinText, where Vietnamese accent marks make some characters visually equivocal, brings new challenges in discriminating a character from multiple similar ones.

Trustworthy AI (1 paper · CSRankings score 0.33)

Topic description

A poisoning attack perturbs training images so that 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstruction becomes costly, and recent defenses detect it through high-frequency image patterns. The paper builds a benchmark of naturally textured scenes with matched poisoned views and reports that the AUROC of an existing detector drops from 0.980 to 0.617, that high-frequency statistics reverse their clean and poisoned ordering, and that purification damages clean textured scenes. A detector built on frozen DINOv2 features improves detection.

Papers (1)
Rethinking High-Frequency Forensic Signals for Poison-Splat Detection ICLR 2027 · rejected
The problem
Poison-Splat perturbs training images so that the adaptive densification of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), a scene representation made of many 3D Gaussians, inflates reconstruction cost; recent defenses treat high-frequency image patterns as forensic evidence of poisoning, and the paper asks whether this evidence stays reliable when legitimate scenes contain fine texture.
The idea
The paper introduces TextureBench, a benchmark of ordinary scenes with naturally occurring fine-scale texture, paired with clean 3DGS reconstructions and matched poisoned views, and uses it to analyse detection, preservation of clean content during purification, and the interaction of both in composed defense pipelines, including a detector trained on frozen DINOv2 features.
Strengths
The paper reports that the AUROC of the RemedyGS detector drops from 0.980 on existing benchmarks to 0.617 on TextureBench, that several high-frequency statistics reverse their clean–poisoned ordering, and that false-positive detections propagate purification damage to clean textured scenes.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The paper does not clearly distinguish the limitations of simple high-frequency statistics from those of high-frequency forensic signals themselves.
  • It remains unclear whether the improvement stems from better distinguishing legitimate textures from attack artifacts or simply from stronger pretrained representations.
  • TextureBench contains only 28 carefully selected static textured scenes. Its representativeness remains uncertain, and the findings may not generalize to more complex real-world environments.
  • The experiments primarily focus on Poison-Splat, without systematically evaluating different perturbation strengths or other related attacks, which limits the generality of the conclusions.
