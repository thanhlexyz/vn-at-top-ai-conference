Research directions: Ngoc-Thanh Dinh

Ngoc-Thanh Dinh (VinUni) · All professors

Prof. Thanh's recent papers develop training objectives that align generative models with human judgments. Work on aligning text-to-image diffusion models with human preferences through an offline objective that weights preference pairs and keeps the model's denoising ability. Work on safety alignment as density ratio matching derives single-stage preference losses from a closed-form likelihood ratio of the optimal safe policy.

Open challenges. Work on preference alignment of diffusion models leaves open how much the ratio objective contributes and how image-level preferences relate to per-step learning. Work on safety alignment of language models leaves open results beyond one safety dataset, small backbones and binary safety labels.

Generative models (1 paper · CSRankings score 0.17)

Topic description

One paper addresses preference alignment of text-to-image diffusion models, where strongly misranked pairs give weak corrective signals and improving preferences can harm denoising. It defines a preference ratio with a reference correction for each noisy diffusion state and optimises it with a Bregman objective, adding learned pair weights and adaptive regularisation. Reviews summarise experiments on Stable Diffusion 1.5 and SDXL reporting better preference scores, visual quality and text-image alignment; the paper was withdrawn.

Open questions (3)

  • Both SD1.5 and SDXL are earlier UNet-based diffusion models. (raised by reviewers)
Papers (1)
RatioDiff: Aligning Diffusion Models via Preference Ratio Matching ICLR 2027 · rejected
The problem
Preference learning fine-tunes text-to-image diffusion models so that their images better match human judgments of image quality and prompt adherence. The paper names two difficulties: weak corrective signals on pairs the model strongly misranks, and balancing preference improvement against preserving the model's denoising behaviour.
The idea
RatioDiff is an offline method built on a preference ratio between denoising transitions, with a reference correction for the fact that the preferred and rejected images sit at different noisy states, optimised through a Bregman divergence objective that keeps pushing on misranked pairs. It adds learned pair weights guided by consistency with the reference model, and adaptive regularisation that limits drift from the reference model and preserves denoising of the preferred image.
Strengths
The paper reports experiments on multiple text-to-image backbones (SD1.5 and SDXL, according to the reviews) and preference benchmarks in which RatioDiff improves human-preference alignment while preserving visual quality and text–image consistency, with no added inference cost.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • Comparisons with methods such as SPO, InPO, and IAPO are missing, making it difficult to assess how competitive RatioDiff is beyond established baselines.
  • There is no ablation of the reference correction and no comparison with DPO using the same weighting and regularization.
  • The reported implementation differs materially from the proposed algorithm.
  • Both SD1.5 and SDXL are earlier UNet-based diffusion models. Results on these backbones provide limited evidence that the method remains effective for stronger DiT-based and flow-matching models.

Language models and NLP (1 paper · CSRankings score 0.17)

Topic description

Aligning language models to be both helpful and safe usually needs separate reward and cost models and multi-stage procedures. The paper shows that the likelihood ratio of the optimal safe policy has a closed-form decomposition, which turns safety alignment into density ratio matching, and minimising Bregman divergences gives a family of single-stage losses with no auxiliary models and one extra hyperparameter. It proves that these losses recover the optimal safe policy, that existing safety-aware methods are special cases, and reports an improved safety-helpfulness trade-off across backbones.

Papers (1)
BSO: Safety Alignment Is Density Ratio Matching NeurIPS 2026 · accepted
The problem
Aligning language models to be both helpful and safe usually requires separate reward and cost models, online reinforcement learning and primal-dual updates, while direct preference optimization (DPO) approaches add safety through multi-stage procedures or heuristic margin terms without a principled derivation.
The idea
The paper shows that the likelihood ratio of the optimal safe policy has a closed-form decomposition, which turns safety alignment into a density ratio matching problem. Minimising Bregman divergences between the data ratio and the model ratio gives Bregman Safety Optimization (BSO), a family of single-stage losses, one per convex generator, that need no auxiliary models and add one hyperparameter beyond standard preference optimization.
Strengths
The paper proves that the BSO losses recover the optimal safe policy, shows that existing safety-aware methods such as SafeDPO are special cases, and reports an improved safety–helpfulness trade-off across backbones and evaluation settings.
What the authors leave open
  • Future work could extend the framework, which uses binary safety labels, to richer safety annotations and broader alignment settings.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The experiments mainly use one safety preference dataset and two relatively small backbones, Qwen2.5-0.5B and Llama3.2-3B.
  • The paper does not sufficiently analyze over-refusal. Higher harmlessness may come from excessive refusal on benign but sensitive prompts rather than better safety alignment.
  • The method assumes binary safety labels. This is a strong simplification, as real safety judgments are often graded, multi-dimensional, and context-dependent.
  • The evaluation relies heavily on reward/cost models and LLM-as-judge metrics.
