Research directions: Tien Vu-Van

Tien Vu-Van (HCMUT) · All professors

Prof. Tien's recent work studies how to shrink convolutional neural networks for resource-limited hardware. Work on pruning convolutional filters by information content, ranking feature maps by conditional mutual information estimated with matrix-based Rényi entropy.

Open challenges. Work on information-based filter pruning was tested only on VGG16 with CIFAR-10; cost and accuracy on larger networks, other datasets and tasks are unknown.

Efficient and scalable systems (1 paper · CSRankings score 0.25)

Topic description

Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) are large for resource-limited hardware, so the paper prunes whole filters layer by layer, ranking feature maps by conditional mutual information with several cross-layer formulations and cutoff strategies, in forward and backward directions. On VGG16 with CIFAR-10 it reports more than a third fewer filters with a 0.32% drop in test accuracy.

Papers (1)
Pruning Deep Convolutional Neural Network Using Conditional Mutual Information ICLR 2025 · rejected
The problem
Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) achieve high performance in image classification, but their large model sizes make them hard to deploy on resource-limited hardware.
The idea
The paper proposes structured filter pruning that ranks the feature maps of each layer by Conditional Mutual Information (CMI), a measure of the information two variables share given a third, estimated with a matrix-based Rényi α-order entropy method; it uses several cross-layer CMI formulations and cutoff strategies and can prune in forward and backward directions in parallel.
Strengths
On VGG16 with CIFAR-10, the paper reports reducing the number of filters by more than a third with a 0.32% drop in test accuracy.
What the authors leave open
  • Extending the approach to other network architectures and to tasks beyond image classification is left for future work.
  • The last convolutional layer is left unpruned to preserve its connections to the following fully connected layer.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • This paper only conducts experiments on VGG-16 on CIFAR-10, hence the generalization capability of the proposed method seems to be limited.
  • The paper lacks the comparison with other state-of-the-art pruning methods to highlight its effectiveness.
  • The algorithm seems to be quite complex, as each layer requires numerous iterations to calculate CMI and the cutoff point. Additionally, it requires a large memory footprint to store the feature maps from previous layers for calculating cross-layer CMI.
  • Does the number of samples affect the stability of the measurement?
