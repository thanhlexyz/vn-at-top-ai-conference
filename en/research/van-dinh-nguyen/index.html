Research directions: Van-Dinh Nguyen

Van-Dinh Nguyen (VinUni) · All professors

Work on communication-efficient federated learning and lightweight WiFi-based sensing for resource-limited settings. Work on communication-efficient hierarchical federated learning that exchanges compact feature statistics between tiers instead of per-sample activations and gradients. Work on human pose estimation from WiFi signals with a lightweight convolutional network, as a lower-cost alternative to camera-based sensing.

Open challenges. Work on communication-efficient hierarchical federated learning leaves open richer feature distributions than diagonal Gaussians and topology-aware client assignment. Work on human pose estimation from WiFi signals leaves open robustness to noise, interference and multipath propagation in the signals.

Efficient and scalable systems (1 paper · CSRankings score 0.20)

Topic description

The paper combines split-model execution with federated learning across cloud, edge and client tiers. Tiers send class-wise first- and second-order feature statistics, which upper tiers use to synthesize features. Experiments on image classification and medical image segmentation report communication savings of up to two orders of magnitude.

Papers (1)
H-SFP: Hierarchical Federated Learning with Decoupled Split-Model Prototyping ECCV 2026 · accepted
The problem
Federated learning must cope with heterogeneous data, resource-limited clients and communication cost; split federated learning cuts client computation but transmits high-dimensional activations and gradients during training.
The idea
In a cloud-edge-client hierarchy, tiers exchange compact class-wise first- and second-order feature statistics instead of sample-level activations or gradients, and upper tiers synthesize feature distributions from them so model segments train independently.
Strengths
On CIFAR-10/100, HAM10000, ImageNet-1K and ISIC-2018, the paper reports robustness under heterogeneous data and communication reduced by up to two orders of magnitude versus federated and split-learning baselines.
What the authors leave open
  • Gaussian feature synthesis with a diagonal covariance may not represent multimodal feature distributions or cross-dimensional correlations; low-rank covariance approximations and richer distribution models are planned.
  • Evaluation uses a random client-to-edge assignment; topology-aware clustering and broader forms of system heterogeneity remain for future study.

Topic description

The paper estimates 3D human skeletons from raw WiFi signals using a multi-branch convolutional network with selective kernel attention. It reports state-of-the-art accuracy on two datasets with a small parameter count, and tests the effect of added noise and denoising filters.

Papers (1)
HPE-Li: WiFi-enabled Lightweight Dual Selective Kernel Convolution for Human Pose Estimation ECCV 2024 · accepted
The problem
WiFi-based human pose estimation is an alternative to vision-based methods but has high computational cost, and WiFi signals are susceptible to noise, interference and multipath propagation.
The idea
Uses camera and WiFi multi-modal sensors to generate 3D skeletons, and a multi-branch convolutional network on raw WiFi signals with selective kernel attention that adjusts kernel sizes to the input.
Strengths
On the MM-Fi and WiPose datasets the paper reports state-of-the-art accuracy with far fewer parameters; MPJPE rises with added noise but stays reliable when uniform or median filters are applied.
What the authors leave open
  • Accuracy (MPJPE) decreases as additive Gaussian or salt-and-pepper noise increases; denoising filters were added without changing the network, and a more robust model is left for future work.
