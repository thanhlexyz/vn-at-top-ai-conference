Research directions: Huynh Thi Thanh Binh

Huynh Thi Thanh Binh (HUST) · All professors

Most of Prof. Binh's papers use large language models or learned policies to automate design and decision tasks. Work on LLM-guided heuristic evolution for NP-hard combinatorial problems with diversity control, multi-turn reflection, multi-objective selection and two-agent turn-taking. Work on reinforcement learning under drifting preferences that infers them from history, and on multi-agent reinforcement learning coordinating LLM agents to write related-work sections. Work on simulated crowds for robot navigation and on synthesizing one million grasp samples with foundation models.

Open challenges. Work on multi-turn LLM heuristic refinement leaves open whether it helps beyond bin packing, which parts matter, its computation cost, and balancing exploration with exploitation. Work on constrained reinforcement learning under drift leaves open long-horizon budgets, simple evaluation environments, and coordination beyond a fixed action set and one benchmark. Work on synthetic grasp data from foundation models leaves diversity dependent on those models' knowledge of everyday objects, and simulated crowds on one dynamics model.

Optimization methods (5 papers · CSRankings score 1.04)

Topic description

LLM-based evolutionary program search lets an LLM write and modify heuristic code for NP-hard problems inside an evolutionary loop. Search diversity is measured and balanced with harmony search, two LLM agents take turns improving solver components, and a Pareto grid guides multi-objective problems; these papers report gains over earlier LLM-based baselines, and the multi-objective one is competitive with traditional multi-objective evolutionary algorithms at much shorter runtime. Heuristics for NP-hard combinatorial optimization are clustered by behaviour or performance profile, and an LLM critiques and refines each group over several turns with accumulated feedback. One paper reports reducing the optimality gap to nearly 9% where variants without multi-turn reflection plateau near 18%, and the other reports lower optimality gaps than one-shot LLM code generation and genetic programming on online bin packing.

Papers (5)
ReVEL: Multi-Turn Reflective LLM-Guided Heuristic Evolution via Structured Performance Feedback EMNLP 2026 · rejected
The problem
Designing heuristics for NP-hard combinatorial optimization problems needs domain expertise, and LLM-guided evolutionary methods mostly refine each heuristic on its own or with pairwise feedback.
The idea
ReVEL groups heuristics by behaviour, into similarity-driven groups for local refinement and diversity-driven groups for exploration, and has the LLM refine each group over several turns using accumulated performance feedback.
Strengths
The paper reports improvements over LLM-guided evolutionary baselines on standard benchmarks and several LLM backbones; in its cost analysis, variants without multi-turn reflection plateau at an optimality gap of about 18% while ReVEL reaches nearly 9%.

Cognitively Inspired Reflective Evolution: Interactive Multi-Turn LLM–EA Synthesis of Heuristics for Combinatorial Optimization ICLR 2026 · rejected
The problem
Reviewers describe the paper as targeting automated heuristic design for combinatorial optimization, where LLMs are typically used as one-shot code generators inside evolutionary algorithms.
The idea
According to the reviews, CIRE clusters candidate heuristics by performance profiles and CodeBLEU code similarity, forms similar and mixed groups for the LLM to critique and refine over several turns, and lets an evolutionary-algorithm meta-controller validate the proposals.
Strengths
Reviewers summarise experiments on online bin packing that report lower optimality gaps and excess-bin fractions than one-shot LLM code generation, genetic programming and evolutionary algorithms without LLM feedback.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The authors claim the proposed method is for “CO”, but actually only for the bin packing problem.
  • There is no ablation isolating the effects of clustering, entropy-based grouping, or reflective multi-turn reasoning on performance. The improvement could stem from increased prompt diversity rather than the proposed reflective mechanism.
  • The computational cost of multi-turn LLM reasoning is not reported.
  • The results rely solely on a single proprietary model (DeepSeek V3), with no comparison across different LLMs or open-source baselines, hindering reproducibility.

MOTIF: Multi-strategy Optimization via Turn-based Interactive Framework AAAI 2026 · accepted
The problem
LLM-based solver design for NP-hard combinatorial optimization problems usually searches over a single component, commonly a heuristic scoring function, rather than the set of interdependent components a solver uses.
The idea
MOTIF casts solver design as jointly optimizing several components and runs Monte Carlo Tree Search over turns in which two LLM agents alternately improve one component, each using the history of its own and its opponent's updates.
Strengths
The paper reports that MOTIF outperforms state-of-the-art methods across multiple combinatorial optimization domains.

Pareto-Grid-Guided Large Language Models for Fast and High-Quality Heuristics Design in Multi-Objective Combinatorial Optimization AAAI 2026 · accepted
The problem
Multi-objective combinatorial optimization problems require trading off conflicting objectives; evolutionary algorithms for them depend on domain knowledge and parameter tuning, and LLM-based heuristic generation has mostly targeted single-objective tasks, neglecting runtime and heuristic diversity.
The idea
MPaGE extends the Simple Evolutionary Multiobjective Optimization (SEMO) framework: it partitions the objective space into a Pareto front grid, keeps top candidates per cell, and asks the LLM to favour heuristics with semantically distinct logic during variation.
Strengths
The paper reports better performance than existing LLM-based frameworks and results competitive with traditional multi-objective evolutionary algorithms at much shorter runtime.

HSEvo: Elevating Automatic Heuristic Design with Diversity-Driven Harmony Search and Genetic Algorithm Using LLMs AAAI 2025 · accepted
The problem
LLM-based evolutionary program search generates heuristics automatically, but how diverse its search is and how it balances exploration and exploitation were not well understood.
The idea
The paper proposes two diversity metrics, uses them to analyse FunSearch, EoH and ReEvo, and introduces HSEvo, which combines harmony search with a genetic algorithm to balance diversity and convergence.
Strengths
The analysis reports that EoH is more diverse but has unstable objective scores while ReEvo scores well but does not optimise diversity; HSEvo is reported to reach high diversity and good objective scores at low cost.

Reinforcement learning, planning and agents (2 papers · CSRankings score 0.70)

Topic description

Constrained multi-objective reinforcement learning is studied when preferences and safety budgets drift or are noisy, delayed or incomplete: the latent context is inferred from recent interaction history, fused with observed signals through uncertainty-dependent gates, and used to condition both the policy and a constraint penalty, with performance loss decomposed into error terms and better feasible-front quality, online adaptation and constraint satisfaction than multi-objective baselines on several benchmarks. Fixed multi-agent pipelines for related work generation are replaced by LLM agents that choose among retrieval, dissemination, composition and critique actions under a policy trained with Independent Proximal Policy Optimization, with higher quality than existing baselines on a standard benchmark at lower token cost, and released code.

Open questions (4)
  • The method enforces a cost limit at each decision step rather than a cumulative or remaining budget, so long-horizon resource depletion and trade-offs with the budget remaining in the future are not directly modeled. (stated by the authors)
  • The empirical evaluation is mostly conducted on relatively simple or toy environments. (raised by reviewers)

  • The experiments reported in the abstract use one standard related-work-generation benchmark; other benchmarks or research fields are outside the reported evaluation. (raised by reviewers)
Papers (2)
BECON: Belief-Conditioned Constrained Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning under Drifting Preferences and Budgets NeurIPS 2026 · accepted
The problem
Constrained multi-objective reinforcement learning (MORL) trains one policy that trades off several rewards according to a given preference while keeping costs under a safety budget, and usually assumes these preferences and budgets are observed exactly; in deployment they may drift or be noisy, delayed or incomplete.
The idea
BECON infers the latent task context from recent interaction history, estimates the preference and budget from it, fuses these estimates with the observed signals through uncertainty-dependent gates, and uses the fused context to condition both the policy and a context-dependent constraint penalty (dual variable).
Strengths
The paper decomposes performance loss into preference-fusion, budget-fusion and cost-model errors, characterises the history-length trade-off under drift, and reports better feasible-front quality, online adaptation and constraint satisfaction than MORL baselines on several benchmarks.
What the authors leave open
  • BECON enforces a cost limit at each decision step rather than a cumulative or remaining budget, so it does not directly capture long-horizon resource depletion or trade-offs with the budget remaining in the future.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The theoretical section assumes that recent histories are informative enough to recover the latent preference and budget, but the experiments mainly report downstream FHV/FUT/SR/PCU numbers.
  • If most policies are comfortably below threshold, the safety dimension may not be testing the proposed context-dependent constraint mechanism.
  • The empirical evaluation is mostly conducted on relatively simple or toy environments.
  • there are other types of dynamics that could lead to uncertainty, as in open agent systems: agent openness, task openness, type openness, frame openness, etc.

Assembling the CREW: A Collaborative Multi-agent Reinforcement Learning Framework for Automated Related Work Generation EMNLP 2026 · accepted
The problem
Automatic related work generation (RWG) aims to draft the related-work section of a research paper. Prior multi-agent LLM methods follow a predefined workflow in which each agent handles one fixed step, which the paper argues limits the adaptive collaboration needed to synthesize scientific literature.
The idea
CREW lets LLM agents coordinate dynamically by choosing actions such as Retrieve, Disseminate, Compose and Critique, with a policy trained by Independent Proximal Policy Optimization (IPPO), a multi-agent reinforcement learning method in which each agent optimizes its own policy.
Strengths
On a standard RWG benchmark, the paper reports quality improvements over existing baselines together with lower token costs, and releases its code.

One paper presents a benchmark simulator for crowd-aware visual navigation that adds a crowd dynamics model to photorealistic environments built on an existing simulator; it reports state-of-the-art collision avoidance and higher computational efficiency for its human model. HabiCrowd: A High Performance Simulator for Crowd-Aware Visual Navigation IROS 2024 · accepted
The problem
Most 3D simulators for visual navigation leave out human movement, which separates simulation from real-world use, and those that include humans are computationally inefficient.
The idea
HabiCrowd adds a crowd dynamics model with diverse human settings to photorealistic environments built on the Habitat 2.0 simulator, with an agent that perceives the scene only through egocentric (first-person) inputs.
Strengths
The paper reports that its human dynamics model achieves state-of-the-art collision avoidance with higher computational efficiency than comparable models, and uses the simulator for studies of crowd-aware navigation and human-robot interaction.

The problem

Language models and NLP (1 paper · CSRankings score 0.25)

Topic description

Large language models can translate optimization problems written in natural language into solver code, but their reliability depends on retrieving the right modeling template. The paper stores templates as a directed acyclic graph and treats retrieval as a walk over it, learned by a deep Q-network from solver and LLM-judge feedback. It reports the largest gains on complex and mixed-category problems.

Papers (1)
COMPASS: Composable Policy-Amortized Structured Search for LLM-Based Optimization Modeling NeurIPS 2026 · accepted
The problem
Large language models (LLMs) can translate optimization problems written in natural language into solver code, but their reliability depends on retrieving the right modeling template, and existing structured retrieval uses fixed or LLM-driven traversal rules that do not improve from past successes and failures.
The idea
COMPASS stores the template library as a directed acyclic graph (DAG) and treats retrieval as a sequence of moves between nodes, learned by a deep Q-network from LLM judgments of template fit and solver execution feedback; problems and templates share an embedding space so new templates can be scored from their text.
Strengths
The paper reports gains over heuristic structured retrieval across several LLM backbones and optimization-modeling benchmarks, largest on complex and mixed-category problems.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The current evidence is insufficient to prove that the model has genuinely learned transferable modeling knowledge, rather than merely memorizing template preferences for specific benchmarks.
  • the action space is heavily constrained by the manually constructed DAG. Consequently, the true performance driver might be the template graph itself, rather than the RL-based policy.
  • The paper relies on both an LLM judge and LLM-generated new templates, which introduces circularity into the training signal.
  • errors could originate from code generation, data parsing, solver configurations, or template merging, and not necessarily from a specific retrieval node.

Video large language models often answer questions about event order, motion direction and object-state change incorrectly. The paper reports that motion information is partly decodable from object-level visual features but not used in the final prediction, and proposes a training-free method that reweights foreground tokens and injects inter-frame feature differences. Papers (1)
Training-Free Positive Spatiotemporal Enhancement for Mitigating Temporal Hallucinations in Video-LLMs ICLR 2027 · rejected
The problem
Video large language models (Video-LLMs) recognise objects and scenes reliably but misread event order, motion direction and object-state changes, producing temporal hallucinations; existing training-free fixes boost per-frame object evidence but not how the same object changes across frames.
The idea
Feature Enhance is a training-free method that modifies the visual tokens before the frozen language model: it up-weights foreground regions that persist across frames and injects foreground-gated differences between features of adjacent frames, without changing model parameters.
Strengths
Across LLaVA-OneVision, Qwen2.5-VL and LLaVA-Video, the paper reports higher average accuracy than the unmodified models, and a reviewer notes gains in all six backbone-benchmark combinations, up to +4.67 percentage points on VideoHallucer and +4.00 on EventHallusion.
What the authors leave open
  • The authors plan to explore motion-aligned correspondence, adaptive frame sampling and causal interventions to better characterise and improve how Video-LLMs use temporal information.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • In Table 2, the gains mainly come from non-temporal categories (e.g., SDH up to +8.50), while TPH gains only 1.71–3.87.
  • The diagnostic evidence does not sufficiently establish the proposed failure mechanism.
  • But the existing ablations do not isolate the contribution of the temporal residual.
  • the parameters of Feature Enhance are from testing 109 different configurations, and the evaluation results on TPH will be unreliable if Feature Enhance is tuned on evaluation data.
