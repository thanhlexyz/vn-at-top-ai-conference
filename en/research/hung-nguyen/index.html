Research directions: Hung Nguyen

Hung Nguyen (HCMUE) · All professors

Prof. Hung's paper regularizes polynomial models so that more complex terms are always penalized more. Work on ordered penalties for polynomial regression designs convex penalties under which higher-degree terms always cost more than lower-degree ones, even near zero.

Open challenges. Work on ordered penalties for polynomial regression leaves open when degree-ordered nonlinear penalties beat degree-weighted lasso, and whether they give simpler or more stable models.

Optimization methods (1 paper · CSRankings score 0.17)

Topic description

Standard degree-dependent power penalties penalize small high-degree coefficients less than small low-degree ones, which reverses the intended preference for simpler terms. The paper proposes two shape-corrected penalties that keep the degree ordering for all coefficient sizes, proves the resulting convex problem has a unique solution, and derives exact coordinate-wise updates. On 9 regression benchmarks, one of the penalties is reported to be competitive with non-parametric Gaussian process baselines.

Papers (1)
Convex Polynomial Regularization for Learning Representations with Degree-Ordered Power Growth ICLR 2027 · rejected
The problem
Polynomial degree gives a natural hierarchy of complexity, so a regularizer should penalize higher-degree terms more than lower-degree ones; linear degree weighting enforces this ordering, but standard degree-dependent power penalties invert it near zero, because a coefficient smaller than 1 raised to a higher power becomes smaller.
The idea
Two shape-corrected regularizers, a linear-power penalty (LiP) and a shifted-power penalty (ShiP), keep the degree ordering for all coefficient sizes while giving coercive, convex objectives with a unique global solution that can be minimized exactly one coordinate at a time.
Strengths
The paper proves uniqueness of the solution and derives exact coordinate-wise updates, and reports that on 9 regression benchmarks ShiP's predictive performance is competitive with non-parametric Gaussian process baselines.
What the authors leave open
  • Extending the univariate degree ordering to partial orders over multivariate monomials remains open.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The practical benefit over degree-weighted lasso is still unclear.
  • The reported coefficient counts do not clearly show whether the proposed methods produce simpler or more stable models than standard regularizers.
  • The abstract’s statement about strictly convex optimization landscapes is broader than Theorem 2’s guarantee of convexity and a unique minimizer.
  • The paper has not identified regimes where this order-preserving nonlinear growth for coefficients could beneficial for the predictive task
