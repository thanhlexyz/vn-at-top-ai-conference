Research directions: Long Dao Van

Long Dao Van (HMU) · All professors

Prof. Long's recent work builds public image data for computer-aided polyp detection in colonoscopy. Work on a public multi-center colonoscopy polyp dataset with annotated still images from five imaging modalities for polyp detection and segmentation.

Open challenges. Work on a multi-center polyp image dataset leaves open imbalance across modalities and centers, single-annotator labels, and the choice of benchmarked models.

Topic description

Polyps missed during colonoscopy can later develop into cancer, and the paper points to a lack of large, diverse, multi-center public datasets for automatic detection and segmentation. It releases 3,934 still polyp images with ground truth from three medical centers in Norway, Sweden and Vietnam, covering five imaging modalities. The paper reports segmentation and detection benchmarks per modality and per center, including federated learning settings in which models are trained across centers without pooling data.

PolypDB: A Curated Multi-Center Dataset for Development of AI Algorithms in Colonoscopy ICLR 2026 · rejected
The problem
Polyps missed during colonoscopy can later develop into cancer, and the paper links high miss rates to variation in endoscopist skill, bowel preparation and the anatomy of the large intestine. It identifies a lack of large, diverse, multi-center public datasets for building automatic polyp detection and segmentation methods.
The idea
PolypDB is a public dataset of 3,934 still polyp images with ground-truth annotations taken from real colonoscopy videos, covering five imaging modalities (Blue Light Imaging, BLI; Flexible Imaging Color Enhancement, FICE; Linked Color Imaging, LCI; Narrow Band Imaging, NBI; White Light Imaging, WLI) from three medical centers in Norway, Sweden and Vietnam.
Strengths
The paper releases the dataset with its train-test split and reports segmentation and detection benchmarks for each modality and each center, including federated learning settings in which models are trained across centers without pooling their data.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The dataset size (3,934 images) and severe imbalance across modalities significantly undermine the claim of “large-scale” and “multi-modal,” limiting its practical generalization potential.
  • Center-wise data imbalance (with one center contributing the vast majority of samples) introduces potential geographic and demographic bias, reducing representativeness.
  • Annotation were only performed by a single annotator (senior research associate), although multiple experts did a quality review it is still challenging since polyps might not have clear boundaries
  • The criteria for choosing the benchmarked segmentation and detection models are not well justified, the chosen methods do not present the current state of the art
