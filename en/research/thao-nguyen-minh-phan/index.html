Research directions: Thao Nguyen Minh Phan

Thao Nguyen Minh Phan (CTU) · All professors

Prof. Thao's listed paper combines a large language model with a patient graph for automated medical diagnosis. Work on diagnosis from doctor-patient dialogues has an LLM extract symptoms, links patients sharing symptoms in a graph, and predicts diseases from neighbours.

Open challenges. Work on patient graphs built from symptoms leaves open whether one shared symptom makes graphs too dense and whether unchecked LLM extraction adds noise.

AI for health and science (1 paper · CSRankings score 0.20)

Topic description

Automated diagnosis systems mostly predict a disease label without analysing symptoms in depth, and LLM-based methods struggle to use structured medical knowledge. The paper has an LLM extract explicit and implicit symptoms from patient-doctor conversations, builds a patient-centric knowledge graph whose nodes are patients linked by shared symptoms, and classifies each patient with a message passing neural network over two-hop neighbourhoods. The reviews summarise accuracy gains over reinforcement-learning, Transformer and graph-based baselines on public benchmarks, a synthetic set and an internal dataset.

Open questions (4)
  • The edge definition in the patient-centric KG, connecting nodes if they share even a single symptom (Equation 3), risks generating excessively dense graphs, especially given the prevalence of common symptoms like fever or cough in medical datasets. (raised by reviewers)
  • The symptom extraction process fully relies on GPT-4.1 without a clear evaluation of extraction accuracy or hallucination errors, which may introduce noise into the knowledge graph. (raised by reviewers)
  • If the global graph is built before data splitting, test nodes could access training information through shared neighbors. (raised by reviewers)
  • The authors plan dynamic graph construction for evolving patient interactions, temporal and longitudinal modelling of disease progression, and encoding of symptom severity beyond mean pooling. (stated by the authors)
Papers (1)
SPKGDiag: Learning Symptom-Linked Patient Knowledge Graphs via Multi-Hop Similarity Message Passing for Automatic Diagnosis ICLR 2026 · rejected
The problem
According to the abstract, automated diagnosis systems mostly focus on predicting the disease and neglect thorough symptom analysis, while LLM-based approaches struggle to integrate structured medical knowledge into coherent, patient-centric representations.
The idea
SPKGDiag uses an LLM (GPT-4.1, per the reviews) to extract explicit and implicit symptoms from patient-doctor conversations, embeds and averages them into patient vectors, links patients who share symptoms into a patient-centric knowledge graph, and classifies diseases with a Message Passing Neural Network (MPNN) over sampled two-hop neighbourhoods.
Strengths
The reviews summarise evaluations on public benchmarks (MZ-4, MZ-10, Dxy and a synthetic set) and an internal dataset, where the paper reports higher accuracy than reinforcement-learning, Transformer and graph-based baselines.
What the authors leave open
  • The authors plan to add dynamic graph construction for evolving patient interactions and to model temporal and longitudinal clinical data to capture disease progression.
  • Symptom severity is not encoded beyond mean pooling of symptom embeddings, which the authors list as future work.
  • The paper notes that relying solely on local feature similarity for classification can let highly similar yet semantically irrelevant neighbours influence the prediction.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The edge definition in the patient-centric KG, connecting nodes if they share even a single symptom (Equation 3), risks generating excessively dense graphs, especially given the prevalence of common symptoms like fever or cough in medical datasets.
  • The symptom extraction process fully relies on GPT-4.1 without a clear evaluation of extraction accuracy or hallucination errors, which may introduce noise into the knowledge graph.
  • Nearly all main results are reported in terms of accuracy only, with no per class F1, no macro F1, no recall, no sensitivity, no top k recall.
  • If the global graph is built before data splitting, test nodes could access training information through shared neighbors.
