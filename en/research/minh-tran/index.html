Research directions: Minh Tran

Minh Tran (FTU) · All professors

Prof. Minh's recent work builds evaluation data for medical language models in Vietnamese. Work on a Vietnamese medical question benchmark: 14,000 expert-annotated multiple-choice questions over 34 specialties and four difficulty levels.

Open challenges. Evaluating LLMs on Vietnamese medical questions is limited to multiple choice, leaving open-ended clinical reasoning, report writing and patient communication unmeasured.

AI for health and science (1 paper · CSRankings score 1.00)

Topic description

The paper states that many non-English-speaking communities lack the resources and standardized methods to build medical benchmarks for large language models (LLMs), and that available non-English medical data is fragmented and difficult to verify. The paper proposes a pipeline that collects verifiable sources such as medical exams and clinical records and has medical experts annotate them, and uses it to build a Vietnamese medical benchmark of 14,000 multiple-choice questions across 34 specialties, released as a public sample set, a full public set and a private leaderboard set, with the pipeline open-sourced.

The problem
Medical benchmarks are needed to evaluate language models for healthcare in non-English-speaking communities, but the paper notes that many communities lack the resources and standardized methods to build them, and non-English medical data is fragmented and difficult to verify.
The idea
The paper builds a data-construction pipeline that collects verifiable sources such as curated medical exams and clinical records and has the questions annotated by medical experts, and applies it to create VM14K, a Vietnamese benchmark of 14,000 multiple-choice questions across 34 medical specialties at four difficulty levels, from textbook biology to clinical case studies.
Strengths
The benchmark is released as a 4k-question public sample set, a 10k-question full public set and a 2k-question private set for leaderboard evaluation, each covering all subfields and difficulty levels, together with the open-source construction pipeline.
What the authors leave open
  • The published questions are multiple-choice only, which the authors say may not capture the complexity of clinical reasoning in real-world scenarios; open-ended questions and clinical case simulations are named as future work.
  • Of 100,000 collected entries, only 14,000 questions could be verified within three months; the rest, including clinical questions and medical records, is left to be used for evaluation and for synthesizing training data.
  • The evaluation covers only answering medical questions, not generating medical reports, doctor-patient communication or adherence to ethical and legal guidelines; the authors call for a more holistic evaluation framework.
