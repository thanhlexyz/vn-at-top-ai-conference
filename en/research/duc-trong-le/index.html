Research directions: Duc-Trong Le

Duc-Trong Le (VNU-UET) · All professors

Multimodal and graph-based deep learning for emotion recognition, image classification and continual learning. Work on multimodal learning under missing modalities and on multi-label image classification that models label dependencies with graph transformers. Work on parameter-efficient continual learning that combines Fourier-domain adapters with hierarchical memory to reduce forgetting without storing past exemplars. Work on multimodal emotion recognition in conversations with graph neural networks, combining utterance-level temporal dependencies and cross-modal interactions.

Open challenges. Work on balanced multimodal learning leaves broader multimodal tasks and large-scale pretrained backbones for future study. Work on exemplar-free continual learning still faces no guaranteed retention of past predictions, modest gains from some components, and sensitivity to many design choices. Work on conversational emotion recognition is limited by incomplete hyperparameter tuning of context windows and attention heads under limited computational resources.

Papers (2)
BALM: A Model-Agnostic Framework for Balanced Multimodal Learning under Imbalanced Missing Rates CVPR 2026 · accepted
The problem
In multimodal learning, information-rich modalities dominate optimization, and this worsens when each modality is missing with a different probability (imbalanced missing rates).
The idea
A model-agnostic plug-in with a Feature Calibration Module that recalibrates unimodal features using global context and a Gradient Rebalancing Module that modulates gradient magnitudes and directions across modalities.
Strengths
The paper reports consistent gains in robustness and performance across several multimodal emotion recognition benchmarks under diverse missing and imbalance settings, without changing the backbone architectures.
What the authors leave open
  • Future work will explore broader multimodal tasks and large-scale pretrained backbones.

Modular Graph Transformer Networks for Multi-Label Image Classification AAAI 2021 · accepted
The problem
Graph-based multi-label image classification models object dependencies, but graph representations can become indistinguishable because label relationships are complex.
The idea
A modular scheme that segregates the computational graph into sub-graphs based on modularity, with multiple backbones and graph transformers and convolutions propagating information over the sub-graphs.
Strengths
The paper reports improvements for multi-label image classification on the MS-COCO and Fashion550K datasets.

Deep learning and representation learning (1 paper · CSRankings score 0.25)

Topic description

The work addresses exemplar-free class-incremental learning, where a pre-trained vision model learns new classes without revisiting past samples. It adapts the model with Fourier-domain adapters parameterized by magnitude and phase, organized in working and long-term memory tiers with Fisher-information-weighted transfers. Reported experiments on standard benchmarks show competitive accuracy, and ablations attribute most of the interference reduction to Fourier-based dispersion.

Papers (1)
Disentangling Optimization Geometry via Hierarchical Polar Adapters for Class-Incremental Learning NeurIPS 2026 · accepted
The problem
In class-incremental learning (CIL) a model learns new classes task by task and tends to lose what it learned before (catastrophic forgetting), a problem the paper describes as particularly pronounced in strictly exemplar-free settings where past training samples cannot be revisited.
The idea
HiPo (Hierarchical Polar Adapters) applies a fixed orthonormal Fourier transform so that localized adapter updates spread across feature coordinates, uses complex block-diagonal adapters parameterized by magnitude and phase, keeps a fixed-depth memory stack with a plastic working tier and deeper long-term tiers, and transfers weights between tiers using importance scores from an adaptive Snapshot Fisher mechanism (a gradient-based estimate of parameter importance).
Strengths
With a frozen ViT-B/16 backbone on standard CIL benchmarks, the paper reports competitive results, accuracy that holds up as ImageNet-R task sequences become more fragmented, and competitive performance on ImageNet-A; its ablations attribute the main gain to Fourier-based dispersion, with smaller, setting-dependent gains from polar parameterization and hierarchical consolidation.
What the authors leave open
  • The theoretical results characterize update geometry and transfer algebra, not guaranteed retention of past-task predictions.
  • Alternative unitary bases and a direct comparison of feature drift with subspace baselines are left to future work.
  • Larger backbones and task distributions remain to be studied.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • Contribution of the proposed polar adapter is marginal compared with “Spatial-Cartesian” version
  • combining these pieces do not clearly justify why the proposed method is robust against forgetting, compared to other methods that more explicitly penalize conflicting gradients.
  • Since rFFT is applied along the ViT feature dimension, which has no physical frequency ordering, the connection to frequency-aware memory dynamics seems more metaphorical than mechanistic.
  • The method is complex and involves many implementation choices, including stack depth, block size, consolidation frequency, retention cascade, SGD-only optimization, and Cartesian transfer clamping.

Language models and NLP (1 paper · CSRankings score 0.20)

Topic description

The work addresses emotion recognition for each utterance in multimodal dialogues. A relational temporal graph network captures utterance-level temporal dependencies per modality, and a cross-modal module captures conversation-level interactions. It reports state-of-the-art results on IEMOCAP and CMU-MOSEI and finds past context more influential than future context.

Papers (1)
Conversation Understanding using Relational Temporal Graph Neural Networks with Auxiliary Cross-Modality Interaction EMNLP 2023 · accepted
The problem
Multimodal emotion recognition in conversation must combine conversation-level cross-modal context with utterance-level temporal dependencies, while existing methods often fuse modalities into one input and lose modality-specific representations.
The idea
A relational temporal graph neural network (RT-GCN) that models local temporal context per modality, combined with a pairwise cross-modal attention module (P-CM) that infers global cross-modal context over the dialogue.
Strengths
The paper reports state-of-the-art results on the IEMOCAP and CMU-MOSEI datasets for multimodal emotion recognition in conversation, and finds that past context influences performance more than future context.
What the authors leave open
  • Hyper-parameters such as the number of attention heads and the sizes of the past and future windows could not all be tuned because of time and computing limits, which may lead to local minima.
  • Possible remedies are automated hyper-parameter optimization and learning mechanisms such as attention over future and past utterances.
