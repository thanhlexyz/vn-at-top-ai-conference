Research directions: Vo Nguyen Le Duy

Vo Nguyen Le Duy (UIT) · All professors

Selective-inference hypothesis tests that attach valid p-values to regions flagged by deep anomaly-detection and attention models. Work on valid p-values for detected regions in image anomaly detection and attention maps, using selective inference to control false positive rates.

Open challenges. Work on selective-inference tests for detected image regions still relies on piecewise-linear networks, mean-based test statistics and high computational cost.

Probabilistic methods and causality (4 papers · CSRankings score 0.77)

Topic description

These papers apply selective inference, a framework for valid hypothesis tests after data-driven selection, to regions flagged by diffusion-model and variational autoencoder anomaly detectors and to attention maps of vision transformers. Each test returns a p-value intended to control the false positive rate, and experiments use synthetic data and medical or industrial images. The papers' own text and the reviews point to limits such as the assumption of piecewise-linear networks and tests based on mean pixel values.

Quantifying Statistical Significance in Diffusion-Based Anomaly Localization via Selective Inference ICLR 2026 · rejected
The problem
Anomaly localization with image generation models inherits the uncertainty and bias of the generative model, and regions chosen from the same data used for testing make standard p-values invalid.
The idea
A selective inference test that conditions on the region selected by a diffusion model; the reconstruction is piecewise-linear for a U-Net with ReLU activations, so the conditional distribution is a truncated Gaussian found by parametric programming.
Strengths
The paper reports that the test controls the false positive rate at the chosen level on synthetic data, brain tumor images (BraTS) and industrial images (MVTec), while naive and permutation tests do not.
What the authors leave open
  • Future work will focus on improving the computational efficiency of the test.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • This study assumes a U-Net with ReLU. It may not apply to architectures that use other activation functions or layers.
  • The computational burden of the parametric programming approach is drastically understated.
  • The evaluation is limited to only Type I error and power at the image level. It does not provide segmentation-level metrics, such as AUROC or F1-score, which are standard in anomaly detection (AD).
  • This test statistic (Eq. 7) is sensitive to changes in mean intensity but may lack statistical power for anomalies characterized by other features.

Statistical Test for Anomaly Detections using Variational Auto-Encoders by Selective Inference ICLR 2025 · rejected
The problem
Variational autoencoders are widely used for anomaly detection, but there is no rigorous way to assess the reliability of the detected anomalies, which limits use in high-stakes settings such as medical diagnostics.
The idea
A selective inference test (VAE-AD Test) that exploits the piecewise-linear structure of the VAE's anomaly detection process to assign valid p-values to the detected anomalies under a pre-specified level.
Strengths
The authors state a proof that the false detection rate can be controlled at a level such as 0.05, and report experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets that support the theoretical results.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • This reliance on a threshold could reduce the method's usability in practice, as users may not know the appropriate threshold value.
  • One dataset with a few thousand images is not enough to establish that the statistical test is reliable in practice.
  • As for experiments, the datasets used for verifying effectiveness of the proposed method are limited. Additionally, the comparison methods are also limited, old and not popular.
  • It is not clear that the VAE need to be trained from scratch or fine tuned from some pre-trained visual models.

Statistical Test on Diffusion Model-based Anomaly Detection by Selective Inference ICLR 2025 · rejected
The problem
There is no established framework for quantifying the reliability of images generated by diffusion models, which hinders their use in medical image diagnosis.
The idea
A selective inference test for anomalous regions detected with a diffusion model, conditioning on the event that the images are produced by the model and returning a p-value.
Strengths
The paper reports that numerical experiments on synthetic data and brain image datasets demonstrate the theoretical soundness and practical effectiveness of the test, with error rates controlled as in medical practice.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The statistical test relies solely on the mean value of the identified anomalous region, without accounting for structural differences.
  • While computational resources are mentioned, there is no discussion of computational time, which is critical for assessing the method’s feasibility in practical applications.
  • The experiments focus on brain images with tumors, limiting the generalizability of the findings.
  • Although the paper includes visualization examples for brain image anomaly detection, it lacks quantitative results.

Statistical Test for Attention Maps in Vision Transformers ICML 2024 · accepted
The problem
Attention in Vision Transformers is used as evidence for decisions in high-stakes tasks such as medical diagnostics, but attention may erroneously focus on irrelevant regions.
The idea
A statistical test for ViT attentions using selective inference, which quantifies the significance of attentions as p-values and so bounds the false positive detection probability.
Strengths
The paper reports validity and effectiveness of the test in numerical experiments and in applications to brain image diagnoses.
