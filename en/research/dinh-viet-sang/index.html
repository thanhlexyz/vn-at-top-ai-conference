Research directions: Dinh Viet Sang

Dinh Viet Sang (HUST) · All professors

Prof. Sang's papers propose training objectives and components for learning from text and images under limited supervision or capacity. Work on cross-lingual topic modelling and neural topic models, with LLMs, optimal transport and contrastive learning, and on few-shot continual relation extraction. Work on distilling vision-language embedding models into compact students, and on multi-label classification from a single known label per image. Work on balancing losses in neural topic models through multi-objective optimization of surrogate losses with more balanced magnitudes.

Open challenges. Work on LLM-refined cross-lingual topics leaves open reliance on dictionaries and costly LLM calls, and the overhead of relation extraction. Work on distilling vision-language embedding models leaves open the teacher-student capacity mismatch in tokenization and embedding size, and noise introduced when combining pseudo-labelling strategies. Work on multi-objective optimization for topic models leaves open handling loss terms of widely differing magnitudes in topic models, where direct gradient-based methods can underperform.

Language models and NLP (5 papers · CSRankings score 0.79)

Topic description

An LLM refines the words of each cross-lingual topic, with self-consistency measuring refinement stability, using fewer bilingual dictionaries and LLM calls. Cross-lingual topic modelling aligns document-topic and topic-word distributions with multilingual embeddings, improving coherence, diversity and alignment. Neural topic models improve through optimal transport on compact pretrained embeddings with hierarchical clustering and contrastive learning. Few-shot continual relation extraction uses orthogonal class prototypes and label-description contrastive learning, outperforming prior methods on two benchmarks. Short-text topic modelling uses optimal transport with global clusters, outperforming state-of-the-art techniques.

LLM-XTM: Enhancing Cross-Lingual Topic Models with Large Language Models ACL 2026 · accepted
The problem
Cross-lingual topic models try to find topics shared across languages, but the paper states that existing models depend on sparse bilingual resources and often produce incoherent or weakly aligned topics, while LLM-based refinements are costly, work per document and can hallucinate.
The idea
LLM-XTM uses an LLM to refine the words of each topic and measures the stability of the refinement with self-consistency uncertainty quantification, so it works with black-box LLMs that do not expose token probabilities.
Strengths
The paper reports higher topic coherence and cross-lingual alignment on multilingual corpora while using fewer bilingual dictionary resources and fewer LLM calls.

XTRA: Cross-Lingual Topic Modeling with Topic and Representation Alignments EMNLP 2025 · rejected
The problem
Cross-lingual topic modeling aims to find shared themes across languages. Previous methods improved topic diversity but, according to the authors, often struggle to ensure high topic coherence and consistent alignment across languages.
The idea
XTRA unifies Bag-of-Words modeling with multilingual embeddings through representation alignment (aligning document-topic distributions by contrastive learning in a shared space) and topic alignment (projecting topic-word distributions into the same space).
Strengths
The authors report that XTRA significantly outperforms strong baselines in topic coherence, diversity and alignment quality on multilingual corpora.

HiCOT: Improving Neural Topic Models via Optimal Transport and Contrastive Learning ACL 2025 · rejected
The problem
Neural topic models (NTMs) still show weak document-topic alignment, high inference cost when using large pretrained language models (PLMs), and limited modeling of hierarchical topic structure.
The idea
HiCOT uses Optimal Transport to refine document-topic relationships with compact PLM-based embeddings, and combines hierarchical clustering with contrastive learning to disentangle topic-word and topic-topic relationships.
Strengths
The authors report superior topic coherence, topic performance, representation quality and computational efficiency over existing NTMs on multiple benchmark datasets.
What the authors leave open
  • The interval for clustering updates must be carefully adjusted so the model learns stable representations first, while overly long intervals may slow convergence; an adaptive scheduling mechanism could help.
  • The choice of positive and negative samples for contrastive learning remains an open research direction.

Mitigating Non-Representative Prototypes and Representation Bias in Few-Shot Continual Relation Extraction ACL 2025 · accepted
The problem
Few-shot continual relation extraction (FCRE) learns new relation types from a handful of examples without forgetting old ones, and the paper states that with similar classes and few samples, class prototypes are unrepresentative and representations are biased.
The idea
Minion builds class prototypes on a General Orthogonal Frame, a structure motivated by Neural Collapse that keeps classes well separated without a preset number of classes, and uses label-description embeddings as global class representatives in fast-slow contrastive learning.
Strengths
The paper reports that Minion outperforms state-of-the-art methods on two FCRE benchmarks, with ablations for each component.
What the authors leave open
  • Minion adds computational overhead, and its label-description contrastive learning component is more expensive than the prototype component.

Topic Modeling for Short Texts via Optimal Transport-Based Clustering ACL 2025 · rejected
The problem
In short-text topic modeling, inferring topic proportions for individual documents is hard, and distinguishing document representations and topic embeddings remains an open problem.
The idea
EnCOT (Enhancing Global Clustering with Optimal Transport in Topic Modeling) introduces an abstract global clusters concept and uses Optimal Transport to align document representations in topic space with global clusters, and the global clusters with topics.
Strengths
The authors report that EnCOT outperforms state-of-the-art techniques in short-text topic modeling across commonly used metrics.

Computer vision and multimodal learning (2 papers · CSRankings score 0.37)

Topic description

A distillation method for large vision-language embedding models aligns the principal geometric components of teacher and student and maximizes mutual information for semantic consistency, outperforming existing distillation methods on all four meta-tasks of a multimodal embedding benchmark. For multi-label classification where each training image has a single known positive label, a loss robust to noisy pseudo-labels is combined with pseudo-labels from a vision-language model, giving state-of-the-art results on four benchmark datasets.

Open questions (2)
  • The capacity mismatch between teacher and student (tokenization, embedding dimensionality, multimodal representations) is mitigated rather than removed, and the evaluation is limited to a single embedding benchmark. (raised by reviewers)
  • Combining pseudo-labelling strategies can introduce additional noise; the loss is designed to mitigate this noise rather than to remove it. (raised by reviewers)
Papers (2)
PGA-KD: Principal Geometry Alignment and Semantic Consistency for VLM2Vec Distillation EMNLP 2026 · accepted
The problem
Distilling large vision-language embedding models (VLM2Vec) into compact architectures is hard because of capacity mismatches in tokenization, dimensionality and multimodal representations.
The idea
PGA-KD combines Principal Geometry Alignment, which aligns principal geometric components to stabilise training, with a Mutual Information Maximization objective enforcing semantic consistency across intra- and inter-modal pairs.
Strengths
On MMEB across all four meta-tasks, in-distribution and out-of-distribution, the abstract reports outperforming state-of-the-art distillation methods; source code is released. Accepted at EMNLP 2026.

More Reliable Pseudo-labels, Better Performance: A Generalized Approach to Single Positive Multi-label Learning ICCV 2025 · accepted
The problem
In single positive multi-label learning (SPML), each training image carries only one positive label and all others are unannotated; treating missing labels as unknown or negative causes false negatives, and combining pseudo-labelling strategies can add noise.
The idea
The AEVLP framework combines the Generalized Pseudo-Label Robust (GPR) loss, which learns from diverse pseudo-labels while limiting the effect of noise, with Dynamic Augmented Multi-focus Pseudo-labeling (DAMP), which produces the pseudo-labels using a vision-language model.
Strengths
Experiments on four benchmark datasets report state-of-the-art multi-label classification results.

Topic description

For neural topic models, whose several loss terms differ widely in magnitude and where naive gradient-based multi-objective optimization can underperform, optimizing surrogate losses with more balanced magnitudes, without hard parameter sharing, improves on baseline topic models and on direct multi-objective optimization across widely used benchmarks.

Papers (1)
Multi-Surrogate-Objective Optimization for Neural Topic Models EMNLP 2025 · rejected
The problem
Neural topic models combine several loss functions whose magnitudes differ, making hyperparameter tuning hard. Gradient-based multi-objective optimization (MOO) algorithms are usually applied to shared parameters in multi-task learning, and the authors report that naive MOO on neural topic models can underperform implementations without MOO.
The idea
The paper proposes a way to integrate MOO algorithms independent of hard-parameter-sharing architectures, using surrogate loss functions with more balanced magnitudes to optimize the multiple losses of neural topic models.
Strengths
The authors report that the approach significantly improves baseline topic model performance and outperforms direct MOO application on benchmark datasets.
