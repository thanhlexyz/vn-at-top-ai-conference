Research directions: Thuy T. Nguyen

Thuy T. Nguyen (RMIT) · All professors

Prof. Thuy's two NeurIPS 2026 papers each diagnose a failure in model training and propose a fix. Work on balancing modalities in multimodal training measures each modality's representation quality with linear probes and boosts the gradients of weaker modalities. Work on routing in mixtures of adapters addresses one expert absorbing all inputs when time series foundation models use instance normalization.

Open challenges. Work on balancing modalities in multimodal training leaves open whether boosting weaker modalities' gradients helps under Adam, which absorbs most of the scaling. Work on routing in mixtures of adapters leaves open other normalization schemes, learned expert pools, and gains over a very small linear model.

Papers (1)
Probe-Guided Gradient Balancing for Multimodal Learning NeurIPS 2026 · accepted
The problem
When multimodal networks are trained jointly, faster-learning modalities dominate the shared fusion objective and suppress the gradients reaching slower ones, so weaker modalities are underused; the paper states that directly boosting weak modalities often fails because monitoring signals derived from the joint loss are themselves dominated by the stronger modalities.
The idea
Probe-Guided Gradient Boosting (PGGB) attaches lightweight linear classifiers (probes) to stop-gradient features of each modality to estimate representation quality separately, and uses the gap between probe scores (the utilization gap) to scale up the weaker modalities' gradients with bounded, smoothed modulation; it leaves the loss and fusion architecture unchanged, so it can be combined with methods that throttle dominant modalities.
Strengths
Across eight benchmarks in four domains with 2 to 4 modalities, the paper reports that PGGB alone is best on all four low-imbalance benchmarks and that combining it with the throttling method OGM-GE gives +2.31 percentage points over the strongest baseline on CREMA-D; it also proves bounded scaling, self-attenuation and a descent bound for stochastic gradient descent (SGD) under smoothness and bounded-variance assumptions.
What the authors leave open
  • Gains concentrate on pipelines trained with SGD, because Adam's second-moment normalization absorbs most of the gradient scaling; optimizer-state-aware actuation is left to future work.
  • The propositions are safety properties that bound the intervention but do not guarantee closing the utilization gap, which is shown only empirically, and combining PGGB with throttling can regress when the dominance gap is small (worst case -4.56 percentage points).
  • PGGB does not distinguish undertrained modalities from intrinsically less informative ones and requires per-setting tuning; an automated criterion for when to combine it with throttling remains future work.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The main results table (Table 1) shows improvements of < 0.5 pp over the best baseline on 7 out of 8 datasets which is extremely minor.
  • The proposed method of scaling the gradients should not be expected to give significant gains for the Adam optimizer which is scale-invariant / unit-less.
  • The ablation table (Table 2) shows that OGM-GE alone recovers most of the gap between PGGB+OGM-GE and the baseline (2.17pp) and PGGB's contribution is minor (0.22pp) well within the per-seed standard deviation.
  • Since the strongest claimed benefit is on high-imbalance data, the authors should explain whether the bounded scaling and descent bound provide meaningful stability guarantees there

Deep learning and representation learning (1 paper · CSRankings score 0.25)

Topic description

On instance-normalized time series foundation models, a mixture-of-experts head collapses onto one expert because normalization removes the per-window mean and standard deviation that the router needs. The paper identifies this cause with a mutual-information analysis and causal controls, then routes on the raw, pre-normalization input. With a strictly frozen backbone it reports wins in 54 of 54 comparisons against the strongest fixed adapter, across six backbones and an imputation task.

Papers (1)
Raw-Routed Mixture of Adapters: A Causal Intervention for Routing Collapse in Time Series Foundation Models NeurIPS 2026 · accepted
The problem
Time series foundation models (TSFMs) usually adapt to new data through a single trainable head on a frozen backbone; replacing that head with a mixture of experts (MoE) fails on instance-normalized backbones, the dominant TSFM design, where routing entropy collapses to zero and one expert absorbs every input, a failure the paper calls normalization-induced routing collapse.
The idea
The paper locates the cause in the router's input: instance normalization before the encoder removes each window's mean and standard deviation, which a router needs to tell regimes apart. A mutual-information decomposition yields a signal ratio computed before training, eight causal controls are reported to isolate normalization as the cause, and Raw-Routed Mixture of Adapters (RR-MoA) routes on the raw, pre-normalization input while the experts use the frozen backbone's features.
Strengths
The paper reports that with a strictly frozen backbone RR-MoA wins 54 of 54 comparisons against the strongest fixed adapter, outperforms LoRA, TRACE, AdaMix and full fine-tuning, holds across six backbones and an imputation task, and that the signal ratio predicts dataset vulnerability with Spearman correlation -0.88.
What the authors leave open
  • The analysis is specific to normalizers that strip the per-window mean and standard deviation.
  • Learned expert pools remain an open question.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • Table 4, Appendix M, and Appendix P.3 show that the whole RR-MoA family is beaten in absolute MSE by a 49K-parameter DLinear (e.g., 0.680 vs 0.416 on ETTh1)
  • The sufficient-statistic router using only [μ,σ] degrades MSE by +48% (Appendix F), showing (μ,σ) is not sufficient
  • The strongest results require a strictly frozen backbone. … this limits applicability when backbone adaptation is genuinely needed.
  • It is therefore unclear whether similar routing-collapse phenomena arise under other normalization schemes, such as BatchNorm, LayerNorm, or RMSNorm.
