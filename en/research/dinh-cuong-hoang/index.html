Research directions: Dinh-Cuong Hoang

Dinh-Cuong Hoang (FPTU) · All professors

Perception and learning methods that use scene context, for editing images from text and for generating robot grasps in clutter. Work on grasp generation in clutter predicts collision-free 6-degree-of-freedom grasps from scene point clouds, using voting and contextual information about neighbouring objects. Work on text-conditioned image editing aligns each edited region with the whole-image context and with text at both region and scene level.

Open challenges. Work on grasp generation in clutter leaves open checking whether generated grasps are reachable by the robot arm. Work on context-aware image editing leaves open richer textual descriptions, because the vision-language encoder used accepts only a limited number of tokens.

Robotics and autonomous systems (1 paper · CSRankings score 0.33)

Topic description

Autonomous grasping methods that rely on a database of known objects cannot handle novel objects, and learning-based methods still struggle in cluttered scenes with partial occlusion. The paper presents an end-to-end network that takes a 3D scene point cloud and generates 6-DOF (six degrees of freedom) collision-free grasps, casting votes to accumulate evidence for feasible grasp configurations and encoding dependencies between objects as context. It reports favorable grasp prediction in cluttered environments compared with state-of-the-art methods.

Papers (1)
Context-Aware Grasp Generation in Cluttered Scenes ICRA 2022 · accepted
The problem
Conventional autonomous grasping relies on a pre-computed database of known objects, which fails for novel objects, and learning-based grasp generation remains difficult in cluttered environments with partial occlusion.
The idea
An end-to-end deep network that generates 6-DOF (six degrees of freedom) collision-free grasps from a 3D scene point cloud, producing candidates by casting votes and accumulating evidence for feasible grasp configurations, and encoding the dependency of objects in the scene as contextual features.
Strengths
Experimental results report favorable grasp prediction in cluttered environments compared with current state-of-the-art methods, with contextual information increasing the likelihood that generated grasps are collision-free.
What the authors leave open
  • Future work is to add a reachability predictor to the grasping network.

Computer vision and multimodal learning (1 paper · CSRankings score 0.25)

Topic description

Text-conditioned image editors localize and edit regions from text, but most treat each region in isolation, which can give inconsistent edits and unnatural transitions. The paper proposes a training framework with a gated module that fuses region and context features, and two levels of vision-language supervision: region embeddings contrasted with fine-grained text, and the whole image aligned with scene-level descriptions generated by a vision-language model. It reports improved edit accuracy, semantic coherence and visual fidelity across several pretrained diffusion-based editors.

Papers (1)
Region in Context: Text-Conditioned Image Editing with Human-Like Semantic Reasoning ICLR 2026 · desk-rejected
The problem
Text-conditioned image editing: most approaches treat edited regions in isolation using local cues, which can give inconsistent edits, unnatural transitions or loss of coherence across the image.
The idea
A region-aware training framework with a gated region-context fusion module and dual-level vision-language supervision: region embeddings are trained with a contrastive loss against fine-grained text descriptions, and the whole image is aligned with detailed scene-level descriptions generated by a vision-language model.
Strengths
The authors report improved edit accuracy, semantic coherence and visual fidelity across multiple pretrained diffusion-based editors.
What the authors leave open
  • CLIP, used for joint image-text embeddings, accepts at most 77 input tokens, which constrains the level of detail that can be captured in textual descriptions.
