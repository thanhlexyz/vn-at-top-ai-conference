Research directions: Tuan Do

Tuan Do (Phenikaa) · All professors

Prof. Tuan's papers are mainly theoretical, proving what can be learned or identified from data and how many samples it takes. Work on sample complexity of causal discovery proves instance-dependent bounds, alongside theory for world-model latent dimensions and activation steering vectors. Work on transparent versus opaque policies in strategic games determines when committing to an observable strategy pays off and how to certify it from data. Work on neural operators for Hamiltonian PDEs builds layers that preserve the symplectic structure of energy-conserving dynamics so long-horizon rollouts stay stable.

Open challenges. Work on sample complexity of causal discovery leaves open how to use bounds that depend on the graph, which is unknown before learning. Work on transparent versus opaque policies leaves open extensions beyond two symmetric players, richer policy transparency, and empirical validation. Work on neural operators for Hamiltonian PDEs leaves open dissipative systems such as heat diffusion, and guarantees of energy conservation beyond linear Hamiltonian systems.

Learning theory and cognitive science (4 papers · CSRankings score 0.56)

Topic description

One paper defines a quantity based on the minimum partial correlation that must be detected to recover a causal graph's Markov equivalence class in linear Gaussian models, and proves lower and upper sample-complexity bounds in terms of it that match up to logarithmic factors. A second paper develops a spectral theory of linear world models, covering the bottleneck for multi-horizon prediction, a generalization bound for stable diagonal state-space models and recovery of factor-aligned latent coordinates. A third paper models activation-steering contrast pairs as revealing a target vector only through a quotient that removes nuisance features, and proves identifiability and sample-complexity bounds for recovering it.

Papers (4)
Spectral Identifiability for World Models: Polynomial Projectors, Resolvent Stability, and a Krylov Bottleneck NeurIPS 2026 · accepted
The problem
World models, which predict future states for model-based reinforcement learning and planning, are poorly understood: it is unclear how many latent dimensions they need, how their architectural spectra affect generalization, and how prediction quality relates to downstream behavior.
The idea
The paper develops a spectral theory of linear world models: the finite-horizon block-Krylov operator is the exact bottleneck for shared multi-horizon prediction, a Rademacher bound for stable diagonal state-space models (SSMs) depends on spectral memory energy rather than parameter count, and disjoint factor spectra make jointly trained diagonal latent dynamics recover factor-aligned coordinates, with a Riesz-projector stability extension.
Strengths
Generated-system experiments reproduce the predicted spectral quantities under the stated assumptions, and single-seed DMControl tables record a ranking inversion: on Walker-Walk, SSM-Mamba has better visual MSE and SSIM (0.0038/0.8238) while RSSM-GRU obtains the higher return (41.950 versus 27.916).
What the authors leave open
  • The guarantees concern linear settings with theorem-specific restrictions; for nonlinear world models they only suggest quantities to check, not a way to choose an architecture.
  • The prediction–control ranking inversion is a single-seed observation whose mechanism is not established, because the quantities needed for the downstream bounds were not estimated for the reported checkpoints.
  • Future work should estimate the spectral and downstream quantities on learned models, relate training to the recovery defects, and extend the analysis to noisy optimization and input-dependent dynamics.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • All results are confined to the linear regime, and the assumptions, including exogenous actions, fixed diagonal spectra, square encoder/decoder maps, contractivity, are restrictive enough to limit practical reach.
  • the DMControl tables (Appendix C) use RSSM-GRU, Transformer-STORM, and SSM-Mamba - none of which is covered by the theory
  • the contribution is a set of exact statements in an idealized regime whose transfer to the world models in the title is asserted rather than demonstrated.

Steering from Random Quotients: A Sample-Complexity Theory of Polysemantic Contrast Pairs EMNLP 2026 · accepted
The problem
Activation steering often builds a steering vector by averaging activation differences between positive and negative prompts (difference-in-means, DiM), but a contrast pair can co-activate the intended concept together with nuisance features such as topic, style, length, refusal or truthfulness.
The idea
The paper models each contrast pair as revealing the target vector only through a quotient that removes its active nuisance span; the resulting polysemantic information operator determines which target components are identifiable and how many contrast pairs are needed to recover them.
Strengths
The paper proves identifiability, sample-complexity bounds, a coactivation phase diagram and sparse-dictionary recovery guarantees, and a controlled TruthfulQA counter-confounder shows that raw behavioral margin can reward a style-entangled DiM vector even when quotienting removes the injected style component.
What the authors leave open
  • The results are best read as a conditional theory of recovery under credible nuisance supports, with broader semantic and model-level validation left for future work.
  • The public-data and Pythia-410M pilots evaluate recovery of the quotient estimand induced by the chosen nuisance supports, not an externally validated semantic target.

The Fisher Dimension: Instance-Dependent Complexity for Causal Discovery ICML 2026 · accepted
The problem
Classical sample-complexity bounds for causal structure learning are minimax and describe only worst-case difficulty, without separating easy from hard instances of recovering the Markov equivalence class (MEC), the set of causal graphs that imply the same conditional independencies, in linear Gaussian structural equation models (SEMs).
The idea
The paper defines the Fisher dimension as the inverse squared minimum partial correlation that must be detected to recover the MEC, and bounds the sample complexity by it from below for any algorithm (via Le Cam's method) and from above for the PC algorithm.
Strengths
The bounds match up to logarithmic factors, the Fisher dimension is proved to be uniformly bounded regardless of graph structure in spectrally well-conditioned models, and the experiments report that the predictor tracks observed sample complexity for structured graph families such as chains and trees.
What the authors leave open
  • A log d gap between the upper and lower bounds, coming from a union bound over the conditional-independence tests, remains an open problem.
  • Confidence sequences for sequential stopping are left for future work and are not a claim of the present analysis.
  • Extending the curvature characterization, proved for chain graphs, to general graphs remains open.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • In practice, if a researcher needs to know the complexity of discovering a graph, they typically do not have the graph yet.
  • The empirical results show that Markov blanket size is a better predictor of empirical sample complexity than Fisher dimension for Erdos-Renyi graphs.
  • The experimental scale is limited to d ≤ 20, which is quite small.
  • No experiments are reported for other causal discovery methods for example based on score such as GES.

An Information-Theoretic Foundation for the Subregular Hierarchy ACL 2026 · accepted
The problem
The Subregular Hypothesis holds that the phonological (sound) patterns of natural languages occupy a restricted region of the formal language hierarchy, but the cognitive basis of this restriction remains unclear.
The idea
The paper shows that Strictly Local (SL) languages, formalized as shifts of finite type, are exactly those admitting stationary Markov sources, which have zero conditional mutual information (MI) between distant positions given the intervening symbols, and proves that non-subregular patterns such as first-last assimilation admit no such Markov realization.
Strengths
On English phonotactics versus Finnish, Turkish and Hungarian vowel harmony, the paper reports that MI profiles statistically distinguish SL-like from tier-based strictly local (TSL)-like patterns (p < 0.001, r = 0.84).
What the authors leave open
  • Extending the characterization to the full SL class with boundary markers remains open.
  • The TSL results assume the tier is known; automatically identifying the tier from MI profiles is an open problem the paper does not address.

Reinforcement learning, planning and agents (1 paper · CSRankings score 0.10)

Topic description

The paper studies two-stage games in which each player first chooses whether to make its policy transparent, meaning a commitment to an observable strategy, or opaque, and then plays a normal-form game. It proves that for two players the transparency incentives reduce to a pair of quantities per player whose signs characterize every equilibrium, and gives an adaptive sampling algorithm that certifies these signs with instance-dependent complexity and matching lower bounds up to logarithmic factors. The analysis also recovers known cases, such as transparency being dominant under Cournot competition.

Papers (1)
Learning in Policy Transparency Games: Wedge Structure and Adaptive Certification NeurIPS 2026 · accepted
The problem
Learning agents in strategic settings can deploy transparent policies (auditable, committing to an observable strategy) or opaque ones (flexible, reacting to others), and it is unclear when each choice pays off and how an agent could learn this from interaction data.
The idea
Policy Transparency Games (PTGs) are two-stage games in which each player first chooses transparency and then plays a normal-form game; for two players all transparency incentives reduce to a leader wedge Li − Ni and a follower wedge Fi − Ni per player, and the adaptive WEDGE-CERTIFY algorithm samples only the informative profiles to certify the wedge signs.
Strengths
The paper proves that the wedge signs characterize every equilibrium, that WEDGE-CERTIFY attains instance-dependent certificate complexity Õ(σ²(d0⁻² + d10⁻² + d01⁻²)) with matching information-theoretic lower bounds up to logarithmic factors, and that the wedge logic recovers known cases such as transparency being dominant under Cournot competition.
What the authors leave open
  • Transparency is modelled as commitment to an observable mixed action; richer policy-function transparency, stochastic or continuous transparency, incomplete information and nonstationary Stage-2 learning are left for future work.
  • A Battle of the Sexes example illustrates the predicted certification allocation, but finite-sample empirical validation remains future work.
  • Structured multi-player extensions, realization-specific certification lower bounds and decentralized certification under restricted feedback remain open.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The core wedge reduction is fundamentally restricted to 2-player environments.
  • this is an oracle lower bound and not explicitly a realization lower bound over the space of finite base games.
  • Lack experimental validation. The paper is all theory, with no experiments.
  • The theory in this paper assumes two symmetric players and no cost to transparency.

Topic description

The paper builds a neural operator from three layer types that are symplectic by construction, so that every layer and every rollout step preserves the symplectic form of Hamiltonian dynamics rather than relying on soft physics penalties. On five Hamiltonian PDE benchmarks it reports stable long-term rollouts, lower trajectory error, more accurate conservation of physical invariants and up to two orders of magnitude fewer parameters than two standard neural operator baselines.

Papers (1)
SympFNO: Structure-Preserving Fourier Neural Operators for Physical Surrogate Modeling NeurIPS 2026 · accepted
The problem
Neural operators learn solution maps of partial differential equations (PDEs) efficiently, but standard architectures break the geometric structure of Hamiltonian (energy-conserving) dynamics during inference, which makes long-horizon rollouts unstable.
The idea
The Symplectic Fourier Neural Operator (SympFNO) is built from three symplectic layers, Fourier Shear, Nonlinear Shear and Physical Rotation, so that every layer and every rollout step preserves the symplectic form of Hamiltonian dynamics by construction rather than through soft physics penalties.
Strengths
On five Hamiltonian PDE benchmarks the paper reports stable long-term rollouts, lower trajectory error, more accurate conservation of physical invariants and up to two orders of magnitude fewer parameters than FNO and PINO.
What the authors leave open
  • Exact symplecticity does not imply exact conservation of all physical invariants: SympFNO can reduce energy drift without guaranteeing perfect energy conservation.
  • Dissipative systems such as heat diffusion, viscous flows, reaction-diffusion and Navier–Stokes dynamics are not purely symplectic, so the architecture is not guaranteed to learn them; a symplectic-plus-corrective decomposition is left beyond the scope of the work.
  • Combining SympFNO with energy, mass or momentum constraints, projection-based post-processing, or multi-step training is left for future work.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The energy-drift guarantee in the paper is derived under restrictive assumptions, mainly for linear Hamiltonian systems and under the condition that the learned map is sufficiently close to the exact flow.
  • the exact symplecticity results are formulated after spatial discretization, on the finite-dimensional phase space
  • The tradeoff between structure and expressivity is not fully analyzed. In Table 2, it seems that ProjFNO is superior to SympFNO in terms of energy drift in some cases
  • The proposed method is restricted to Hamiltonian PDEs. In general, symplecticity does not imply full conservation. No convergence rate analysis or UAT-like result is provided.
