Research directions: Ngo Van Linh

Ngo Van Linh (HUST) · All professors

Knowledge distillation, continual learning and topic modelling for language and vision models, aiming at compact and efficient systems. Work on distilling large language models across tokenizers, plus topic modelling, relation and event extraction, retrieval-augmented generation and token-level preference optimization. Work on continual learning with prompts, including evolving centroids for online incremental learning, and on distilling nested embeddings usable at several sizes. Work on distilling vision-language embedding models into compact students, covering a survey, nested embeddings and alignment of hierarchical structure.

Open challenges. Work on language-model systems leaves open computational cost, reliance on dictionaries and LLM calls, and evaluation limited to English-Vietnamese settings. Work on continual learning with prompts leaves open reliance on hand-designed class hierarchies, restriction to image classification, and theory that ignores distribution shift over time. Work on distilling vision-language embedding models leaves open the teacher-student capacity mismatch in tokenization and embedding size, black-box teachers, and evidence beyond single runs.

Topic description

The papers cover several lines of language-model work: cross-tokenizer and embedding distillation, neural and cross-lingual topic models, few-shot and continual relation and event extraction, retrieval-augmented question answering and code generation, memory for conversational agents, and token-level preference and reinforcement-learning methods. Many pair a new alignment or weighting objective with experiments on standard text benchmarks, and a recurring cost is the extra computation these objectives add.

Selective Off-Policy Reference Tuning with Plan Guidance NeurIPS 2026 · accepted
The problem
In reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards, GRPO-style methods stall on hard prompts where all sampled rollouts fail, because the group-relative advantage gives no gradient.
The idea
SORT adds a repair update for all-wrong prompts: it derives a plan from the reference solution, compares token probabilities with and without that plan, and gives higher weight to tokens that become more predictable under plan conditioning, without changing rollout generation.
Strengths
Across three backbones and eight reasoning benchmarks, SORT improves over GRPO and guidance baselines, with the largest gains on weaker models. The meta-review reports that random token emphasis stays near the GRPO baseline (41.2) while SORT reaches 43.5. Accepted as a NeurIPS 2026 poster.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The performance gains over vanilla GRPO appear relatively modest, especially on stronger backbones.
  • The proposed method introduces additional complexity compared with vanilla GRPO, as it requires extra probability evaluations along the reference path under both base and plan-conditioned contexts, as well as an additional auxiliary optimization step.
  • The theoretical analysis relies on a strong plan-quality assumption, but the paper gives mostly qualitative evidence that extracted plans approximate the ideal plan or identify causally important tokens.

Beyond Repository Boundaries: Cross-Repository Graph Retrieval for Code Generation EMNLP 2026 · rejected
The problem
Repository-level code generation must be compatible with the repository and its dependency environment. Existing retrieval methods mostly use the local repository and one-hop evidence, leaving external API use to pretrained knowledge, which may fail for unseen or version-specific APIs.
The idea
CrossCoder builds a unified knowledge graph over repository and library entities, identifies important nodes via planning and semantic retrieval, and selectively expands neighbours for multi-hop context. The paper also introduces VersionExec, an execution-based benchmark derived from BigCodeBench for dependency versions.
Strengths
On RepoExec, DevEval and VersionExec, the abstract reports improved functional correctness (up to 6.3% pass@1) and robustness to dependency-version changes. Ablations show removing planning gives the largest drop. Rejected from EMNLP 2026.
What the authors leave open
  • The implementation and evaluation focus on the Python ecosystem; other languages may need dedicated structural parsers such as AST libraries or compiler front-ends.
  • Building and indexing multi-hop cross-repository graphs adds computational and memory overhead, especially for large repositories with deep dependency chains; the authors plan to explore knowledge distillation to reduce it.
  • Future work includes using higher-level repository intent from documentation.

Don't Generate, Just Score: Enhancing Topic Coherence with Masked Semantic Alignment EMNLP 2026 · rejected
The problem
Neural topic models often yield topics with limited coherence and weak topic-document alignment. LLM-based refinements rely on repeated generative inference, with high cost, prompt sensitivity, stochastic variability and out-of-vocabulary content.
The idea

EventKD: Event-Aware Knowledge Distillation for Generative Event Extraction EMNLP 2026 · rejected
The problem
LLM-based generative event extraction (triggers, event types, arguments) is costly, so it is distilled into smaller students. Existing KD methods work at the token level and neither focus on event-relevant spans nor capture relations among event components.
The idea
EventKD is a two-level framework: a token-level divergence loss with event-aware span importance weighting, and a span-level Relational-Aware loss preserving pairwise geometry among event spans across Transformer layers.
Strengths
On ACE05, MAVEN, GENEVA and RAMS, the abstract reports EventKD outperforming existing distillation methods on trigger identification and argument extraction. Rejected from EMNLP 2026.
What the authors leave open
  • Span-level alignment alone cannot resolve cases where the teacher gives no disambiguating signal across nearly co-located spans; addressing it would likely need explicit coreference or position-aware objectives.
  • Extension to low-resource languages and other downstream domains is left to future work.

HyperProve: Answer-Guided Hypergraph Expansion for Multi-Hop Question Answering EMNLP 2026 · accepted
The problem
Multi-hop question answering often fails when retrieval treats evidence as isolated matches to the original question, though the needed facts are connected through intermediate entities, relations and constraints.
The idea
HyperProve couples question decomposition with answer-conditioned expansion over a hypergraph of atomic facts, carrying intermediate answers and supporting hyperedges as retrieval state that biases the next local hypergraph expansion.
Strengths
Across multi-hop QA benchmarks, the abstract reports average relative improvements of 6.2% in answer accuracy and 4.9% in F1 over the strongest baselines. Accepted at EMNLP 2026.
What the authors leave open
  • HyperProve incurs substantial LLM cost during offline extraction and online reasoning; the authors suggest distilling these components.
  • LLM-as-a-judge accuracy may miss subtle reasoning, evidence-sufficiency and faithfulness errors.
  • Experiments are limited to fixed open-domain QA benchmarks with stable corpora, which do not capture noisy, conflicting, multilingual or temporally changing evidence.

TokenRatio: Principled Token-Level Preference Optimization via Ratio Matching ICML 2026 · accepted
The problem
Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) models preferences over full sequences although generation is driven by per-token decisions. Existing token-level extensions decompose a sequence-level Bradley-Terry objective, leaving per-prefix optimality implicit.
The idea
Token-level Bregman Preference Optimization (TBPO) posits a token-level Bradley-Terry model and derives a Bregman-divergence density-ratio matching objective that generalises the DPO loss, with two variants: TBPO-Q learns a state baseline, TBPO-A uses advantage normalisation.
Strengths
On instruction following, helpfulness/harmlessness and summarization benchmarks, the abstract reports better alignment quality, training stability and output diversity than sequence-level and token-level baselines. Accepted at ICML 2026.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The paper does not evaluate a variant where the per-state correction weight is fixed to 1 (i.e., without state-level correction).
  • the paper does not evaluate whether adding a similar baseline head to DPO or TDPO would yield comparable gains.
  • Section 5.1 relies heavily on LLM-as-a-judge models for evaluation.
  • Time complexity: The loss function requires computing probability differences for each generated token (step), which might be expensive.

MemORAI: Memory Organization and Retrieval via Adaptive Graph Intelligence for LLM Conversational Agents ACL 2026 · rejected
The problem
LLM conversational agents lack persistent memory for long-term personalised conversations, and the paper states that existing graph-based memory systems dilute information, do not track where facts came from, and retrieve the same way regardless of the query.
The idea
MemORAI filters and compresses memories to keep user-persona content, stores them in a multi-relational graph that records the conversation turn each fact came from, and retrieves query-specific subgraphs with a Dynamic Weighted PageRank whose edge weights depend on the query.
Strengths
The paper reports state-of-the-art memory retrieval and personalised response generation on the LOCOMO and LongMemEval benchmarks.
What the authors leave open
  • Reliance on turn-level provenance and static entity linking may limit adaptability when user intent shifts abruptly or coreferences span many sessions with few explicit mentions.
  • Running large LLMs and complex graph retrieval in real time has high computational and memory cost, which limits use in resource-constrained environments.
  • Future work plans to deploy MemORAI with small language models and small embedding models trained by knowledge distillation while keeping personalised memory retrieval quality.

MTA: Multi-Granular Trajectory Alignment for Large Language Model Distillation ACL 2026 · accepted
The problem
Most LLM distillation methods align representations at fixed layers or token-level outputs, ignoring how representations evolve across depth, so the student is weakly guided to capture the teacher's internal relational structure.
The idea
MTA aligns teacher and student along the layer-wise trajectory: word-level alignment in lower layers and phrase-level spans in higher layers via a Dynamic Structural Alignment loss on relative geometry, plus a Hidden Representation Alignment loss.
Strengths
The abstract reports consistent gains over state-of-the-art baselines on standard benchmarks, with ablations confirming each component. Accepted at ACL 2026.
What the authors leave open
  • Span extraction with spaCy adds training overhead, a 1.85x slowdown over DistiLLM after optimisation (2.54x initially); inference cost is unchanged. Designing lighter-weight alignment is future work.

SRA: Span Representation Alignment for Large Language Model Distillation ACL 2026 · accepted
The problem
Cross-tokenizer knowledge distillation (CTKD) transfers knowledge from a large language model to a smaller student that uses a different tokenizer, and the paper argues that the token-level alignments used so far are brittle when the tokenizers disagree.
The idea
SRA aligns tokenizer-agnostic spans instead of tokens: each span is treated as a cluster of particles represented by its attention-weighted centre of mass, combined with a geometric regulariser on the representation space and distillation of aligned span logits.
Strengths
The paper reports that SRA outperforms state-of-the-art CTKD baselines in cross-architecture distillation experiments.

TALAS: Teacher-Anchored Layer Alignment with Adaptive Sharpness-Aware Minimization for Embedding Distillation ACL 2026 · accepted
The problem
Distilling large sentence-embedding models into small students is costly when the student must mimic the teacher's embeddings or internal features, and the paper states that the capacity gap between the two models makes this mimicry suboptimal.
The idea
TALAS distills the teacher's final sentence embeddings only into the student's upper layers, passes knowledge down to lower layers by layer-aligned self-distillation with geometric relational constraints, and trains with Adaptive Sharpness-Aware Minimization (ASAM) to reach flat minima.
Strengths
The paper reports that TALAS outperforms distillation baselines on standard sentence embedding benchmarks with lower computational cost and memory footprint during training.

DistillMoE: Multi-Faceted Knowledge Distillation for Cross-Tokenizer Embedding Models ICLR 2026 · rejected
The problem
Distilling text embedding models across different tokenizers is hard because of tokenizer mismatch, and the paper states that standard distillation frameworks do not capture the different kinds of semantic signal in the teacher's embeddings.
The idea
DistillMoE uses a lightweight Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) module whose router weights three sentence-level experts (pointwise, contrastive and pairwise objectives), plus DynamicCKA, a token-level alignment of teacher and student hidden states based on Centered Kernel Alignment.
Strengths
The paper reports gains over prior CTKD methods when distilling LLM2Vec, BGE-M3 and Qwen3 embedding models into a BERT-base student on text classification, sentence-pair classification and semantic textual similarity.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The most significant weakness of DistillMoE is the substantial computational overhead introduced during training, which is not sufficiently justified in the context of creating an efficient student model.
  • The paper's choice of the three expert objectives (pointwise, contrastive, pairwise) is intuitively appealing but lacks a strong theoretical foundation.
  • A priori, there seems to be a large potential for noise in the proposed token alignment mechanism.
  • the proposed method is only evaluated using a single, relatively small bert-base model.

Token-Regulated Group Relative Policy Optimization for Stable Reinforcement Learning in Large Language Models ICLR 2026 · rejected
The problem
In reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) for large language models, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) lets low-probability tokens dominate gradient updates because of their large gradient magnitudes, which leads to unstable training and suppresses high-probability tokens.
The idea
TR-GRPO assigns token-level weights positively correlated with the model's predicted probability, down-weighting low-probability tokens and emphasizing high-probability ones. The authors provide a theoretical analysis of how token probability governs gradient norms.
Strengths
The authors report that TR-GRPO consistently outperforms GRPO across RLVR tasks including logic and math.
What the authors leave open
  • The primary limitation of TR-GRPO is the additional computational overhead introduced by token-level weighting.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The proposed solution—the design of the weighting function—feels somewhat heuristic and lacks a strong theoretical derivation.
  • The paper's empirical analysis is primarily positioned against the established GRPO baseline and omits comparisons with other recent state-of-the-art RL methods for LLMs, such as DAPO.

GloCTM: Cross-Lingual Topic Modeling via a Global Context Space AAAI 2026 · accepted
The problem
Cross-lingual topic models should produce coherent topics that mean the same thing across languages, but the paper states that most models learn topics in separate language-specific spaces linked by bilingual dictionaries and ignore multilingual pretrained representations.
The idea
GloCTM expands bag-of-words inputs with cross-lingual lexical neighbours, infers topic proportions with aligned local and global encoders, uses one topic-word distribution over the combined vocabulary, and adds a Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) loss that aligns the topic space with multilingual contextual embeddings.
Strengths
The paper reports better topic coherence, topic diversity and cross-lingual classification than the baselines on multiple benchmarks, and shows example topics with fewer unrelated words than InfoCTM, SVD-LR and u-SVD.
What the authors leave open
  • The authors identify language-specific bag-of-words vectors that live in disjoint feature spaces as a key limitation of traditional cross-lingual topic models.

MCW-KD: Multi-Cost Wasserstein Knowledge Distillation for Large Language Models AAAI 2026 · accepted
The problem
Standard knowledge distillation (KD) for compressing large language models falters when teacher and student differ in architecture or tokenization, creating mismatched representations. Optimal Transport (OT) based methods that align them usually rely on a single cost function.
The idea
MCW-KD optimizes several cost functions at once within a unified OT formulation, using specific cost matrices to align both the final hidden states and the output distributions of teacher and student, with a theoretical foundation for the Multi-Cost Wasserstein Distance.
Strengths
The authors report significant gains over state-of-the-art KD baselines on instruction-following datasets, especially when teacher and student have different tokenizers.
What the authors leave open
  • Predefined cost matrices may not capture all representational aspects, and computing the optimal transport plan can be demanding for large-scale matrices.
  • Future work will investigate new cost matrices tailored to diverse representation spaces and ways to improve computational efficiency.

Token-Level Self-Play with Importance-Aware Guidance for Large Language Models NeurIPS 2025 · accepted
The problem
Self-Play Fine-Tuning (SPIN) lets a model generate its own rejected samples, reducing the need for human preference labels, but it applies learning signals uniformly to all tokens. As the model improves, rejected samples increasingly contain high-quality tokens, so uniform treatment becomes suboptimal.
The idea
SWIFT (Self-Play Weighted Fine-Tuning) assigns token-level importance weights estimated from a stronger teacher model. It also serves as a knowledge distillation strategy, using the teacher for reward-guided token weighting rather than logit matching.
Strengths
The authors report that SWIFT consistently surpasses existing alignment approaches and conventional knowledge distillation methods across diverse benchmarks and settings.
What the authors leave open
  • Using the teacher model repeatedly across multiple self-play iterations may introduce additional overhead.
  • Future work may explore more efficient strategies and a deeper analysis of how token weighting influences learning dynamics.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • A limitation of the experimental setup is that the student model used is relatively small, with approximately 1 billion parameters, which may not accurately represent more complex models.
  • By introducing a teacher model, and computing token-level weight, the computational cost can be an issue.
  • The success of SWIFT is fundamentally tied to the availability of a powerful teacher model.
  • In the alignment experiments, SWIFT is trained on 50k SFT dataset and other DPO-style baselines are trained on 62k preference samples.

EMO: Embedding Model Distillation via Intra-Model Relation and Optimal Transport Alignments EMNLP 2025 · accepted
The problem
When a text embedding teacher and its student use different tokenizers, vocabulary mismatch blocks the transfer of relational knowledge held in hidden states and attention matrices, which the paper states existing CTKD methods neglect by aligning only outputs.
The idea
EMO maps tokens one-to-one by minimum edit distance, aligns the attention patterns of the top-m most important mapped tokens with Centered Kernel Alignment, and aligns final hidden states with optimal transport whose mass is assigned by attention-based importance scores.
Strengths
The paper reports that EMO outperforms existing CTKD baselines when distilling LLM2Vec and BGE models into BERT-base-uncased on text classification, sentence-pair classification and semantic textual similarity.
What the authors leave open
  • Because attention distillation only uses tokens mapped one-to-one by minimum edit distance and then the top-m of these, some tokens are excluded from structural alignment, which could lose nuanced relational information.
  • The authors describe transferring relational knowledge across different tokenizers for embedding models as an open challenge.

Mitigating Non-Representative Prototypes and Representation Bias in Few-Shot Continual Relation Extraction ACL 2025 · accepted
The problem
Few-shot continual relation extraction (FCRE) learns new relation types from a handful of examples without forgetting old ones, and the paper states that with similar classes and few samples, class prototypes are unrepresentative and representations are biased.
The idea
Minion builds class prototypes on a General Orthogonal Frame, a structure motivated by Neural Collapse that keeps classes well separated without a preset number of classes, and uses label-description embeddings as global class representatives in fast-slow contrastive learning.

What the authors leave open
  • Minion adds computational overhead, and its label-description contrastive learning component is more expensive than the prototype component.

A Spectral Viewpoint on Continual Relation Extraction EMNLP 2023 · rejected
The problem
In Continual Relation Extraction, models undergo representation shift, where the learned deep space changes during continual learning and degrades performance on old tasks. The paper studies this phenomenon from a spectral viewpoint.
The idea
A spectral experiment shows that, for each class shape, eigenvectors with larger eigenvalues are better preserved after learning new tasks. The authors then propose a class-wise regularization (feature decorrelation) that increases eigenvalues in representation learning.
Strengths
The authors report significant improvement over state-of-the-art models on FewRel and TACRED, and further analyses supporting that larger eigenvalues lead to better performance.
What the authors leave open
  • There is a lack of interpretation of the directions with larger eigenvalues; examples demonstrating their superiority would be preferable.
  • Like prior CRE work, it only classifies a given pair of entities into relation types; a complete solution would require studying named entity recognition in continual learning, and the authors plan to extend the analysis to other information extraction tasks.

Selecting Optimal Context Sentences for Event-Event Relation Extraction AAAI 2022 · accepted
The problem
For subevent relation extraction (SRE) and temporal event relation extraction (TRE), transformer-based models such as BERT can encode only limited-length input (up to 512 sub-tokens), so they cannot capture important context sentences located farther away in the document.
