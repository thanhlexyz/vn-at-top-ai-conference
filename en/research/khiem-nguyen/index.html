Khiem Nguyen (EIU) · All professors

Prof. Khiem's paper proposes a neural network building block for representing multiplicative relationships between input features. Work on learnable power-product layers multiplies affine layers by gates with learnable exponents, so networks directly express power laws and input products.

Open challenges. Work on learnable power-product layers leaves open whether gains hold against parameter-matched and closely related baselines, and for inputs that are not strictly positive.

Deep learning and representation learning (1 paper · CSRankings score 0.50)

Topic description

Piecewise-linear networks approximate multiplicative and power-law interactions only piecewise, and earlier product units were unstable and undefined on non-positive inputs. The paper multiplies an affine layer by a power-product gate with learnable exponents, keeps inputs positive with a dedicated normalization, and normalizes the gate by dimension; it proves exact monomial representation and stability properties. It is evaluated on physics-law extrapolation, synthetic monomials, tabular regression and image classification, with mixed results across these tasks.

Papers (1)
PolyGate: Continuous Power-Product Gating for Deep Neural Networks ICLR 2027 · rejected
The problem
Networks with piecewise-linear activations such as ReLU can only approximate smooth polynomial and multiplicative interactions (for example power laws in physics) piecewise, while earlier product units that model them directly suffered from unstable optimization, logarithmic singularities near zero and undefined fractional powers of negative inputs.
The idea
PolyGate multiplies an affine layer by a gate that is a product of the inputs raised to learnable continuous exponents; Strict-Positivity Batch Normalization (SP-BN) keeps inputs positive so the powers are well defined, and dimension-normalized gating controls activation variance as width grows.
Strengths
The paper proves exact representation of univariate and multivariate monomials, gradient regularity on strictly positive bounded domains and near-identity initialization, and reviewers note that on several multiplicative AI-Feynman physics laws it extrapolates better than a parameter-matched MLP.
What the authors leave open
  • Power operations are only defined on strictly positive inputs, so the design depends on SP-BN and explicit positive-domain handling to stay away from zero and negative values.
  • In the purely multiplicative regime, the logarithm of the gate is a single linear function of the log-inputs, so the gate is restricted to a rank-1 subspace of logarithmic coordinate space.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The theoretical claims are not fully consistent with the implementation. In particular, the exact monomial representation results do not account for the numerical clamps used in the implemented operator.
  • POLYGATE often obtains negative OOD (R^2) on the controlled monomial tasks and underperforms standard MLPs and tree-based methods on tabular datasets.
  • In Table 4, the matched-wider network has the same parameter count and beats PolyGate on all four datasets (e.g., CIFAR-100 63.65 vs 61.05).
  • The paper does not directly compare against the closest related methods, making it difficult to assess the advantage of the proposed combination.
