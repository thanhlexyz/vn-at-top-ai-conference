Research directions: Minh-Triet Tran

Minh-Triet Tran (HCMUS) · All professors

Research on robust and trustworthy learning, diffusion-based generation and computer vision recognition. Work on few-shot instance segmentation with diffusion mask models, dictionary-guided scene text recognition and multi-camera tracking via dynamic-graph link prediction. Work on zero-shot real-image editing through attention control and personalized single- and multi-concept generation. Work on auditing modality-importance claims from attribution scores, using statistical inference across several plausible ways of removing a modality.

Open challenges. Work on few-shot instance segmentation with open non-COCO evidence, scene text recognition with open similar characters and multi-camera tracking with open incremental scaling. Work on mask-guided editing with open mask dependence and personalized generation with open many-concept prompts. Work on auditing modality-importance claims leaves open results beyond one dataset and predictor, and effects such as feature scale and covariance.

Computer vision and multimodal learning (3 papers · CSRankings score 0.45)

Topic description

A paper on few-shot instance segmentation replaces prototype-based point estimates with a diffusion probabilistic model of the binary mask conditioned on an object region and K-shot information, using classifier-free guided sampling for category information, and reports consistent gains over state-of-the-art methods on base and novel classes of COCO. A paper on scene text recognition uses a dictionary in training and inference to generate candidate words and select the one most compatible with the visual appearance of the text, improving text spotting frameworks such as ABCNet on TotalText, ICDAR13, ICDAR15 and VinText, a new Vietnamese dataset released with the code. A paper on multi-camera multiple object tracking casts global tracklet association across cameras as link prediction on a dynamic graph with attention modules for structural and temporal variations, reporting large margins over existing multi-camera tracking methods on several datasets.

Papers (3)
MaskDiff: Modeling Mask Distribution with Diffusion Probabilistic Model for Few-Shot Instance Segmentation AAAI 2024 · accepted
The problem
Few-shot instance segmentation segments objects of novel categories from a few annotated examples; prototype-based point estimation (for example the mean of K shots) leads to unstable performance.
The idea
Models the conditional distribution of a binary mask given an object region and K-shot information with a diffusion probabilistic model, and uses classifier-free guided mask sampling to bring in category information.
Strengths
Reports consistently better results than state-of-the-art methods on both base and novel classes of COCO while being more stable than existing methods.

Dictionary-Guided Scene Text Recognition CVPR 2021 · accepted
The problem
Scene text recognition methods often use a lexicon only by casting the output to the dictionary word with the smallest edit distance, which the paper says has many limitations, in particular for text whose characters are visually ambiguous.
The idea
The method incorporates a dictionary in both training and inference: it uses the dictionary to generate a list of candidate words and selects the one most compatible with the visual appearance of the text.
Strengths
The paper reports that adding the dictionary improves state-of-the-art text spotting frameworks such as ABCNet on TotalText, ICDAR13, ICDAR15 and VinText, a new Vietnamese scene text dataset released with the code.
What the authors leave open
  • VinText, where Vietnamese accent marks make some characters visually equivocal, brings new challenges in discriminating a character from multiple similar ones.

Link · Source

DyGLIP: A Dynamic Graph Model With Link Prediction for Accurate Multi-Camera Multiple Object Tracking CVPR 2021 · accepted
The problem
Data association in multi-camera multiple object tracking is difficult under inconsistent lighting, varying movement patterns and trajectory occlusion between cameras.
The idea
Treats global tracklet ID association across cameras as link assignment in a dynamic graph with link prediction, with attention modules capturing structural and temporal variations across cameras and time steps.
Strengths
Reports outperforming existing multi-camera tracking methods by a large margin on several datasets, up to 63% on some metrics on overlapping-view datasets and up to 35% on one non-overlapping dataset.

Generative models (2 papers · CSRankings score 0.37)

Topic description

A paper on zero-shot real image editing with diffusion models keeps the edited object's identity and texture apart from the background through a preservation adaptation of self-attention, limits cross-attention to the object's pixels with a localized extraction module, tailors mask strategies to different editing tasks without fine-tuning Stable Diffusion, and introduces a new real-image editing benchmark. A paper on personalized image generation combines a single-concept part, with a Kronecker adapter, disentangled learning and attention regularization, and a multi-concept part that reuses the single-concept models to generate multi-concept images directly without layout boxes or semantic masks.

Papers (2)
Rethinking Attentions in Zero-Shot Real Image Editing ICLR 2025 · rejected
The problem
Editing real images from text with diffusion models is hard when consistency and complex non-rigid objects are needed; existing methods often fail to preserve texture and identity, need extensive fine-tuning, or struggle to edit one region while keeping the background.
The idea
A zero-shot method that does not fine-tune Stable Diffusion: a preservation adaptation of self-attention keeps the edited object's identity and texture apart from the background, a localized extraction module limits cross-attention to the object's pixels, and mask strategies are tailored to different editing tasks.
Strengths
Shows edits for object replacement, removal, background alteration, adding items and changes of pose or view while preserving the structure and layout of the original image, and introduces a new benchmark for real-image editing.
What the authors leave open
  • A limitation is attributed to diffusion models being trained on image-caption datasets whose captions may lack detailed descriptions of text prompts for real-world images.
  • Failure cases occur when the mask is imprecise; the authors list addressing these problems as future work.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The dependency on high-quality masks can limit practicality in fully automated editing and in situations where generating precise masks is difficult.
  • The proposed mask-guidance strategies are rule-based and not flexible enough to cope with diverse editing needs.
  • Table 1 shows only marginal improvements in metrics that measure overall image quality and background.

ConceptFlow: Unified Framework for Personalized Image Generation ICLR 2025 · rejected
The problem
Personalized image generation: for one concept it is hard to balance identity preservation and prompt alignment in complex prompts; for several concepts, generating from a single prompt without layout boxes or masks causes identity loss and concept omission.
The idea
A framework with a single-concept part (a Kronecker adapter with weight and embedding decomposition, disentangled learning and a new attention regularization objective) and a multi-concept part that reuses the single-concept models to generate multi-concept images directly, without additional conditions.
Strengths
Covers single- and multi-concept personalization in one framework; the multi-concept part needs no layout boxes or semantic masks, and evaluation uses DINO (identity) and CLIP-T (editability) scores.
What the authors leave open
  • The framework cannot yet handle prompts with too many concepts.
  • The single-concept model fails to capture sophisticated concept details such as text and logos when training and generation resolutions differ.
  • The multi-concept model shows failures in generating multiple humans, and the underlying Stable Diffusion 1.5 struggles with complex prompts of more than two subjects.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • it remains uncertain if the proposed work can handle multiple personalized instances (> 2), particularly for contexts involving up to five subjects.
  • Only a limited number of comparison methods, especially in multi-concept generation, does not adequately support the authors' claim.
  • This is particularly evident in CLIP-T, which evaluates editability and does not support the authors' claims of balancing the trade-off between reconstruction and editability.

Papers (1)
When Same Sign Is Not Enough: Auditing Modality-Dominance Claims ICLR 2027 · rejected
The problem
Explanations of multimodal predictors are often summarised as claims that one input modality (for example numeric features) dominates another (for example text); the paper asks whether such a directional claim stays statistically resolved across a declared family of plausible ways of removing a modality, since removal semantics and estimation uncertainty can each change explanations.
The idea
The paper audits a fixed text–numeric predictor under three removal operators, subtracts an operator-specific empirical no-signal reference from the observed reliance gap, and uses paired inference that propagates dependent-sample and finite-null-bank uncertainty; the underlying attribution score is unchanged.
Strengths
In a leakage-audited financial forecasting stress test, the abstract reports that all 21 operator–measurement point estimates favour numeric reliance, yet only 5 of 7 measurements are resolved across the full operator family, while a prespecified marginal-versus-regime-matched contrast excludes zero on all seven.
What the authors leave open
  • Featurewise scale, covariance, support, angular and model-direction effects are left open.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The paper’s experimental focus is on a single dataset and a single logistic predictor.
  • I would like to see how that count moves with the block length (Appendix A.1), the confidence level and the null subsample size
  • It is well known that permutation approaches are not really meaningful when the goal is to learn something about the data / the model's behavior on realistic data
  • The 5/7 versus 2/7 number is basically a thresholding effect, and the paper reads the wrong pattern out of it.

Papers (1)
Restoring Co-Adaptation: An Endpoint Audit of Asymmetric Training in Multi-Agent Market Making ICLR 2027 · rejected
The problem
When the endpoint of asymmetric multi-agent training (some agents frozen while others learn) is evaluated with all policies frozen, the evaluation switches off the learning responses whose suppression helped shape that endpoint, so an outcome that looks stable when frozen may change once agents adapt again.
The idea
The paper separates frozen persistence from co-adaptive response stability and introduces a restoration audit: resume all suppressed update channels from the learned endpoint and compare the change in a system statistic with a same-budget continuation from a co-adaptive (simultaneously trained) reference.
Strengths
In a three-agent market-making testbed, the abstract reports that staged train-and-freeze training raises the mean minimum quoted spread (MQS) by 0.02854 over simultaneous co-adaptation (20/20 seeds positive), that after 600 restored episodes the mean recovery fraction is 0.9953, and that the matched simultaneous continuation drifts by 6.67×10^-5.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • No cross domain analysis beyond the toy testbed environment is conducted.
  • Many market mechanisms important for studying market making, such as those related to limited order book, are missing from the testbed.
  • Is this something we should generally expect from asymmetric training? Or does it depend on the particular game, the learning algorithm, or the way the training stages are arranged?
  • For example, does this audit tell us something that we would otherwise miss when evaluating existing MARL methods? Would it change which algorithm we consider better?

Papers (1)
Improving Resistance to Noisy Label Fitting by Reweighting Gradient in SAM ICLR 2025 · rejected
The problem
Noisy labels cause overfitting and poor generalization; Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) generalizes better than SGD under label noise, but its behaviour in more realistic training settings is less explored.
The idea
Analyses SAM's gradient at each iteration to identify the components that slow noisy-label fitting, then reweights those components in a SAM variant that manages the rate of noisy fitting.
Strengths
Experiments on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100 and Mini-WebVision show the variant outperforming SAM, with up to an 8% gain on CIFAR-100 with 50% label noise.
What the authors leave open
  • The analysis of the third group of SAM gradient components is left for future work; the paper compares only the first two groups.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • Experimental results for high label noise settings (e.g., 80% symmetric label noise) are absent.
  • One glaring gap in the analysis of the paper is the effect of early-stopping.
  • Both the reweighting factor and k needs to be tuned and I didn't find an ablation study or any insight on how to set these parameters for different datasets/architectures.
  • The paper lacks a detailed explanation of how the gradient components are divided into Group A, B, and C.
