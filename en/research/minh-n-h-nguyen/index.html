Research directions: Minh N. H. Nguyen

Minh N. H. Nguyen (UDN) · All professors

Federated learning from multimodal data with few labels, exchanging embedding knowledge between server and clients. Work on multimodal federated learning with scarce client labels exchanges embedding knowledge between server and clients using a small proxy dataset.

Open challenges. Work on multimodal federated learning still depends on a shared proxy dataset and on exchanging local embeddings, which raises privacy and practicality concerns.

Topic description

This paper addresses federated learning (FL), where most methods use single-modal, labeled client data. A semi-supervised multimodal framework lets server and clients exchange personalized and generalized representations through a small multimodal proxy dataset, with a generalized encoder guiding personalized encoders. Experiments on three multimodal human activity recognition tasks are reported.

The problem
Most federated learning (FL) approaches use only single-modal data and rely on labeled data at the client side, which is limited in practice because users cannot self-annotate their data.
The idea
A multimodal FL framework in a semi-supervised setting: server and clients exchange personalized and generalized multimodal representations (embedding knowledge) through a small multimodal proxy dataset; a generalized encoder, updated from the clients' collaborative embedding knowledge such as modality-averaging representations, guides personalized encoders, and personalized classifiers are trained on the proxy labeled data.
Strengths
Reports, on three multimodal human activity recognition tasks, superior performance of both local and global encoder models on linear evaluation, and privacy for personal data and model parameters.
What the authors leave open
  • Future work will extend the research with multimodal fusion design and a larger number of modalities, to bridge the gap to deploying FL in personalized applications.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • Another crucial limitation of the method is exchanging the local embeddings.
  • A major concern was the use of public proxy labeled data.
  • The experiment is incomplete. The baseline for comparison is too limited.
  • The superiority of the method is doubtable since it can not surpass the only baseline model on several settings
