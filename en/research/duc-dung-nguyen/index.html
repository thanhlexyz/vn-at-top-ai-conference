Research directions: Duc-Dung Nguyen

Duc-Dung Nguyen (HCMUT) · All professors

Prof. Dung's papers aim to lower the computation or the annotation that computer-vision models need. Work on lightweight image super-resolution widens the receptive field at low computational cost, and work on 3D scene completion learns from few labels.

Open challenges. Work on semi-supervised 3D scene completion with few labels still reaches only part of fully supervised performance.

Topic description

One paper combines window-based spatial attention, a distillation-based large-kernel module and cross-layer parameter sharing for lightweight super-resolution, and reports 31.63 dB PSNR on Manga109 at 4× upscaling with 48.4G MACs. The other uses 2D vision foundation models to generate 3D geometric and semantic cues that guide semantic scene completion with few labels, and reports up to 85% of fully supervised performance on SemanticKITTI and NYUv2 with 10% of the labelled data.

Papers (2)
UCAN: Unified Convolutional Attention Network for Expansive Receptive Fields in Lightweight Super-Resolution CVPR 2026 · accepted
The problem
Hybrid CNN-Transformer models give strong image super-resolution results, but enlarging attention windows or convolution kernels to widen the receptive field raises computational cost and, according to the paper, limits deployment on resource-constrained devices.
The idea
UCAN combines window-based spatial attention with a Hedgehog Attention mechanism to model local texture and long-range dependencies, adds a distillation-based large-kernel module to preserve high-frequency structure, and uses cross-layer parameter sharing to reduce complexity.
Strengths
The paper reports that UCAN-L reaches 31.63 dB PSNR on Manga109 at 4× upscaling with 48.4G MACs (multiply–accumulate operations), and that UCAN reaches 27.79 dB on BSDS100, above methods with significantly larger models.

Semi-supervised 3D Semantic Scene Completion with 2D Vision Foundation Model Guidance AAAI 2025 · accepted
The problem
Semantic scene completion predicts the 3D occupancy and semantic class of every voxel of a scene from 2D images, which autonomous agents use for planning and navigation; fully supervised methods need large datasets labelled voxel by voxel from expensive LiDAR scans.
The idea
A semi-supervised framework uses 2D vision foundation models to generate 3D geometric and semantic cues that guide training with few labels, and applies to both 2D-3D lifting and 3D-2D transformer approaches to scene completion.
Strengths
The paper reports that on SemanticKITTI and NYUv2 the method reaches up to 85% of fully supervised performance using only 10% of the labelled data.
