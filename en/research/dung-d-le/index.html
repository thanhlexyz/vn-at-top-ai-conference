Research directions: Dung D. Le

Dung D. Le (VinUni) · All professors

Efficient and robust training methods and multimodal learning, plus federated settings, unlearning and dialogue planning. Work on adapting CLIP to long captions, on few-shot classification with synthetic images, and on object-level out-of-distribution detection with generative models. Work on meta pseudo-labeling without second-order gradients, memory-efficient continual learning, and attention architectures with fewer parameters. Work on removing specified training data from models, in mixture-of-experts and federated settings, and on predicting adversarial robustness.

Open challenges. Work on image-text alignment in CLIP that is evaluated mainly on retrieval, at small training scale and with limited comparison to region-based methods. Work on semi-supervised and continual learning where analysis covers idealized settings and reported gains remain small or weakly demonstrated. Work on unlearning by design that needs deletion groups known at training time and may degrade with very many clients.

Papers (5)
MulCLIP: A Multi-level Alignment Framework for Enhancing Fine-grained Long-context CLIP NeurIPS 2026 · accepted
The problem
Vision-language models such as CLIP are trained on short captions and struggle with lengthy, detailed descriptions; recent fixes that map visual regions to sentences using region proposals incur notable deployment costs.
The idea
An end-to-end multi-level alignment framework: global contrastive alignment of images with summary and long captions (with extended positional embeddings), a token reconstruction alignment over locally calibrated features linking words and image patches, and a subcaption-aggregated patch alignment that extracts and aggregates context-rich patches for each subcaption.
Strengths
Reports consistent downstream gains on diverse benchmarks, and ablations attribute the fine-grained improvement to the multi-scale alignment; the reviews describe long-caption retrieval on DOCCI, DCI and Urban1K and short-caption retrieval on COCO and Flickr30k.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • Although the paper is clearly written, its experimental performance is still inferior to that of FG-CLIP, a method proposed in 2025.
  • Furthermore, it is only evaluated in retrieval scenarios, but presented overall as a potential replacement to CLIP
  • This shows empirical incompatibility under the tested configuration, but does not establish that the cause is specifically gradient or optimization interference.
  • the preservation of short-text performance is better described as a trade-off or competitive performance rather than a consistent improvement.

CLIP-FMoE: Scalable CLIP via Fused Mixture-of-Experts with Enforced Specialization ICLR 2026 · accepted
The problem
Existing mixture-of-experts adaptations of CLIP suffer significant computational overhead during sequential training and degradation of zero-shot capabilities.
The idea
Integrates a mixture-of-experts into CLIP fine-tuning using Isolated Constrained Contrastive Learning, which trains specialized experts on cluster-based data partitions to speed up expert specialization, and a Fusion Gate that mitigates catastrophic forgetting of pre-trained knowledge.
Strengths
Reports consistent downstream improvements while preserving zero-shot capabilities and robust performance across context lengths; the reviews cite 72.5% less training time than a prior CLIP mixture-of-experts method and 14% higher inference FLOPs.
What the authors leave open
  • Future work could extend the approach to other multimodal alignment scenarios such as video-text, audio-text or multilingual vision-language tasks.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The method is only evaluated on small-scale CLIP training, and the generalization over much larger-scale dataset is hard to determine.
  • The impact of the specific clustering algorithm and hyperparameters (e.g., choice of N and M clusters) on final performance is not thoroughly explored.
  • The expert specialization results in Table 4 suggest the unified model often matches the performance of the single best expert, rather than demonstrating strong synergistic gains from combining multiple experts.
  • The paper uses image modality only for the clustering, but more discussion is needed.

SyNC: Balancing Fidelity and Diversity of Synthetic Data Representations in CLIP-based Few-Shot Learning via Neural Collapse ICLR 2026 · rejected
The problem
In few-shot learning, augmenting real data with images from text-to-image diffusion models is promising, but existing work does not adequately address the trade-off between fidelity and diversity when training with synthetic data.
The idea
Two complementary mechanisms in feature space: an equiangular tight frame prototype structure from the Neural Collapse phenomenon guides real and synthetic representations toward their class prototypes for fidelity, and a regional contrastive loss separates misclassified synthetic features for diversity.
Strengths
Outperforms state-of-the-art approaches on average across few-shot image classification benchmarks, with the largest gains on fine-grained datasets such as FGVC Aircraft and Stanford Cars according to the reviews.
What the authors leave open
  • Future work could explore other Neural Collapse variants, such as equiangular tight frame structures for imbalanced data, or improvements to Proxy-NCA loss functions.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • While the method is designed to be robust, there is no analysis of how its performance degrades with lower-quality generators or noisier synthetic data.
  • the four key assumptions of NC (large batch size, class balance, sufficient training, and real data) are all violated in the small-sample + synthetic regime.
  • The average gain over ImagineFSL is small (~+0.4 pp) and mainly driven by FGVC-Aircraft, with several datasets underperforming
  • There is no empirical complexity comparison for fine-tuning (latency, peak memory), leaving the cost–accuracy trade-off unclear.

Zero-shot Object-level Out-of-distribution Detection with Context-aware Inpainting ICLR 2025 · rejected
The problem
Modern object detectors are overconfident, so relying on their responses alone is unreliable for detecting when they misrecognize an out-of-distribution object as a seen class.
The idea
A post-hoc, zero-shot detector that does not fine-tune the object detector: given a detected box and predicted label, an off-the-shelf text-to-image model (e.g., Stable Diffusion) performs class-conditioned inpainting on the box-removed image, and the reconstruction error against the original serves as an error indicator.
Strengths
Reports outperforming prior zero-shot and non-zero-shot OOD detection approaches; the reviews describe PASCAL VOC and BDD100k as in-distribution sets and MS-COCO and OpenImages as OOD sets, with one setting (BDD100k versus OpenImages) where it trails comparison methods.
What the authors leave open
  • Failure cases arise from limitations of the underlying diffusion models; the paper presents three examples where correct OOD detection failed for this reason.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • As the authors themselves discuss, high-quality inpainting is computationally time-consuming, as inference also takes a long time for each image.
  • If the ID class and OOD class are close, image inpainting with clear class distinctions and precise similarity evaluation may become difficult, resulting in less accurate OOD detection.
  • The results of inpainting are dependent on the initial noise, which can lead to large variations in OOD detection performance.
  • Further validation on a variety of datasets is needed to ensure broad applicability and robustness.

Trajectory-Matching Meta Pseudo-Labeling for Semi-Supervised Learning NeurIPS 2026 · accepted
The problem
Meta pseudo-labeling updates a teacher according to whether its pseudo-labels help a student improve on labeled data, but one-step meta-gradient methods differentiate through the student update, introducing mixed higher-order derivatives, extra memory cost and noisy minibatch meta-gradients.
The idea
A first-order alternative that matches finite differences of the outer objective and the pseudo-label-induced student objective along recent student displacements, storing only a FIFO cache of detached scalar objective values; the paper shows the one-step meta objective is locally equivalent to maximizing gradient alignment and proves small matching error controls gradient mismatch within the span of recent displacements.
Strengths
Improves over meta pseudo-labeling in all eight label regimes on CIFAR10, CIFAR100, SVHN and STL10 and is best in five; on CIFAR100 with 2500 labels it reduces peak memory by 39.7% relative to meta pseudo-labeling and 54.0% relative to a raw differentiable metagradient implementation.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • In other words, the theoretical results do not fully cover the practical implementation.
  • If these trajectory directions lack sufficient diversity or are highly collinear, the amount of recoverable gradient information is limited.
  • The scalar-cache implementation is only an approximation.
  • The experiments focus primarily on standard image classification SSL benchmarks (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, SVHN, and STL-10), without validation in more complex real-world scenarios, such as large-scale ImageNet-style SSL, or under challenging conditions like class imbalance, label noise, and distribution shift.

Memory-Efficient Continual Learning with Prototypical Exemplar Condensation CVPR 2026 · rejected
The problem
Rehearsal-based continual learning counters catastrophic forgetting by replaying stored samples of earlier tasks, but coreset-selection methods typically need to store many samples per class, often more than 20.
The idea
Synthesizes and stores prototypical exemplars that form representative prototypes when passed through the feature extractor, so few samples per class suffice and privacy is preserved, plus a perturbation-based augmentation that generates synthetic variants of previous data during training.
Strengths
Reports superior performance over existing baselines on widely used benchmarks, particularly with large-scale datasets and many tasks, using 2 samples per class versus 20 for replay-based comparisons.
What the authors leave open
  • A single sample per class is insufficient for strong class discrimination; the authors plan to explore margin-based objectives to further enhance inter-class discriminability.
  • Generating prototypical exemplars relies on noise, a limitation shared with a prior method; mitigating this is stated as an important direction for future work.
  • Balancing exemplar fidelity with computational efficiency remains an open challenge.

Exploiting Client Heterogeneity for Forgetting Mitigation in Federated Continual Learning: A Spatio-Temporal Gradient Alignment Approach ICLR 2026 · rejected
The problem
In federated continual learning where clients may have unrelated or heterogeneous tasks, local updates point in divergent directions (gradient conflicts), and statistical heterogeneity and data noise can create spurious correlations that lead to biased features and catastrophic forgetting; generative replay itself suffers forgetting and task divergence.
The idea
Spatio-Temporal grAdient alignMent with Prototypical coreset: a model-agnostic selection of samples that form prototypes with a prototypical network, plus temporal gradient alignment on clients and spatial gradient alignment on the server; the paper also derives a generalization bound.
Strengths
Per the reviews, experiments on several vision datasets report improvements over existing federated continual learning baselines in accuracy, stability and efficiency, and performance on par with FedWeIT with much lower disk usage.

  • Calculating gradient alignment implicitly requires storing gradients from previous tasks and clients. This may become costly for large models.
  • Gradient alignment might slow the convergence, especially when updating the global model.

Improving Transformers with Probabilistic Attention Keys ICML 2022 · accepted
The problem
Multi-head attention drives state-of-the-art transformers across NLP and vision, but many heads learn redundant embeddings and most can be removed without degrading performance.
The idea
Replaces redundant heads with a mixture of keys at each head, where the keys follow a Gaussian mixture model so each head can focus on different parts of the input sequence; the formulation also extends to linear attention.
Strengths
Compared with a conventional transformer it accelerates training and inference, has fewer parameters and FLOPs with comparable or better accuracy; on Wikitext-103 and Long Range Arena, 4-head models match or beat 8-head baselines.
What the authors leave open
  • Applying the approach to improve the vision transformer is left as future work.

Papers (3)
ModULE: Learning Modular Expert Representations for Localized Machine Unlearning ICLR 2027 · rejected
The problem
Existing machine unlearning methods typically modify models post hoc and often need broad parameter updates, which costs computation and degrades performance on retained data.
The idea
A deletion-aware mixture-of-experts framework: sparse routing, balanced expert utilization and centered kernel alignment encourage sparse, differentiated expert pathways. Given a deletion request it selects experts with high forget-route coverage and limited retain exposure, updates only those, and uses output and route distillation to protect exposed retained examples.
Strengths
Under hard routing and frozen shared parameters, retained inputs whose pathways avoid the updated experts remain unchanged; experiments cover class- and domain-level deletion on PACS, OfficeHome, CIFAR-100 and Tiny-ImageNet against 15 baselines, as listed in the reviews.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The idea of unlearning by design is interesting, but it is not practical because the method needs to know the class or domain to be deleted at training time.
  • This means update only the selected experts is not enough and a good forgetting requires the last encoder block to be updated too, which does not satisfy the assumption of Lemma 1.
  • It remains unclear what evidence demonstrates that forgotten information cannot be readily recovered from the frozen backbone, prediction head, or non-selected experts.
  • The proposed method combines several distinct objectives and introduces multiple hyperparameters, raising concerns regarding its practicality and generalizability.

Computation and Communication Efficient Federated Unlearning via On-server Gradient Conflict Mitigation and Expression CVPR 2026 · accepted
The problem
Federated unlearning aims to remove specific participants' data contributions from a trained federated learning model, but progress is limited by cross-client knowledge inaccessibility and high computation and communication costs.
The idea
Two stages: a learning-to-unlearn stage in which the model identifies and encodes the key features of the forget clients, then an on-server knowledge aggregation stage that performs unlearning at the server without client data, restricting updates to a sub-network; the paper also introduces a new data setting for evaluation and a time-to-forget metric.
Strengths
On three datasets under various unlearning scenarios it outperforms retraining and achieves competitive or superior results with significantly reduced time-to-forget while keeping communication and computation costs low.
What the authors leave open
  • The gradient-alignment optimization performs best with a moderate number of clients (e.g., fewer than 100); with an extremely large client population it may converge to suboptimal solutions, and scalability and robustness in that regime is left for future research.

FLoRA-NA: Nearly Accurate Aggregation for Federated Low-Rank Adaptation ICLR 2026 · rejected
The problem
Federated low-rank adaptation (FedLoRA) suffers from inexact updates because separately averaged low-rank matrices differ from the average of their products; existing remedies introduce a local-global generalization gap or substantial communication overhead.
The idea
The server uses the clients' local LoRA matrices to estimate aggregated matrices that minimize the divergence between the ideal update (the sum of products) and the practical update, via learned weighting vectors, and distributes them to clients without adding communication beyond vanilla FedLoRA.
Strengths
Evaluated across natural language understanding, reasoning and code tasks, reporting state-of-the-art global performance with communication efficiency, alongside a convergence analysis that includes the aggregation error term.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The paper never explains why this weighting approach should outperform SVD-based aggregation, nor provides any theoretical justification beyond hand-waving about computational efficiency.
  • the experiments cannot disentangle aggregation effects from data heterogeneity
  • The experiments focus mainly on language-related tasks; evaluations on other modalities, e.g., vision-language, would better demonstrate the method’s generality.
  • The introduced matrices P and Q may increase communication costs, but a detailed analysis is missing.

Towards Data-Agnostic Pruning At Initialization: What Makes a Good Sparse Mask? NeurIPS 2023 · accepted
The problem
Pruning at initialization (PaI) removes weights before training to improve training and inference efficiency, but existing methods lag post-training pruning, are poorly understood, and appear to find only good layerwise sparsities rather than weights.
The idea
Analyzes subnetwork topology through the number of effective paths and effective nodes, observes that top-performing subnetworks balance the two (the node-path balancing principle), and proposes a data-agnostic pruning method that solves a multi-objective optimization problem.
Strengths
Across architectures and datasets it outperforms state-of-the-art PaI methods while finding subnetworks with up to 3.4x lower inference FLOPs.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • However, it does not predict the right balance.
  • The experiments are limited to image datasets and two neural network architectures.
  • the standard deviation with respect to random seeds should be included in the paper for fair comparison
  • The sweet spot of the proposed framework seems to be the extreme sparsity regime (> 99%).

Latent Abstraction for Retrieval-Augmented Generation NeurIPS 2026 · accepted
The problem
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) helps large language models with external knowledge, but existing systems generate natural-language queries at each hop and keep a strict architectural separation between retriever and generator.
The idea
A single LLM performs encoding, retrieval and generation in its own latent space: dense retrieval vectors come from the hidden state of a designated [PRED] token and are matched against document representations from the same model, and a lightweight MLP control head over the same hidden states decides when enough evidence has been retrieved, motivated by the observation that answer-token entropy signals retrieval sufficiency.

Improving Pareto Set Learning for Expensive Multi-objective Optimization via Stein Variational Hypernetworks AAAI 2025 · accepted
The problem
Expensive multi-objective optimization problems need costly objective evaluations; Pareto set learning with surrogate models such as Gaussian processes yields fragmented surrogates whose small uncertain regions become pseudo-local optima under acquisition functions like the lower confidence bound.
The idea
Integrates Stein Variational Gradient Descent with hypernetworks: particles move collectively and interact through a kernel to smooth the solution space, keep diversity and avoid clustering around pseudo-local optima, learning a mapping from trade-off reference vectors to Pareto solutions.
Strengths
Extensive experiments on synthetic and real-world multi-objective benchmarks show it significantly improves the quality of the learned Pareto set.

Papers (1)
Hawkes-Augmented World Model for Event-Driven Reinforcement Learning ICLR 2027 · rejected
The problem
World models let reinforcement learning agents improve policies through imagined latent trajectories; in environments with temporally clustered events, recent event history can give useful context for predicting subsequent dynamics and rewards.
The idea
Equips a recurrent stochastic state-space model with learned event inference and a Hawkes-style occurrence prior: an observation-conditioned posterior infers event occurrences and types, a latent-conditioned prior predicts them during imagination, and both share an exponentially decaying memory of binary event occurrences that also conditions the recurrent dynamics and actor-critic learning. An auxiliary future-reward objective shapes the event representation.
Strengths
According to the reviews, it is evaluated on seven robotic manipulation tasks from ManiSkill3 and MS-HAB against DreamerV3, EADream and TD-MPC2, with component ablations, event visualizations and unseen-scene and illumination experiments.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The experiments primarily focus on robotic manipulation tasks with distinct stages and temporally clustered events.
  • When rewards are very sparse or substantially delayed, consequential interactions may fall outside the fixed prediction horizon, weakening the learning signal for event discovery.
  • Event discovery is primarily supported by qualitative visualizations, with insufficient quantitative evidence of the learned events' relevance and temporal structure.
  • The ablation studies do not adequately isolate the benefits of Hawkes-style self-excitation from those of additional event memory or representational capacity.

Provably Improving Generalization of Few-shot models with Synthetic Data ICML 2025 · accepted
The problem
Few-shot image classification is hard because labeled examples are scarce; augmenting them with synthetic data helps, but models trained on synthetic samples degrade because of the gap between real and synthetic distributions.
The idea
A theoretical framework quantifies the impact of the distribution discrepancy on supervised learning, and a theory-based algorithm integrates prototype learning to optimize both data partitioning and model training, bridging real few-shot data and synthetic data.
Strengths
The paper reports that the approach outperforms state-of-the-art methods across multiple datasets in extensive experiments.
What the authors leave open
  • The case of extremely limited real data remains a limitation of the approach.

En-ViMedNER: An English-Vietnamese Parallel Biomedical Corpus with UMLS Semantic Type Annotations EMNLP 2026 · rejected
The problem
Biomedical named entity recognition underpins healthcare AI, and corpora with UMLS annotations such as MedMentions drove progress in English, but no comparable resource existed for Vietnamese.
The idea
A parallel English-Vietnamese corpus built by automatic translation, expert post-editing, LLM-assisted label projection and human verification and adjudication, with 21 UMLS semantic types adapted from MedMentions ST21pv; evaluated for Vietnamese NER and English-to-Vietnamese cross-lingual NER with encoder models, encoder-decoder models and prompt-based LLMs.
Strengths
4,392 PubMed abstract pairs, 44,892 sentence pairs and 202,949 aligned entity-mention pairs; the best Vietnamese NER model reaches F1 52.70 on the test set and 53.78 on the mini-test set, and the best cross-lingual model reaches 45.44 on the mini-test set. The corpus, construction pipeline and baseline models are released.
What the authors leave open
  • The corpus is characterized as silver-standard: the human-audited, consensus-corrected evaluation is limited to a 450-sentence mini-test subset.
  • In cross-lingual generation, models often fail to consistently follow the required biomedical tagging format.
  • Future work will extend the effort to other low-resource Southeast Asian languages, particularly Indonesian and Thai.
