Research directions: Tho Quan

Tho Quan (HCMUT) · All professors

Language-model methods for code, multilingual and Vietnamese text, plus theory of sparse mixture-of-experts routing. Work on language model adaptation and retrieval for code completion, fine-tuning, multilingual alignment, paraphrasing, summarization and Vietnamese morphology. Work on object-level visual grounding to improve language representations through cross-modal pretraining, evaluated on language understanding tasks. Work on discontinuities in sparse mixture-of-experts routing, classifying them geometrically and smoothing the expert selection near them.

Open challenges. Work on extending evaluations and methods to frontier-scale models and low-resource languages remains open. Work on object-level visual grounding lacks analysis of each component's contribution and comparison with recent strong baselines. Work on smoothing expert routing is demonstrated only on small models and random-perturbation theory, leaving large-scale and structured-perturbation behaviour open.

Papers (6)
Fast and Focused: Interactive Repository-Level Fill-in-the-Middle Code Completion with Hybrid Structural Retrieval EMNLP 2026 · accepted
The problem
Repository-level fill-in-the-middle code completion (predicting missing code between a given prefix and suffix) is hard for language models when the needed evidence is code and structural relationships spread across files rather than local.
The idea
Combines lexical retrieval with structural retrieval based on abstract syntax trees (AST) and the language server protocol (LSP) through line-level reciprocal rank fusion, then applies syntax-aware post-processing to align completions with the surrounding code.
Strengths
On RepoEval, ReccEval and CrossCodeEval (Python, Java, TypeScript, C#; over 3,000 repositories) with Qwen2.5-Coder-3B, reports 42.98 exact match (EM) on ReccEval and 32.86 on CrossCodeEval versus 36.25 and 28.40 for the strongest retrieval baseline, with 87-95 ms of non-decoding overhead under warm-cache conditions.
What the authors leave open
  • Evaluation on frontier-scale code models is left for future work.

SIMPA: Restricting What Experts See, Not What They Produce, in MoE-PEFT EMNLP 2026 · accepted
The problem
In mixture-of-experts parameter-efficient fine-tuning (MoE-PEFT), routing collapses: on LLaMA-3 8B with MixLoRA a single expert absorbs 82.5% of tokens, and prior remedies act on expert outputs while leaving each routed expert's input view identical.
The idea
Restricts what experts see instead of what they produce: a magnitude-thresholded input drives a sparse routed LoRA (low-rank adaptation) branch, a parallel dense LoRA branch acts as an information safeguard, and a column-wise gate fuses the two. The same block-aligned mask drives a Triton GPU kernel.
Strengths
At a matched 2.9% trainable-parameter budget, reports 86.2% on seven LLaMA-3 8B reasoning tasks (+3.9 over MixDoRA), 38.4 HumanEval Pass@1 and 59.4 GSM8K, and 88.7 GLUE / 77.7 SuperGLUE on T5-base; expert-input Jaccard overlap of 0.184 versus 1.000 for MixLoRA, and 1.46x end-to-end inference speedup on A100.
What the authors leave open
  • Scaling of mixture-of-experts during pretraining remains open.
  • A separate kernel implementation is left for future work.
  • A full sweep over the block size and per-location sparsity tuning are left for future work.

Transporting Concepts Across Languages: Optimal Transport Toward English-Level Performance in Multilingual LLMs EMNLP 2026 · accepted
The problem
Large language models perform strongly in English, which dominates pretraining data, but inconsistently in other languages; contrastive cross-lingual alignment perturbs English representations symmetrically and degrades the proficiency used as a reference.
The idea
An optimal transport (OT) framework maps non-English hidden-state distributions toward English at middle transformer layers via entropy-regularised Sinkhorn iterations, while an English consistency regularisation term keeps the English anchor stationary.
Strengths
On XNLI, XQuAD and MMMLU, reports cross-lingual transfer gains without the English degradation seen with the contrastive baseline; ablations indicate that all three objective components are necessary.
What the authors leave open
  • Optimal transport alignment over linear-attention states (a compressed recurrent state rather than token-level hidden states) is left for future work.
  • Adaptive, language-specific anchors are proposed as future work.

When Morphology Hides in Plain Sight: Breaking the Isolation in Vietnamese and Beyond ACL 2026 · accepted
The problem
In isolating languages such as Vietnamese, morphological structure is encoded by the composition and ordering of monosyllabic morphemes rather than by inflection, and standard Transformer encoders largely overlook this signal.
The idea
Augments a pretrained Vietnamese encoder with two lightweight inductive biases: morpheme boundary embeddings (BMES tags: begin, middle, end, single) fused into token representations through a learnable gate, and a fixed structural attention matrix injected into early self-attention layers. The same design is applied to Chinese-BERT-wwm.
Strengths
Reports consistent gains over strong baselines on Vietnamese POS tagging, NER and sentence classification, largest on syntactic tasks; on Chinese POS and NER the adapted model reaches 83.16% F1 versus 78.16% with a 0.91 percentage-point drop in accuracy.
What the authors leave open
  • Integration with joint segmentation-tagging frameworks is left for future work.

LAMPAT: Low-Rank Adaption for Multilingual Paraphrasing Using Adversarial Training AAAI 2024 · accepted
The problem
Multilingual paraphrasing through zero-shot machine translation requires parallel translation datasets, so it is not applicable to languages without parallel corpora.
The idea
An unsupervised multilingual paraphrasing model that uses low-rank adaptation (LoRA) combined with adversarial training and virtual labeling, needing only monolingual data.
Strengths
Reports generating human-like, diverse sentences, working well for English and generalizing to unseen languages, and contributes a multilingual multi-domain evaluation dataset covering 13 languages.
What the authors leave open
  • The model still needs improvement in handling idiomatic expressions.
  • The evaluation dataset covers only 13 languages, leaving out many, especially those with limited resources.

Enriching and Controlling Global Semantics for Text Summarization EMNLP 2021 · accepted
The problem
Transformer-based abstractive summarizers suffer from a short-range dependency problem, producing summaries that miss the key points of the document.
The idea
A neural topic model empowered with normalizing flow captures the global semantics of the document and feeds them to the summarizer, while a contextualized gating mechanism controls how much global semantics the text generation module receives.
Strengths
Reports outperforming state-of-the-art summarization models on CNN/DailyMail, XSum, Reddit TIFU, arXiv and PubMed; on XSum, ROUGE-1 is 49.57 for the full model versus 48.13 with a VAE-based topic model and 46.83 without gating.

Papers (2)
Geometric and Stochastic Analysis of Discontinuities in Sparse Mixture-of-Experts ICML 2026 · accepted
The problem
Top-k expert selection in sparse mixture-of-experts (SMoE) layers makes the input-output map discontinuous: arbitrarily close inputs can activate different sets of experts and give substantially different outputs.
The idea
Classifies discontinuities by order (the number of tied experts at a switching event), derives volume estimates with measure-theoretic slicing, models random input perturbations as a diffusion process to bound hitting and occupation times, and proposes a smoothing mechanism that softly includes experts near discontinuities.
Strengths
Proves that lower-order discontinuity sets dominate in volume and that the first hit of the diffusion path is almost surely on an order-1 discontinuity; reports that the smoothing enforces continuity and improves performance on language and vision tasks.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The primary stochastic analysis is based on a driftless diffusion $dx_t = \sigma dB_t$, which substantially departs from practical scenarios such as adversarial perturbations or SGD noise.
  • All experiments are conducted on relatively small-scale models (e.g., BERT-large with 16 experts, ViT-S/16 with 6 experts).
  • The theoretical analysis establishes that order-1 discontinuities dominate (Theorems 4.2–4.7, 5.1, 5.3), but the smoothing mechanism does not use this.
  • The claim of "consistent improvements" in Sections 7.3–7.4 overstates what the numbers show.

Discontinuities in Sparse Mixture-of-Experts: A Measure-Stochastic Analysis ICLR 2026 · desk-rejected
The problem
Top-k expert selection in sparse mixture-of-experts (SMoE) layers makes the input-output map discontinuous: arbitrarily close inputs can activate different sets of experts and give substantially different outputs.
The idea
Classifies discontinuities by order (the number of tied experts at a switching event), derives volume estimates with measure-theoretic slicing, models random input perturbations as a diffusion process to bound hitting and occupation times, and proposes a smoothing mechanism that softly includes experts near discontinuities.
Strengths
Proves that lower-order discontinuity sets dominate in volume and that the first hit of the diffusion path is almost surely on an order-1 discontinuity, with explicit finite-time probability bounds; reports smoothing that keeps the added computational overhead small.
What the authors leave open
  • Adversarial or structured perturbations may deviate from random diffusion and are harder to study; addressing them is left for future work.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • Moreover what matters in practice, e.g. in an adversarial setting or for gradients under the proposed smoothing method, is distance in $x$ space.
  • Is the smoothing useful at test or does it primarily help by giving more informative gradients during training?
  • they fail to provide any direct empirical comparisons, even on tasks where those methods are perfectly applicable, such as the GLUE benchmark or DomainBed classification.
  • Smoothing ablation on very large LLMs (e.g., >7B) not shown.
