Research directions: Hoa Tien Nguyen

Hoa Tien Nguyen (HUST) · All professors

Prof. Hoa's listed work is an ICLR 2024 submission on optimizers for training neural networks with very large batches. Work on large-batch training without learning-rate warm-up, replacing warm-up in the LARS optimizer with a sigmoid-like schedule meant to avoid sharp minima.

Papers (1)
Revisiting LARS for Large Batch Training Generalization of Neural Networks ICLR 2024 · rejected
The problem
LARS (layer-wise adaptive rate scaling) rescales the learning rate of each layer to stabilise large-batch training and is usually combined with a learning-rate warm-up; the paper argues that LARS with warm-up tends to be trapped early in sharp minimizers (narrow, high-curvature minima of the loss) because of redundant ratio scaling, and that a fixed steep decline in the later phase keeps the network from escaping them.
The idea
TVLARS (time-varying LARS) replaces warm-up with a configurable sigmoid-like function that promotes gradient exploration early in training and gradually transitions to standard LARS in later stages.
Strengths
The paper reports that TVLARS outperforms LARS and LAMB in most cases, with up to 2% improvement in classification and up to 10% in all self-supervised learning cases; reviewers describe experiments on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100 and Tiny-ImageNet.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • I am unsure whether TVLARS could be applied in big datasets that might benefit more from large batch training. After all, CIFAR and TinyImageNet are becoming antique from the modern ML perspective.
  • For your proposed method, I think we need to tune more hyper-parameters to get a great performance, which may make the proposed method less convenient to use.
  • Although the proposed method can improve the performance of LARS and LAMB when we don't use warm-up, I noticed that the accuracy is still too low compared with LRAS/LAMB + warm-up.
  • I think the reason why the initial training process is unstable is related to the initialization method.
