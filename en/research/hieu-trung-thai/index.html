Research directions: Hieu Trung Thai

Hieu Trung Thai (QNU) · All professors

Transfer learning without retraining, studied through topological similarity between tasks. Work on transfer learning without retraining between tasks, using topology-preserving matrix mappings with approximation guarantees.

Open challenges. Work on identifying transferable task pairs remains open, since the method assumes topological similarity rather than measuring it.

Deep learning and representation learning (1 paper · CSRankings score 0.20)

Topic description

The work studies whether topological similarity between a source and a target task determines how well features transfer without retraining. It shows such similarity is necessary but not sufficient, and proposes a topology-preserving matrix transformation with a randomised approximation algorithm. Experiments transfer features from ImageNet to four other datasets with three pretrained architectures.

Topological Vanilla Transfer Learning ICLR 2022 · rejected
The problem
Vanilla transfer learning (transferring a pretrained model to a new task without retraining) needs source and target feature representations that are close; the paper studies how topological similarity between the two tasks relates to this.
The idea
Learns a matrix transformation that acts as a homeomorphism (a topology-preserving mapping) on the features to bring source and target closer. Finding the optimal matrix is shown to be APX-hard (no arbitrarily accurate polynomial-time approximation is expected), and an efficient randomised algorithm gives probably-correct approximation guarantees.
Strengths
Reports that strong topological similarity between tasks is necessary but not sufficient for efficient transfer, and that the matrix transformation raises F-score by up to 3-fold when transferring features from ImageNet to CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, MNIST and ISIC 2019 with ResNet50, EfficientNetB3 and InceptionV3.
What the authors leave open
  • Future work: find heuristics for other kinds of homeomorphism that may capture richer geometric relationships.
Questions raised by reviewers
