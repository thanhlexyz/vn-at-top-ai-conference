Research directions: Cuong Pham

Cuong Pham (PTIT) · All professors

Prof. Cuong's papers are mainly in computer vision, with a focus on image generation and restoration. Work on unsupervised image deblurring learns without paired data, alongside work on extreme-pose face data, 3D instance segmentation and fine-grained classification. Work on one-step text-to-image diffusion speeds up inpainting, personalization and negative prompts, and covers erasing narrow concepts and quality-controllable GANs. Work on reinforcement-learning seed selection for spreading influence across multiplex networks, and on choosing which nodes to label in incomplete graphs.

Open challenges. Work on face verification across extreme poses remains open, as do overfitting of blur conversion, motivation for graph models and batch-size-dependent test performance. Work on erasing narrow concepts leaves open robust, effective removal of concepts such as specific characters that lie close to neighboring concepts. Work on seed selection for spreading influence leaves open unknown spreading patterns, scarce high-quality training samples, and active learning on graphs with missing links.

Computer vision and multimodal learning (6 papers · CSRankings score 1.18)

Topic description

Two papers deblur images without paired data, one building pseudo-ground-truth from reference sharp images with dense matching and one converting unseen camera blur into blur that is easier to remove, and a third recovers a sharp video sequence from one blurry image with a self-supervised ordering scheme. A face dataset of up to 450k extreme-pose images with a cross-view verification benchmark is reported to improve cross-pose generalization, and 2D instance masks are aggregated across frames for open-vocabulary 3D instance segmentation. Graph neural network blocks, added as a plug-in module to different architectures, refine the feature vectors a deep network extracts for fine-grained classification by modeling them as graph nodes, with average accuracy gains of 2.78% on CUB200-2011 and 3.83% on Stanford Dogs and a state-of-the-art 95.79% on Stanford Dogs.

Papers (6)
BluRef: Unsupervised Image Deblurring with Dense-Matching References CVPR 2026 · accepted
The problem
Image deblurring methods usually need paired blurred and sharp images, which are costly to collect.
The idea
The method uses unpaired blurred and sharp images of similar scenes and applies a dense matching model to find correspondences between a blurry image and reference sharp images, generating pseudo-ground-truth training data.
Strengths
The paper reports state-of-the-art deblurring performance without relying on existing paired training data or pre-trained networks, for networks of different sizes including those for low-resource devices.

Blur2Blur: Blur Conversion for Unsupervised Image Deblurring on Unknown Domains CVPR 2024 · accepted
The problem
A deblurring algorithm trained for one blur domain struggles with unseen blur from a particular camera device, so deblurring must be adapted to the target camera.
The idea
Blur2Blur transforms a blurry input into another blurry image that is more amenable to deblurring, learning this blur-to-blur conversion from unpaired sharp and blurry images captured by the target camera.
Strengths
The paper reports that the approach significantly outperforms state-of-the-art methods quantitatively and qualitatively on various benchmarks, arguing that blur-to-blur conversion is simpler than direct blur-to-sharp reconstruction.
What the authors leave open
  • The data ratio used in training may cause Blur2Blur to overfit to limited sharp content, and a 6:4 ratio is fixed for all experiments.

EFHQ: Multi-purpose ExtremePose-Face-HQ dataset CVPR 2024 · accepted
The problem
Existing facial datasets have many near-frontal images but few extreme head poses, so models perform worse on profile or pitched faces.
The idea
EFHQ curates VFHQ and CelebV-HQ with a dataset processing pipeline into up to 450k high-quality extreme-pose face images, with sub-datasets for face synthesis and reenactment and a cross-view face verification benchmark.
Strengths
The paper reports that training with EFHQ improves generalization across poses and that state-of-the-art face recognition models drop 5-37% on the cross-view benchmark compared with frontal-to-frontal scenarios.

Open3DIS: Open-Vocabulary 3D Instance Segmentation with 2D Mask Guidance CVPR 2024 · accepted
The problem
Open-vocabulary 3D instance segmentation struggles with small-scale and geometrically ambiguous objects when relying on class-agnostic 3D instance proposal networks.
The idea
Open3DIS aggregates 2D instance masks across frames and maps them to geometrically coherent point cloud regions as object proposals, which are combined with 3D class-agnostic instance proposals.
Strengths
The paper reports significant gains in segmenting objects of diverse categories over state-of-the-art approaches on ScanNet200, S3DIS and Replica.

Leveraging Graph Neural Networks to Boost Fine-Grained Image Classification ICLR 2024 · rejected
The problem
Fine-grained image classification requires precise differentiation among visually similar object categories.
The idea
Graph Neural Network (GNN) blocks refine the feature vectors a deep network extracts, modeling them as graph nodes to capture dependencies, as a plug-in module added to different architectures.
Strengths
The paper reports average accuracy gains of 2.78% on CUB200-2011 and 3.83% on Stanford Dogs, and a state-of-the-art result of 95.79% on Stanford Dogs.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • How can a GPH augmented model perform similarly when the batch size is 1?
  • The method proposed in the paper uses a fully connected graph, so there isn’t really a structure that the GNN can exploit.
  • It is challenging to deploy and evaluate the trained model because its test performance is tied to both the test batch size and the data sampling method.
  • My main concern is the lack of motivation for using a graph-based approach.

HyperCUT: Video Sequence From a Single Blurry Image Using Unsupervised Ordering CVPR 2023 · accepted
The problem
Image-to-video deblurring recovers a sequence of sharp frames from one blurry image, but forward and backward frame orderings are both plausible, which disturbs training.
The idea
A self-supervised ordering scheme maps each video sequence to a latent vector so that a hyperplane separates the vectors of a sequence and its reversed sequence, and the side of the vector defines the order.
Strengths
The paper reports experiments confirming the method's effectiveness and introduces a real-image dataset for image-to-video deblurring covering faces, hands and streets.

Generative models (6 papers · CSRankings score 1.11)

Topic description

Several papers speed up text-to-image diffusion to one or few steps: an inversion network improves few-step inpainting, a one-step tool personalizes subjects, negative prompts are integrated with only a 1.89% increase in FLOPs, and revised training makes a one-step model competitive with multi-step Stable Diffusion. Another paper maps a target concept subspace to a reference subspace to erase narrow concepts such as characters or celebrities, with image quality comparable to effectiveness-focused methods. A GAN-based paper adds a quality control code to synthesize and restore image degradations, so low-quality images can be edited without changing their quality.

Papers (6)
InverFill: One-Step Inversion for Enhanced Few-Step Diffusion Inpainting CVPR 2026 · accepted
The problem
Few-step text-to-image models make inpainting faster, but naively applying them gives poor harmonization and artifacts between background and inpainted region, which the paper traces to random Gaussian noise initialization.
The idea
InverFill is a one-step inversion network that injects semantic information from the masked input image into the initial noise, used inside a blended sampling pipeline with few-step text-to-image models, without training inpainting models.
Strengths
The paper reports improved image quality and text coherence over few-step baselines, matching specialized inpainting models at low function evaluations, with as little as 0.06 seconds of added overhead.

SuMa: A Subspace Mapping Approach for Robust and Effective Concept Erasure in Text-to-Image Diffusion Models ICCV 2025 · accepted
The problem
Concept erasure in text-to-image diffusion models struggles to be both robust and effective for narrow concepts such as copyrighted characters or celebrities, because they lie close to neighboring non-target concepts.
The idea
Subspace Mapping (SuMa) derives a target subspace representing the concept to erase and neutralizes it by mapping it to a reference subspace that minimizes the distance between the two.
Strengths
The paper reports experiments on subclass, celebrity, artistic style and instance erasure, with image quality comparable to effectiveness-focused methods and results on par with methods targeting complete erasure.

Supercharged One-step Text-to-Image Diffusion Models with Negative Prompts ICCV 2025 · accepted
The problem
One-step text-to-image diffusion models are fast but offer less control over image attributes, and applying classifier-free guidance directly to them gives blending artifacts and lower output quality.
The idea
Negative-Away Steer Attention (NASA) integrates negative prompts in the intermediate representation space, using cross-attention to suppress undesired visual attributes, and can be combined with timestep distillation frameworks.
Strengths
The paper reports only a 1.89% increase in FLOPs, compared with the doubled computation of classifier-free guidance, and an HPSv2 score of 31.21 as a new state of the art for one-step diffusion models.

SwiftBrush v2: Make Your One-step Diffusion Model Better Than Its Teacher ECCV 2024 · accepted
The problem
SwiftBrush, a one-step text-to-image diffusion model, shows a quality-diversity trade-off against SD Turbo (SwiftBrush excels in diversity, SD Turbo in quality), leaving it short of its multi-step Stable Diffusion counterpart.
The idea
SwiftBrush v2 changes the training methodology, including better weight initialization and efficient LoRA training, to make the one-step model competitive with multi-step Stable Diffusion.
Strengths
The paper reports the quality-diversity trade-off between SwiftBrush and SD Turbo that motivates its training modifications, with the stated aim of being competitive with multi-step Stable Diffusion.
What the authors leave open

QC-StyleGAN - Quality Controllable Image Generation and Manipulation NeurIPS 2022 · accepted
The problem
StyleGAN models are built on high-quality data, so they do not suit the in-the-wild low-quality images that are common inputs for image manipulation.
The idea
QC-StyleGAN adds a quality control code that lets the generator synthesize image degradations and restore the sharp image, so GAN inversion and manipulation can edit low-quality images without changing their quality.
Strengths
The paper reports restoration of noise, blur, compression artifacts and their mixtures, plus degradation synthesis, transfer and interpolation, with the code released.
What the authors leave open
  • Although many image degradations were used in training, they might not cover all in-the-wild degradations.
  • QC-StyleGAN was only implemented from StyleGAN2-Ada, and the authors plan to improve on these aspects in the future.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • However, for any application, only some visual results are shown, and no statistical performance values are reported.
  • There is no baseline method of the degraded image generation models for comparison.
  • The proposed method can be used as a blind Face Restoration method, but the provided experiments are not enough to show the actual potential.
  • Although interesting, the practice value of the studied problem is not that obvious.

Data mining and graph learning (2 papers · CSRankings score 0.53)

Topic description

Influence maximization selects seed users so that information spreads as widely as possible; for multiplex networks, propagation is encoded with a mixture of experts and a generative model acts as a reinforcement-learning policy that generates and improves seed sets, with better influence spread, scalability and inference time than prior methods on real-world datasets. Active learning on graphs with missing links combines weighted node-classification and link-prediction losses with batch queries, with results above state-of-the-art baselines on four citation datasets.

Papers (2)
REM: A Scalable Reinforced Multi-Expert Framework for Multiplex Influence Maximization AAAI 2025 · accepted
The problem
Influence maximization selects a small set of seed users in a social network so that information spreads as widely as possible; for multiplex networks (several interconnected network layers), the paper states that learning-based methods cannot handle unknown diffusion patterns and depend on high-quality training samples.
The idea
REM (Reinforced Expert Maximization) encodes the dynamic propagation of large multiplex networks with a Propagation Mixture of Experts and treats a generative model as a policy that generates seed sets and learns to improve them through reinforcement learning.
Strengths
The paper reports that on several real-world datasets REM exceeds prior methods in influence spread, scalability and inference time.

Topic description

State space models process long sequences in linear time but often become unstable and lose memory over long horizons because their first-order memory updates are poorly conditioned. The paper adds a momentum pathway and a lightweight Newton-Schulz transformation, which pushes singular values toward one, to the low-rank input writes, preserving parallel-scan complexity. Papers (1)
MuonSSM: Orthogonalizing State Space Models for Sequence Modeling ICML 2026 · accepted
The problem
State space models (SSMs) process long sequences in linear time as an alternative to attention, but the paper reports that they often become unstable and lose memory over long horizons because their first-order memory updates are poorly conditioned.
The idea
MuonSSM adds a momentum pathway to the SSM memory update and applies a lightweight Newton-Schulz transformation, which pushes the singular values of a matrix toward one, to the low-rank input writes, so updates stay bounded and spectrally conditioned while parallel-scan complexity is preserved.
Strengths
The paper gives theory on improved gradient propagation, reduced spectral amplification and richer memory representations, and reports consistent gains in accuracy, robustness and long-context performance on language, vision and time-series benchmarks across several SSM backbones.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The experimental setup is conducted at a limited scale, making it unclear whether the results will hold at larger data and parameter scales.
  • The theoretical analysis of rank enrichment provides strong motivation but lacks direct empirical validation
  • According to Fig. 6, little can be agreed a priori on how to select those; the results seem to present high variability to changes in any of those parameters.
  • it appears that they selected models based on the best test accuracy rather than the best validation accuracy. This may have artificially boosted their results and would not be a fair evaluation protocol.

Human-AI interaction and dialogue (1 paper · CSRankings score 0.20)

Topic description

Counting human actions of interest from wearable sensor data needs a way for users to specify which action to count, including action classes and subjects not seen in training. The paper has users vocalize one, two and three while performing exemplars, localizes these utterances in audio, computes a similarity map between the exemplars and the whole sensor sequence and sums a density estimate into the count. It introduces a dataset of 37 subjects and 50 action categories and reports an average count error of 7.47, lower than frequency-based and transformer-based methods.
