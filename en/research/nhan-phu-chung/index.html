Research directions: Nhan-Phu Chung

Nhan-Phu Chung (UEH) · All professors

Mathematical analysis of Transformer token dynamics and of unbalanced optimal transport for spatial resource allocation. Work on unbalanced optimal transport with total variation penalties for spatial allocation, deriving existence, duality and tessellation-based optimality conditions. Work on the continuous-time dynamics of token representations in self-attention, characterizing when they converge or diverge and how positional encoding changes this.

Open challenges. Work on unbalanced transport still rests on small-scale experiments without method comparisons, and the alternating optimization may reach only local minima. Work on token dynamics in Transformers still rests on simplified attention-only settings with tied weights, leaving full architectures with feed-forward and normalization layers open.

Optimization methods (1 paper · CSRankings score 0.25)

Topic description

The paper proposes a class of unbalanced weak optimal transport problems with total variation penalties, motivated by service area partitioning and facility location. It proves existence of optimal solutions and a dual formulation, and in the semi-discrete setting derives explicit optimality conditions and gradient expressions from a tessellation structure. Preliminary numerical examples illustrate the framework.

Papers (1)
Unbalanced Optimal Total Variation Transport: A Theoretical Approach to Spatial Resource Allocation Problems NeurIPS 2025 · accepted
The problem
Classical optimal transport requires balanced measures, while spatial resource allocation tasks such as service area partitioning and facility location involve supply-demand mismatch with operational costs.
The idea
A class of unbalanced weak optimal transport problems with total variation penalties, with existence of optimal solutions and a dual formulation, explicit optimality conditions in the semi-discrete setting through a tessellation structure, and a quantization problem over locations and weights.
Strengths
The paper establishes existence of optimal solutions and a dual formulation, derives explicit gradient expressions from the dual-tessellation structure, and includes preliminary numerical examples of service area partitioning and facility location.
What the authors leave open
  • The emphasis is theoretical, and only preliminary numerical examples are included.
  • Further exploration of additional applications is left for future research.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The alternating scheme might converge only to a local minima, as the loss seems to be non-convex.
  • Empirical support is limited to small toy instances, with no complexity analysis, convergence guarantee or sensitivity study.
  • The experiments are conducted only on small-scale applications.
  • The applications considered are relatively restricted.

Learning theory and cognitive science (1 paper · CSRankings score 0.14)

Topic description

The paper studies the continuous-time limit of self-attention in pre-trained Transformers and gives sufficient conditions, based on the query, key and value matrices, under which tokens converge to zero or diverge to infinity. It examines how absolute and rotary positional encoding affect these regimes and reports that convergence is associated with lower model performance. Simple architecture refinements are proposed to mitigate convergence.

Papers (1)
Dynamical Properties of Tokens in Self-Attention and Effects of Positional Encoding NeurIPS 2025 · accepted
The problem
How token representations evolve through the layers of a pre-trained Transformer, and when tokens move closer together, converge to zero or diverge, is not well characterized under realistic assumptions on the model parameters.
The idea
An analysis of the continuous-time limit of self-attention as a dynamical system, giving sufficient conditions on the parameter matrices for convergence or divergence, a study of absolute and rotary positional encoding, and simple refinements to Transformers that mitigate convergence.
Strengths
The paper reports that its conditions are broader than those of prior work, that the convergence scenario adversely affects model performance in experiments on pre-trained Transformers, and that the proposed refinements yield measurable improvements.
What the authors leave open
  • The analysis focuses on self-attention and omits components such as layer normalization and feed-forward networks.
  • Extending the framework to the full Transformer architecture is left as a direction for future research.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • Tying the attention weight matrices across layers of a transformer is a strong restriction, which mirrors the ALBERT architecture but does not reflect standard architectures of interest.
  • As a further significant restriction, this paper does not incorporate any analog of the FFN layers of transformers in the theoretical analysis.
  • The Neural ODE model is a coarse approximation of how information propagates through multiple layers of a Transformer.
  • These assumptions simplify the analysis but may significantly limit its applicability to real-world models.
