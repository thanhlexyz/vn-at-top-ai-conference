Research directions: Cuong Viet Ta

Cuong Viet Ta (VNU-UET) · All professors

Graph representation learning on curved manifolds, plus hierarchical discrete representations. Work on graph models on curved manifolds, working directly on the manifold for node classification and link prediction on static and dynamic graphs. Work on hierarchical discrete latent representations that go beyond flat latents, analysed through sample complexity and codebook utilization.

Open challenges. Work on selecting and scaling curved-space graph models remains open: manifold search is heuristic and evaluation covers mostly small graphs. Work on hierarchical discrete latent spaces is covered only through sample complexity and codebook utilization in the available material.

Papers (2)
Pseudo-Riemannian Graph Transformer NeurIPS 2025 · accepted
The problem
Real-world graphs have diverse topological structures that manifolds of uniform global curvature (hyperbolic or spherical spaces) capture poorly; pseudo-Riemannian manifolds generalize both, but existing approaches use an ineffective framework, shallow architectures and give no guideline for choosing the manifold.
The idea
A diffeomorphic framework decomposes a pseudo-Riemannian manifold into a product of spherical and hyperbolic spaces. On it, linear attention, residual connection and layer normalization are defined to build a graph transformer, and a lightweight search algorithm selects a suitable manifold for each input graph.
Strengths
Reports that the model outperforms Riemannian and pseudo-Riemannian baselines in node classification and link prediction on diverse real-world graphs, with the clearest margin in link prediction.
What the authors leave open
  • The framework could be extended to graph-level learning, dynamic graph learning or more generalized domains.
  • Applications such as recommender systems and social networks are left for future analysis.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The space searching algorithm is grounded in a linear relationship between ideal standard deviation and space dimension, but this is mostly heuristic and lacks theoretical justification or sensitivity analysis.
  • The paper lacks some analysis on training/inference time and scalability or complexity.
  • Most benchmark datasets are small (< 10 k nodes), with the largest having 169 k nodes.
  • Gains over the strongest baselines (e.g., SGFormer, Hypformer) are often ≤1–2 F1 on `Cora`, `Pubmed` and `Penn94`, calling into question whether the extra geometric machinery justifies its cost.

Toward a Manifold-Preserving Temporal Graph Network in Hyperbolic Space IJCAI 2024 · accepted
The problem
Hyperbolic geometry suits hierarchical or scale-free graphs, but most hyperbolic dynamic-graph methods perform graph operations in tangent spaces, which can distort the structure as the graph grows over time.
The idea
A temporal graph network that works directly on the hyperbolic manifold, combining a graph convolution module for spatial dependencies, an attention architecture for temporal properties and a gated recurrent unit for spatio-temporal relationships.
Strengths
Reports significant improvements over baselines in link prediction and new link prediction on diverse real-world dynamic graphs.

Unified Deep Discrete Representation Learning Framework ICLR 2025 · rejected
The problem
Deep discrete representation learning frameworks typically operate on flat latents and overlook the hierarchical structure inside discrete latent spaces.
The idea
A hierarchical discrete representation learning framework that generalizes across tasks, accompanied by a theoretical analysis of sample complexity and a study of how codebook utilization affects task performance.
Strengths
States effectiveness across diverse applications and offers practical insights into how sample complexity and codebook utilization interplay in different learning scenarios.
