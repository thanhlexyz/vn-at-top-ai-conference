Research directions: Minh Nhat Vu

Minh Nhat Vu (VinUni) · All professors

Prof. Minh's papers study how robots can model, decide and act reliably under uncertainty or with little data. Work on few-shot adaptation of vision-language-action models through future-embedding conditioning, stopping rules for probing articulated objects and uncertainty-weighted physics-informed dynamics models.

Open challenges. Work on few-shot robot adaptation with open grounding-model failures, articulated-object probing with open real-robot evaluation and physics-informed dynamics with open real-time cost.

Robotics and autonomous systems (3 papers · CSRankings score 0.41)

Topic description

A paper on few-shot adaptation of vision-language-action (VLA) models, whose performance degrades sharply as demonstrations for a new task are reduced, combines explicit prediction of task-grounded future interaction embeddings with implicit contrastive alignment to future goal observations, and supports action-free co-training on synthetic videos. It reports 95.7% success with 20 demonstrations on LIBERO, gains of 7-12% on RoboCasa and up to 26% absolute gains on real robots. A paper on probing articulated objects such as drawers and hinged doors combines a common-path motion estimator with a calibrated stopping rule; in simulation it cuts probing queries from 12 to 1.87 with 97.5% versus 98.5% task success. A paper on hybrid physics-informed dynamics models attaches an epistemic uncertainty estimator to weight the physics and learned terms, with a convergence proof and lower long-horizon position error than fixed hybrid models and other baselines on a simulated quadrotor and a racing car.

Papers (3)
Probe2Act: Knowing When to Stop Probing Articulated Objects ICLR 2027 · rejected
The problem
A robot can find out how an unfamiliar drawer or hinged door moves by probing it, but every extra interaction uses budget; the paper asks when the observed motion is enough to stop identifying the mechanism and start executing the task.
The idea
Probe2Act combines an analytic common-path estimator, which keeps the displacement between probes so that weak individual responses can jointly reveal a local motion direction, with a calibrated stopping rule that stops when the direction's calibrated angular radius meets a declared tolerance and abstains when the budget runs out.
Strengths
In Panda robot-arm simulation with 100 calibration and 100 test geometries and supplied contact and link tracking, the abstract reports that Probe2Act cuts clean interaction cost from 12 to 1.87 queries (97.5% versus 98.5% task success) and uses 6.78 queries under combined pose noise with 98% success for both methods.
What the authors leave open
  • Transfer to benchmark-scale settings is stated as a remaining limitation.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The experimental evaluation is conducted entirely in simulation, without any real-world robotic experiments.
  • Moreover, the probing actions are predefined rather than adaptively optimized based on previously acquired information.
  • most real-world situations do not assume a fixed, single-degree-of-freedom articulation model, and that assuming fixed grasp makes the claims weaker.
  • Since the reported query savings are measured for one acquisition sequence, their sensitivity to alternative fixed orderings remains unclear.

EPIC-PIML: Epistemic-Constructed Physics-Informed Machine Learning ICLR 2027 · rejected
The problem
Hybrid physics-informed machine learning (HPIML) models of robot dynamics add a learned term to a known physics model, but the paper notes that under environmental uncertainty they are not necessarily robust: in-distribution regions favour the ML term while out-of-distribution regions favour the physics term.
The idea
EPIC-PIML attaches an Epistemic Neural Network (ENN) to a pre-trained HPIML model to estimate its epistemic uncertainty (uncertainty due to limited data) in closed form, and uses that uncertainty to set how much the physics and ML terms each contribute to the prediction; reviewers describe this as a monotone gate on the learned residual, used inside a model predictive path integral (MPPI) controller.
Strengths
The paper proves that the weighting mechanism converges to the correct trade-off as data and capacity grow, and reviewers summarise experiments on a simulated quadrotor and an F1TENTH racing car that report lower long-horizon position RMSE than fixed HPIML and other baselines.
What the authors leave open
  • The authors plan to move the method's computational cost offline by training a reinforcement-learning policy on EPIC-PIML data, with the goal of real-time deployment on real robots.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The reported runtime of 119.4 ms per step for EPIC-PIML versus 33.2 ms for HPIML raises concerns for the claimed 50 Hz control setting.
  • Theorem 1 assumes the true residual can be recovered through a monotone function of estimated uncertainty, without much justification.
  • Variation across ENN outputs does not, by itself, establish that real-system prediction errors follow that distribution, particularly under distribution shift.
  • the paper lacks ablations for the ENN, monotone gate, final refitting, and alternative uncertainty estimators.

FOCA: Future-Oriented Conditioning for Data-Efficient Vision-Language-Action Adaptation ICML 2026 · accepted
The problem
Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models map camera observations and language instructions to robot actions after large-scale multimodal pretraining. The paper's stress test of state-of-the-art VLA models shows that their performance degrades sharply as the number of demonstrations for a new task is reduced (few-shot imitation learning).
The idea
FOCA adds future-oriented conditioning during adaptation: explicit prediction of task-grounded future interaction embeddings and implicit contrastive alignment with future goal observations, so that long-horizon reasoning happens in latent space without pixel-level prediction. Because the implicit objective needs no action labels, it also supports co-training on synthetic videos from video world models.
Strengths
The paper reports 95.7% success with 20 demonstrations on LIBERO, improvements of 7–12% on RoboCasa and up to 26% absolute gains on real robots.
What the authors leave open
  • The authors point to an analogy with offline reinforcement learning methods that penalise out-of-distribution value estimates, but state that extending those guarantees to occupancy estimation at the representation level remains open.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • the explicit future prediction module is disabled when training on DreamGen videos.
  • The paper does not thoroughly analyze how errors or failures in this grounding model affect the overall system, which introduces a potential point of failure in novel domains.
  • If the dataset contains low-quality or failed demonstrations (common in real-world robot data), aligning with these "negative" futures could misguide the policy.
  • Rigidly fixing explicit tokens as static parameters during deployment may impair out-of-distribution generalization
