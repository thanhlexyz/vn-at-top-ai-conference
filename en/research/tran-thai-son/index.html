Research directions: Tran Thai Son

Tran Thai Son (HCMUS) · All professors

Prof. Son's listed work is a NeurIPS 2025 paper on how large multimodal models align what they see with what they write. Work on vision-language alignment trains multimodal models to reorder shuffled image patches and text, plus a loss linking responses to image tokens.

Open challenges. Work on vision-language alignment leaves open whether synthetic shuffling tasks capture real misalignment, how to balance the objectives, and whether gains need many GPUs.

Computer vision and multimodal learning (1 paper · CSRankings score 0.20)

Topic description

The paper attributes robustness and generalization limits of large multimodal models to weak alignment between visual and textual features. It adds image-order and text-order reconstruction tasks with a learnable token that predicts the image order, plus a loss that strengthens attention from responses to image tokens, and reports state-of-the-art results against prior models on academic task-oriented and instruction-following benchmarks.

Papers (1)
Directed-Tokens: A Robust Multi-Modality Alignment Approach to Large Language-Vision Models NeurIPS 2025 · accepted
The problem
Large multimodal models (LMMs), which connect a vision encoder to a large language model, still show limitations in robustness and generalization that the paper attributes to the alignment and correlation between visual and textual features.
The idea
The paper adds two shuffle-learning tasks, reconstructing the order of permuted image patches and of permuted text, to pre-training and fine-tuning, predicts the image order from a learnable directed token appended to the input, and adds an Image-to-Response Guided loss that strengthens attention from response tokens to visual tokens.
Strengths
The paper reports state-of-the-art performance compared with prior LMMs on academic task-oriented and instruction-following benchmarks, with gains over LLaVA-1.5 using the same training data and several LLM backbones.
What the authors leave open
  • The study covers the proposed tasks and losses only under selected hyperparameters and model scales, and the method still has limitations in balancing its multiple objectives.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The authors experiment with 32 A100 GPUs, while LLaVA 1.5 only requires 8 A100 GPUs (assuming 80GB of VRAM for each A100).
  • the Image-to-Response Guided Loss may lead the model to over-rely on visual cues, possibly affecting its instruction-following or text-only capabilities.
  • The shuffle learning tasks are somewhat synthetic and may not fully represent the types of semantic misalignment encountered in real-world scenarios.
  • While the Directed Token is empirically effective, the paper lacks deeper theoretical justification or analysis regarding why it facilitates better cross-modal alignment.
