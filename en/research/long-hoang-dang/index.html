Research directions: Long Hoang Dang

Long Hoang Dang (PTIT) · All professors

Prof. Long's papers span three separate lines of machine learning research. Work on fairness of medical multimodal LLMs bounds quality gaps between demographic groups by five differences in the data and benchmarks ten models. Work on aligning text with image regions step by step from simple to complex concepts, so vision-language models reason about object relations. Work on neural operators for Hamiltonian PDEs builds layers that preserve the symplectic structure of energy-conserving dynamics so long-horizon rollouts stay stable.

Open challenges. Work on fairness of medical multimodal LLMs leaves open whether a bound over five dependent data differences shows which one causes the unfairness. Work on grounded compositional reasoning is tested only on visual grounding and compositional question answering, with training data derived from Visual Genome. Work on neural operators for Hamiltonian PDEs leaves open dissipative systems such as heat diffusion, and guarantees of energy conservation beyond linear Hamiltonian systems.

Trustworthy AI (1 paper · CSRankings score 0.20)

Topic description

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) that answer clinical questions about medical images and draft reports show quality differences across demographic groups. The paper defines fairness for open-ended generation as a gap in LLM-as-a-judge scores, bounds it by five distribution differences (image, query, two image-query dependencies, response generation) with an estimator for each, and extends the bound to distribution shift. The benchmark covers ten models on eight datasets and four imaging modalities, reporting widespread demographic gaps and inconsistent effects of two fairness interventions.

Papers (1)
Rethinking Fairness in Multimodal Large Language Models for Medical Imaging ICLR 2027 · rejected
The problem
Multimodal large language models (MLLMs), which take a medical image and a text query and write free text, are used to answer clinical questions and draft radiology reports, and early audits show their output quality differs across demographic groups. The paper states that fairness research in medical imaging has mostly studied image-only label predictors, leaving no theoretical account of how unfairness arises in MLLMs and no systematic benchmark.
The idea
The paper (MedFairVLM) defines fairness for open-ended generation as the gap in LLM-as-a-judge scores between demographic groups, derives an upper bound that splits unfairness into five sources (differences in the image distribution, the query distribution, the two image-query dependencies, and response generation given both inputs), extends the bound to distribution shift, and gives an estimator for each source from data.
Strengths
According to the reviewers' summaries, the benchmark evaluates ten models on eight datasets spanning four imaging modalities, closed- and open-ended question answering, report generation, fine-tuning and distribution shift, and reports widespread demographic gaps and inconsistent effects of two fairness interventions.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • Therefore, the same five sources are shared by different MLLMs on the same dataset, even though their fairness behavior can be very different.
  • An upper bound involving several dependent discrepancies does not identify their individual contributions or establish that intervening on one will reduce the observed gap.
  • F1 depends on prevalence, while judge scores can depend on disease complexity and reference-report characteristics.
  • GPT-5-mini is used for much of the QA construction and for open-ended evaluation, without quantified expert agreement across demographic groups and modalities.

Computer vision and multimodal learning (1 paper · CSRankings score 0.20)

Topic description

Large vision-language models match concepts across images and text but, the paper states, struggle with compositional concepts and relationships between entities. The paper builds a hierarchy of image-text alignments, aligning descriptions with image regions from simple to complex so lower levels inform higher ones, and generates a nested compositional dataset from Visual Genome. It reports gains over baselines on several visual grounding and compositional question answering tasks.

Papers (1)
Progressive Multi-granular Alignments for Grounded Reasoning in Large Vision-Language Models AAAI 2025 · accepted
The problem
Large vision-language models (LVLMs) match concepts across images and text but, according to the paper, struggle with compositional concepts and high-level relationships between entities, which grounded visual reasoning requires.
The idea
PromViL (Progressive multi-granular Vision-Language alignments) builds a hierarchy of image-text alignments from simple to complex concepts and aligns textual descriptions with image regions progressively, so that lower levels inform higher-level reasoning; a data generation process derived from Visual Genome produces a dataset of nested compositional vision-language pairs, named CompoVL in the paper.
Strengths
The paper reports that PromViL outperforms baselines on several visual grounding and compositional question answering tasks.

Topic description

The paper builds a neural operator from three layer types that are symplectic by construction, so that every layer and every rollout step preserves the symplectic form of Hamiltonian dynamics rather than relying on soft physics penalties. On five Hamiltonian PDE benchmarks it reports stable long-term rollouts, lower trajectory error, more accurate conservation of physical invariants and up to two orders of magnitude fewer parameters than two standard neural operator baselines.

Papers (1)
SympFNO: Structure-Preserving Fourier Neural Operators for Physical Surrogate Modeling NeurIPS 2026 · accepted
The problem
Neural operators learn solution maps of partial differential equations (PDEs) efficiently, but standard architectures break the geometric structure of Hamiltonian (energy-conserving) dynamics during inference, which makes long-horizon rollouts unstable.
The idea
The Symplectic Fourier Neural Operator (SympFNO) is built from three symplectic layers, Fourier Shear, Nonlinear Shear and Physical Rotation, so that every layer and every rollout step preserves the symplectic form of Hamiltonian dynamics by construction rather than through soft physics penalties.
Strengths
On five Hamiltonian PDE benchmarks the paper reports stable long-term rollouts, lower trajectory error, more accurate conservation of physical invariants and up to two orders of magnitude fewer parameters than FNO and PINO.
What the authors leave open
  • Exact symplecticity does not imply exact conservation of all physical invariants: SympFNO can reduce energy drift without guaranteeing perfect energy conservation.
  • Dissipative systems such as heat diffusion, viscous flows, reaction-diffusion and Navier–Stokes dynamics are not purely symplectic, so the architecture is not guaranteed to learn them; a symplectic-plus-corrective decomposition is left beyond the scope of the work.
  • Combining SympFNO with energy, mass or momentum constraints, projection-based post-processing, or multi-step training is left for future work.
Questions raised by reviewers
  • The energy-drift guarantee in the paper is derived under restrictive assumptions, mainly for linear Hamiltonian systems and under the condition that the learned map is sufficiently close to the exact flow.
  • the exact symplecticity results are formulated after spatial discretization, on the finite-dimensional phase space
  • The tradeoff between structure and expressivity is not fully analyzed. In Table 2, it seems that ProjFNO is superior to SympFNO in terms of energy drift in some cases
  • The proposed method is restricted to Hamiltonian PDEs. In general, symplecticity does not imply full conservation. No convergence rate analysis or UAT-like result is provided.
